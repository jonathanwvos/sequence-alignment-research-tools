## Executive summary

- The author introduces strobealign, a short-read aligner based on variable-size syncmer and randstrobe seeding. (PDF p. 2).
- Strobealign achieves 5 to 8-fold speedup over BWA-MEM while maintaining comparable alignment accuracy. (PDF p. 16).

## Objectives

- Speed up short-read alignment to large eukaryotic genomes while maintaining high tolerance for sequencing errors. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Randstrobes provide dynamic-length seeds that span insertion and deletion variants better than rigid k-mers. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- The E-hits metric is formulated to quantify and minimize seed repetitiveness across reference genomes. (PDF p. 25).

## Methods

- Randstrobes couple pairs of syncmers separated by variable distances into fuzzy seeds with dynamic lengths. (PDF p. 18).
- Candidate mapping locations are scored and chained before fine-grained base alignment. (PDF p. 23).

## Applications

- Genomic short-read alignment for human, plant, and animal resequencing projects. (PDF p. 5).

## Optimization

- Strobe seeds reduce candidate hit counts in repetitive genomic regions without requiring seed extension. (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- Strobealign is benchmarked against BWA-MEM, BWA-MEM2, Bowtie2, and Minimap2 across fruit fly, maize, human, and rye genomes. (PDF p. 5).

## Results

- Strobealign is 5 to 8.5 times faster than BWA-MEM across benchmark genomes with comparable accuracy. (PDF p. 16).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Peak memory footprint during index building is higher than Bowtie2 and BWA-MEM. (PDF p. 9).
