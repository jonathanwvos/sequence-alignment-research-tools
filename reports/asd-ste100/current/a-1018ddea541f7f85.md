## Executive summary

- The authors present a progressive sequence alignment method based on the rule once a gap, always a gap. (PDF p. 1).
- The alignment order follows a branching order determined from pairwise similarity scores. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- Construct reliable multiple sequence alignments to infer correct phylogenetic trees. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- Initial gap placements between closely related sequences should remain fixed during subsequent group alignments. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- Alignment distances are converted to estimated evolutionary distances using amino acid substitution models. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- The algorithm calculates pairwise alignment scores using dynamic programming. (PDF p. 2).
- Sequences and pre-aligned groups are progressively aligned in order of decreasing similarity. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- Multiple sequence alignment of globin and kinase protein families. (PDF p. 3).

## Optimization

Not stated in the extractable source pages.

## Evaluation and testing

- The authors evaluate reconstructed phylogenetic trees against known organismal phylogenies. (PDF p. 7).

## Results

- Progressive alignment yields more accurate tree topologies than unguided multiple alignment. (PDF p. 7).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Errors introduced in early alignment steps cannot be corrected during later stages. (PDF p. 9).
