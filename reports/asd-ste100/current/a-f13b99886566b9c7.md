## Executive summary

- The authors introduce Emblask for hybrid haplotype assembly and Weaver for short-read mapping to pangenomes. Their goal is to reduce reference bias. (PDF p. 1).
- HPRC-ICE contains 788 haplotypes and 51.41 million small variants. Weaver produces alignments in reference coordinates for use with existing analysis tools. (PDF p. 1).
- The population analysis reports 6.17% more reliable small variants than an earlier linear-reference analysis. The two cohorts differ in sample membership. (PDF p. 6).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to make pangenome mapping practical for large cohorts while retaining existing file formats, coordinates, and annotations. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that separating structural variants in an rGFA graph from phased small variants in VCF reduces graph complexity and resource requirements. (PDF p. 3).
- They argue that rare variants should remain available during mapping because these variants can enable detection of clinically relevant alleles. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- Graph vertices and edges have ranks tied to their source assemblies. Paths of one rank preserve source sequences as new assemblies enter the graph. (PDF p. 10).
- Weaver uses a weighted average of alignment scores across represented haplotypes. Common variants affect this score more than rare variants. (PDF p. 10).
- On the smaller HPRC reference, Weaver achieves an HG002 low-confidence-region F1 score of 83.26%. The authors attribute gains to both the mapper and the larger reference. (PDF p. 6).

## Methods

- Emblask corrects offspring long reads with Ratatosk and trio short reads. It uses Flye assembly, phased variant calls, parental assignment, and separate assembly and polishing of the two haplotypes. (PDF p. 2).
- Weaver indexes minimizers from graph and VCF haplotypes. Its default k-mer length is 23, and its window contains 11 k-mers. (PDF p. 10).
- Weaver chains seeds with matching rank and spacing. It extends chains along paths of that rank and aligns the resulting sequence to each read. (PDF p. 10).
- Pairwise scoring uses match reward 1, mismatch penalty 4, soft-clip penalty 6, gap-open penalty 7, and gap-extension penalty 1. The maximum weighted haplotype score selects the primary alignment. (PDF p. 10).

## Applications

- The authors map 57,630 Icelandic genomes with Weaver and jointly call variants with GraphTyper. (PDF p. 6).
- Targeted remapping of 429,193 UK Biobank participants recovers GBA1 p.Leu483Pro and replicates its association with Parkinson’s disease. (PDF p. 8).

## Optimization

- The ICU index records vertex pairs connected within 1,500 bases by default. Paired-read chain visibility helps discard short candidate chains. (PDF p. 10).
- The graph excludes structural variants from Icelandic assemblies to limit complexity. Structural variants come from T2T-CHM13 and HPRC assemblies. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors select GraphTyper for population genotyping. They estimate that DeepVariant would need over ten million CPU hours for this task. (PDF p. 6).

## Evaluation and testing

- Mapping benchmarks compare Weaver with BWA-MEM 0.7.16a, DRAGEN 4.2.4, and Giraffe 1.51.0 workflows. Simulated reads come from both T2T-Q100 HG002 haplotypes. (PDF p. 4).
- A 19-genome Icelandic benchmark measures dark regions, runtime, and variant accuracy. Its baseline variants come from assemblies built with PacBio HiFi and parental short reads. (PDF p. 6).
- Variant benchmarks use mapper-specific DeepVariant models, except for DRAGEN, which uses its own caller. HG002 analyses separate GIAB high- and low-confidence regions. (PDF p. 6).
- Population quality checks use 6,286 trios, 446 monozygotic twin pairs, and 355 duplicate samples. Reported genotype checks require genotype quality above 40. (PDF p. 6).

## Results

- In 329 Icelandic trios, Emblask produces 658 haplotype assemblies with average Q50 accuracy and 98.9% completeness. (PDF p. 2).
- Weaver leaves 40.71 Mb dark, compared with 57.34 Mb for BWA-MEM, 69.13 Mb for DRAGEN, and 69.96 Mb for GiraffePPR. The authors report 40% less mapping time than existing methods. (PDF p. 6).
- Mean genome-wide F1 across 19 genomes is 97.34% for Weaver-DeepVariant, 97.30% for DRAGEN, 97.24% for GiraffePPR-DeepVariant, and 97.06% for BWA-MEM-DeepVariant. (PDF p. 6).
- The cohort pipeline uses 9.37 million CPU hours for mapping and compression, then 0.9 million CPU hours for variant calling. It produces 98,961,991 filtered variants. (PDF p. 6).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors could not evaluate GiraffePPR on HPRC-ICE because of its high haplotype count. (PDF p. 6).
- The population comparison uses 57,630 Weaver samples and 63,460 BWA-MEM samples, with 47,306 shared samples. Interpretation: the 6.17% increase is not an isolated mapper comparison. (PDF p. 6).
- Regions near centromeres and telomeres show no improvement in mean variant score. The authors attribute this to repetitive sequence, structural complexity, and GC content. (PDF p. 6).
- Supplementary contigs are excluded from further analyses because of variable ploidy, limited annotations, and limited downstream tool support. (PDF p. 8).

Statisticalanalyses Weusedlogisticregressionwithanadditivemodeltotestfortheasso- ciation between sequence variants and binary traits. The reported P values are based on two-sided tests with age and sex as covariates. No statistical methods were used to predetermine sample size for association testing. Reported correlations are Pearson’s correlation coefficients (r). In Figs. 2 and 4, the box plots show the distributions of data points withtheinterquartilerange(IQR)fromthe25thtothe75thpercentiles represented as a box, the median value represented as a line within the box, the whiskers as lines extending the box to the minimum and maximum values within 1.5 times of the IQR and the outlier values as data points extending beyond the whiskers.
