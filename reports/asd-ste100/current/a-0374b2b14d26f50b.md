## Executive summary

- The authors describe a rapid database search algorithm using word lookup tables (ktup). (PDF p. 1).
- High-scoring diagonal regions are identified and joined, followed by local dynamic programming optimization. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- Search large protein sequence databases rapidly and sensitively on microcomputers. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- Filtering matches by shared diagonal offsets avoids computing the full dynamic programming matrix. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- Statistical significance is evaluated by comparing similarity scores to randomized sequences. (PDF p. 5).

## Methods

- A lookup table identifies exact matches of length ktup between query and database sequences. (PDF p. 2).
- Matching regions on consistent diagonals are scored with PAM250 matrices and joined with gap penalties. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- Rapid sequence database searches for homology detection in protein libraries. (PDF p. 1).

## Optimization

- Pre-indexing sequences with word lookup tables minimizes character comparison overhead. (PDF p. 2).

## Evaluation and testing

- The authors test the algorithm on kinase oncogenes and cytochrome c sequences. (PDF p. 4).

## Results

- The tool resolves distant homologies orders of magnitude faster than full dynamic programming. (PDF p. 4).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Word-based heuristics may miss distantly related sequences lacking exact ktup matches. (PDF p. 5).
