## Executive summary

- The authors replace linear layers in a protein-coordinate projector with Clifford Group Equivariant Neural Network layers. (PDF p. 3).
- Six projector configurations compare one or two linear, multivector linear, or fully connected geometric product layers. (PDF p. 3).
- On PSICOV150, two multivector linear layers achieve a median GDT TS of 20.81 with 232 projector parameters. Two linear layers achieve 18.76 with 421 parameters. (PDF p. 4).

## Objectives

- Measure the effect of Clifford layers on protein structure prediction and compare them with non-geometric layers. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that multivector representations preserve geometric meaning that scalarized features can lose. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors identify geometric interpretation as the main advantage. Intermediate projector outputs can be viewed as proposed protein structures. (PDF p. 5).

## Analysis

- Multivector linear layers learn separate weighted combinations for each grade. Fully connected geometric product layers add interactions between pairs of multivectors. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors describe equivariance as commutation between an orthogonal input transformation and the network mapping. They relate this property to arbitrary coordinate reference frames. (PDF p. 2).
- Interpretation: the table gives a 2.05-point increase in median GDT TS for two multivector linear layers over two linear layers. This is an absolute score difference. (PDF p. 4).

## Methods

- A Graph Transformer encodes biochemical node and edge features. A separate 3D projector converts its node representations into alpha-carbon coordinates. (PDF p. 3).
- The Clifford projector reshapes 27 features into nine 3D coordinate proposals. It embeds each proposal as the vector part of an eight-component multivector in G3,0,0. (PDF p. 3).
- One or two Clifford layers reduce the proposals to one multivector per residue. Grade-1 extraction returns the predicted 3D coordinates. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The pipeline predicts protein alpha-carbon coordinates and exposes intermediate coordinate proposals for geometric inspection. (PDF p. 5).

## Optimization

- Training minimizes distance-map MAE plus 20 times one minus SSIM. The authors chose the factor 20 empirically to balance the loss terms. (PDF p. 4).
- Adam starts at a learning rate of 0.01. The rate is multiplied by 0.9 every two epochs. Batch size is 32, with early-stopping patience 4 and tolerance 0.1. (PDF p. 4).
- The implementation ran in a Google Colaboratory notebook on an NVIDIA Tesla A100 GPU. It used Biopython for protein data. (PDF p. 5).

## Evaluation and testing

- PDNET supplies 3456 DEEPCOV training chains and 150 PSICOV150 test chains. The authors reserve 20% of the training set for validation and report no domain homology between training and test sets. (PDF p. 4).
- Training chains contain 50–500 amino acids. Test chains contain 50–266. Inputs have 27 residue features and five pairwise features. (PDF p. 4).
- Evaluation uses MAE and SSIM between predicted and reference distance maps. GDT scores compare coordinates after structural alignment. (PDF p. 4).
- GDT TS averages residue percentages within 1, 2, 4, and 8 Å. GDT HA uses thresholds of 0.5, 1, 2, and 4 Å. (PDF p. 4).

## Results

- Single-layer median GDT TS scores are 15.15 for linear, 16.98 for multivector linear, and 18.13 for geometric product projectors. (PDF p. 4).
- Two-layer median GDT HA scores are 5.17 for linear, 6.77 for multivector linear, and 6.79 for geometric product projectors. (PDF p. 4).
- Two multivector linear layers give the lowest test MAE. Two multivector linear or geometric product layers give the highest median SSIM. (PDF p. 5).
- Clifford layers reach lower training and validation losses than linear layers, but converge more slowly. (PDF p. 5).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Parameter savings apply to the multivector linear configurations. Geometric product projectors use 682 parameters for one layer and 1240 for two, exceeding the corresponding linear projectors. (PDF p. 4).
- Interpretation: the reported comparison concerns six projector variants within one Graph Transformer pipeline and one stated test set. It does not establish gains across other prediction architectures. (PDF p. 4).
