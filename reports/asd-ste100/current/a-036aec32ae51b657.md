## Executive summary

- The authors present TM-align, a protein structure alignment algorithm that optimizes TM-score rather than RMSD. (PDF p. 1).
- TM-align combines dynamic programming with heuristic rotation matrices to align protein tertiary structures. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- Develop a structural alignment algorithm that avoids the length-dependent bias and local distortion sensitivity of RMSD. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- TM-score weights smaller residue distances stronger than larger distances, making it sensitive to global fold topology. (PDF p. 7).

## Analysis

- Alignment coverage and TM-score accuracy are benchmarked across thousands of non-homologous structural pairs. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- Initial fragment alignments are generated using gapless eight-residue superposition and secondary structures. (PDF p. 2).
- Dynamic programming iteratively refines residue pairings guided by rotation matrices that optimize the TM-score. (PDF p. 7).

## Applications

- Structural fold classification, remote homology detection, and structural model assessment. (PDF p. 5).

## Optimization

- Iterative dynamic programming converging on stable TM-score rotation matrices avoids exhaustive superposition search. (PDF p. 7).

## Evaluation and testing

- TM-align is compared against DALI, CE, and SAL on a benchmark set of 17,086 protein structure pairs. (PDF p. 3).

## Results

- TM-align is approximately 20 times faster than DALI and CE while generating alignments with higher average TM-scores. (PDF p. 7).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Non-topological alignments and circular permutations are not detected by the sequential dynamic programming framework. (PDF p. 2).
