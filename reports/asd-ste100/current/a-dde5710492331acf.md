## Executive summary

- Sankoff gives an algorithm to find a highest-value sequence match under deletion/insertion constraints. (PDF p. 1).
- The method limits changes in spacing between successive matched positions. It also reduces the computation needed for unconstrained matching. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- Find a longest common subsequence subject to a bound on its deletion/insertion index. (PDF p. 2).
- Support studies of genetic relationships through nucleotide or amino-acid sequence comparisons. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The author argues that a match with fewer spacing changes can indicate genetic similarity better than a longer match with more spacing changes. (PDF p. 2).
- The author states that the proposed constraint cannot be expressed through the cell weights and gap penalties of the Needleman–Wunsch method. (PDF p. 2).
- The author presents the DI index as a genetically meaningful alternative to arbitrarily selected numerical criteria. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- A match preserves position order in both sequences. Its value counts paired positions with equal symbols. (PDF p. 1).
- The DI index counts successive matched-position pairs whose position increments differ between the two sequences. (PDF p. 2).
- A constrained path can extend along a diagonal without using another DI allowance. An extension with unequal position increments uses one allowance. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- The unconstrained algorithm stores prefix maxima in W. Each cell takes the maximum of the upper cell, the left cell, and the diagonal cell plus an equality indicator. (PDF p. 1).
- The constrained algorithm constructs score matrices V_q for successive DI bounds. V_0 accumulates equality indicators along diagonals. (PDF p. 2).
- For positive q, V_q selects between a diagonal predecessor in V_q and a prefix maximum in W_(q−1), then adds the equality indicator. (PDF p. 3).
- Backward search reconstructs a match from the score matrices. The constrained search follows diagonal segments and moves to a lower DI level when required. (PDF p. 2).

## Applications

- The author proposes the algorithm for genetic homology analysis of nucleotide and amino-acid sequences. (PDF p. 1).
- The author proposes the algorithm as a basis for statistical tests of sequence similarity and the number of separating deletions and insertions. (PDF p. 3).

## Optimization

- Prefix maxima remove repeated searches over predecessor regions. Unconstrained matrix construction and reconstruction together take time proportional to mn. (PDF p. 1).
- Each constrained score level takes time proportional to mn. The stated total computation across relevant DI bounds is proportional to mnq_max. (PDF p. 3).
- The algorithm first obtains the unconstrained optimum v. It stops increasing the DI bound at the smallest q for which v_q equals v. (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- A worked example compares AGCCAU with CCAGUCU. It provides the unconstrained V and W matrices and alternative best matches. (PDF p. 2).
- The same example compares matches with zero and one DI event. (PDF p. 3).

## Results

- In the worked example, a DI index of one permits the unconstrained optimum of four matched symbols. (PDF p. 3).
- The best zero-DI matches contain three pairs. These are the first three pairs of the illustrated unconstrained best matches. (PDF p. 3).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The author says q_max is usually much smaller than either sequence length. The supplied complexity argument does not establish this as a worst-case bound. (PDF p. 3).
- Statistical testing is a proposed application. This page does not provide a test procedure or statistical results. (PDF p. 3).
