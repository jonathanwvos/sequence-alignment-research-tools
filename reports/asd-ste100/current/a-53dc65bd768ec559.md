## Executive summary

- MO-SAStrE refines protein multiple sequence alignments with NSGA-II. It jointly maximizes STRIKE score, non-gap percentage and totally conserved column percentage. (PDF p. 2).
- The algorithm combines existing alignments and returns a Pareto front of non-dominated solutions. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors report significant improvement over initial alignments for all 218 BAliBASE problems under their hypervolume assessment. (PDF p. 7).

## Objectives

- Use structural contacts to improve alignment evaluation, while retaining conserved columns and reducing gaps. (PDF p. 4).
- Combine accurate sections from different input alignments through crossover. (PDF p. 4).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that different aligners capture different biological features. A single input alignment can therefore contain both accurate and inaccurate regions. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors argue that protein structures can reveal distant relationships because structures are more conserved than sequences. (PDF p. 2).
- The non-gap objective addresses methods that increase residue identities through excessive gap insertion. (PDF p. 4).

## Analysis

- Pareto-front solutions express tradeoffs among the three objectives. The authors leave the final alignment choice to the user's objective preference. (PDF p. 5).
- The hypervolume analysis normalizes each objective to [0, 1]. Under the paper's convention, lower hypervolume indicates better combined objective values. (PDF p. 6).
- Interpretation: improvement in hypervolume measures improvement under the chosen objectives. The separate BAliscore comparison tests agreement with reference alignments. (PDF p. 6).

## Methods

- Initial alignments come from ClustalW, Muscle, Kalign, Mafft, RetAlign, T-Coffee, FSA and ProbCons. (PDF p. 2).
- An integer matrix stores each residue by its sequence position. Each gap uses the negative position of the preceding residue. (PDF p. 3).
- Mutation moves gap blocks within a sequence and removes columns that contain only gaps. One-point crossover matches residue positions between parents and inserts gaps where needed to preserve sequences. (PDF p. 4).
- STRIKE scores aligned residue contacts using at least one known protein structure. When several structures are available, their scores are averaged. (PDF p. 4).

## Applications

- MO-SAStrE refines existing protein alignments when structural information is available for at least one sequence. (PDF p. 4).
- When no PDB structure is available, the implementation can replace STRIKE with PAM250. The authors describe this as a secondary use. (PDF p. 9).

## Optimization

- NSGA-II selects successive non-dominated fronts from parents and offspring. Crowding distance selects individuals when the last accepted front exceeds the remaining population capacity. (PDF p. 4).
- The configuration uses 100 individuals, up to 500 generations, 80% crossover probability and 20% mutation probability. (PDF p. 5).
- The algorithm stops at the generation limit or when the Pareto front remains unchanged across consecutive generations. (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- BAliBASE 3.0 supplies 218 protein sequence sets and reference alignments. BAliscore measures agreement with these references through a Sum-of-Pairs score. (PDF p. 2).
- Each problem is optimized ten times, producing 2,180 Pareto fronts. The parameter configuration is checked on 20 BAliBASE problems. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors use hypervolume to compare initial and optimized fronts. They use the Kruskal–Wallis test to assess improvement across repeated runs. (PDF p. 4).
- Comparisons with published aligners use 20 problems shared by BAliBASE 2.0 and 3.0. Pairwise Wilcoxon tests compare BAliscore results. (PDF p. 5).

## Results

- The authors report better hypervolume in every run of every problem. Mean improvement is 63.01%, rising to 70.34% for the less similar Ref.1 v.1 sequences. (PDF p. 6).
- All 218 problems show significant hypervolume improvement at the Kruskal–Wallis significance level of 0.01. (PDF p. 7).
- Wilcoxon comparisons favor MO-SAStrE over the listed genetic and non-genetic alternatives at P < 0.05, except 3D-COFFEE. The 3D-COFFEE comparison gives P = 0.562. (PDF p. 8).
- In the eight-problem comparison, MO-SAStrE exceeds MSA-GA and VDGA in seven problems and 3D-COFFEE in five. (PDF p. 7).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Improved objective values require more computation time than the initial alignment methods. (PDF p. 6).
- Limited PDB structure availability restricts use of the main structural objective. (PDF p. 8).
- The external comparisons cover small subsets shared between benchmark versions. Some comparator scores are the best results from repeated runs reported in earlier studies. (PDF p. 7).
- The method returns several non-dominated alignments, so it does not identify one best alignment across all three objectives. (PDF p. 5).
