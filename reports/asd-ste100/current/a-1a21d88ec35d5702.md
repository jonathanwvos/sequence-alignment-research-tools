## Executive summary

- The paper describes BNDM for single-pattern search and a Shift OR filter for multiple-pattern search. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors report lower search times for their multiple-pattern Shift OR implementation than for Aho–Corasick. The filter also reports more matches, which require verification. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- The experiment compares multiple-pattern Shift OR with Aho–Corasick by execution time and number of reported matches. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- The authors attribute the speed benefit of bit parallelism to packing several values into one computer word and updating them with one operation. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors argue that large pattern sets increase the memory needed for the Aho–Corasick trie. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- The stated worst-case time for BNDM is O(nm). This occurs when each window requires a full scan and advances by one character. (PDF p. 2).
- The stated Shift OR scan time is O(n). Its character-class matches are candidates that require a separate verification phase. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- BNDM scans each text window backwards. It updates its state with D′ = (D & B[tj]) << 1 and stops a scan when the state becomes zero. (PDF p. 2).
- The multiple-pattern filter clears bit i in B[c] when any pattern has character c at position i. It starts D with all bits set and updates D = (D << 1) | B[c]. (PDF p. 2).
- The filter has three phases: pattern preprocessing, candidate matching, and candidate verification. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The authors list text editing, virus scanning, digital libraries, web search, and intrusion detection as application areas. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors also identify searches for DNA sequences with possible mutations as an application. (PDF p. 4).

## Optimization

- The authors propose using character 2-grams to process two characters at a time and reduce false combinations. This proposal uses a 256×256 array. (PDF p. 4).
- They propose 3-grams as a further extension. They expect better filtering and speed at the cost of more memory. (PDF p. 4).

## Evaluation and testing

- The benchmark uses a 350 MB text on Windows 7 Professional with 8 GB RAM and a reported Intel Core i7-260 M CPU at 2.80 GHz. The system is 64-bit. (PDF p. 3).
- The stated pattern lengths are 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100 characters. The tables report match counts and elapsed time in milliseconds. (PDF p. 4).

## Results

- For the row labeled 20 characters, Shift OR takes 180 ms and Aho–Corasick takes 207 ms. For 100 characters, the times are 463 ms and 532 ms. (PDF p. 4).
- For the row labeled 100 characters, Shift OR reports 9,721,031 matches and Aho–Corasick reports 7,654,355. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors report that Aho–Corasick takes 10–20% more time. Shift OR reports 20–30% more matches. (PDF p. 3).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The described multiple-pattern method assumes equal pattern lengths with m ≤ w, where w is the computer word size. (PDF p. 2).
- Interpretation: additional filter matches do not establish better detection accuracy. The paper states that candidate matches need verification. (PDF p. 3).
- The benchmark labels are inconsistent. The tables identify pattern length in characters, but the graphs identify pattern-set size. (PDF p. 4).
- The experimental description does not specify the text source, verification cost, or how patterns longer than one word are processed. (PDF p. 3).
