## Executive summary

- The authors construct the transitive closure of an acyclic directed graph in O(n³/ln n) operations. (PDF p. 2).
- The algorithm combines a partition into ranks with precomputed unions for products of multivalued mappings. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- The authors seek to reduce the operation count for transitive closure from the cited O(n³) bound. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors cite a reduction from a general directed graph to its acyclic Herz graph. The first and last stages each require O(n²) operations. (PDF p. 1).
- The rank structure reduces transitive closure to a triangular sequence of mapping products. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- For mappings α: A → B and β: B → C, with set sizes p, q, and r, the lemma gives O((p + q)qr/ln q) operations for βα. (PDF p. 2).
- For a rank of size n_i, the proof bounds the mapping product by O(n²n_i/ln n). Summation over all ranks gives O(n³/ln n). (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- Place vertices without predecessors in the first rank. Each later rank contains remaining vertices whose predecessors are all in earlier ranks. (PDF p. 1).
- Compute Γ_i = γ_iG_(i−1) ∪ γ_i. This combines paths through earlier ranks with direct edges into rank i. (PDF p. 1).
- Partition B into blocks of at most ln q elements. For each block, enumerate all subsets and construct their images under β. (PDF p. 2).

## Applications

- The acyclic closure algorithm supplies the middle stage of the cited method for transitive closure of a general directed graph. (PDF p. 1).

## Optimization

- Index subsets by binary sums. Construct each nonsingleton subset image as the union of two images with smaller indices. (PDF p. 2).
- Precompute all block images in O(q²r/ln q) operations. Then combine the required images in O(pqr/ln q) operations. (PDF p. 2).
