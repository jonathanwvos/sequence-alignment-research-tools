## Executive summary

- The authors compare LCS methods and their use in sequence comparison. They distinguish length calculation from subsequence reconstruction. (PDF p. 1).
- The proposed changes remove empty prefixes and the zero boundary row and column from the dynamic programming table. (PDF p. 5).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to explain common LCS algorithms and identify opportunities to improve their time and space requirements. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that complexity comparisons must distinguish algorithms that return only the LCS length from algorithms that return an LCS. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors present LCS length as a measure of similarity between biological sequences. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- The review describes Hirschberg's method as a combination of divide-and-conquer and dynamic programming. It reports O(mn) time and O(m) space. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors report O(m+n) time for reconstruction by backtracking through the table. (PDF p. 4).

## Methods

- The presented algorithm stores LCS lengths in table C and traceback directions in table B. It initializes the first row and column to zero. (PDF p. 3).
- For matching symbols, the algorithm adds one to the diagonal predecessor. It records a diagonal direction for reconstruction. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The authors describe applications in DNA and protein comparison, gene investigation, and spelling correction. (PDF p. 1).

## Optimization

- The authors propose an m by n table in place of an (m+1) by (n+1) table to reduce initialization and iterations. (PDF p. 4).
- Traceback directions can be inferred from table C without table B. The authors state that total space remains quadratic because C is retained. (PDF p. 4).
- Length calculation can retain only the current and previous rows. The authors distinguish this reduction from full-table reconstruction. (PDF p. 4).

## Evaluation and testing

- The worked example compares CBABDCB with BDACBC and illustrates table construction and traceback. (PDF p. 3).

## Results

- The worked example reports an LCS length of four. It gives BABC and BDCB as possible longest common subsequences. (PDF p. 3).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Interpretation: The mismatch branch marked with a left arrow uses a diagonal value plus one. This conflicts with the stated leftward traceback direction. (PDF p. 3).
- Interpretation: Removing the boundary row and column does not demonstrate an asymptotic improvement. The proposed time reduction is stated as a possibility, without measurements. (PDF p. 5).
- Interpretation: The reconstruction claim concerns removal of table boundaries. It does not establish reconstruction from the two-row storage scheme discussed immediately before it. (PDF p. 5).
