## Executive summary

- Mean-field direct-coupling analysis, or mfDCA, predicts residue contacts from multiple-sequence alignments by separating direct from indirect correlations. (PDF p. 2).
- Across 131 domain families, an average of 84% of the top 20 predicted residue pairs were contacts under the selected structural evaluation. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to make DCA fast enough for broad evaluation and test whether it predicts native contacts beyond two-component signaling proteins. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- Indirect correlations can link residues that do not interact. The authors argue that contact prediction therefore requires direct-coupling inference rather than raw covariance alone. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors propose that compensating substitutions at contacting residues preserve favorable interactions and create detectable evolutionary correlations. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- Predicted contact distances have peaks near 3–5 Å and 7–8 Å. The authors suggest that these reflect short-range and longer-range interactions. (PDF p. 3).
- Direct-information predictions cover contact maps more evenly than mutual-information predictions. They also recover more contacts between residues far apart in sequence. (PDF p. 6).
- In two subsampled families, about 250 effective sequences supported high accuracy among the strongest predictions. Accuracy increased with more sequences. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- The input consists of local alignments to Pfam profile HMMs. Gaps form a 21st residue state. Sequences are weighted by the inverse count of neighbors above 80% identity. (PDF p. 7).
- A pairwise maximum-entropy model matches single-column and paired-column frequencies. The mean-field approximation estimates couplings as the negative inverse connected-correlation matrix. (PDF p. 7).
- For each residue pair, an isolated two-site model preserves single-site frequencies. Its mutual information defines direct information, which ranks candidate contacts. (PDF p. 8).

## Applications

- The authors propose predicted contacts as constraints for protein structure prediction and identification of oligomerization interfaces. (PDF p. 6).
- Strong couplings can also indicate alternative conformations, ligand-mediated relationships, or interdomain contacts. (PDF p. 2).

## Optimization

- Mean-field inference replaces iterative message passing with a single matrix-based coupling estimate. The authors report a speed increase of three to four orders of magnitude. (PDF p. 7).
- Pseudocounts regularize the correlation matrix. The study uses a pseudocount equal to the effective sequence count to avoid unstable couplings and spurious high scores. (PDF p. 7).
- The higher-order TAP approximation did not produce systematic improvement over mfDCA. (PDF p. 8).

## Evaluation and testing

- The benchmark contains 131 predominantly bacterial domain families and 856 structures. Each family has over 1,000 effective sequences and at least two high-quality crystal structures. (PDF p. 2).
- Evaluation excludes nearby sequence positions and defines contacts by minimum atomic distance below 8 Å. (PDF p. 2).
- The study compares direct-information rankings with mutual information and an approximate Bayesian method. The main average uses the structure with the highest true-positive rate for each family. (PDF p. 3).
- Additional checks use all 856 structures, 25 eukaryotic proteins, sequence subsampling, and reweighting variants. (PDF p. 3).

## Results

- The average contact rate among the top 20 predictions was 84%. mfDCA exceeded both mutual information and the approximate Bayesian comparator. (PDF p. 3).
- For the Sigma-70 region-2 example, 19 of the top 20 direct-information pairs were contacts. Mutual information recovered 13 contacts. (PDF p. 2).
- For trypsin, 25 of the top 30 predictions were contacts. Most of these contacts lay outside the functional sectors identified by statistical-coupling analysis. (PDF p. 6).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The method depends on sufficiently large and diverse sequence alignments. The main benchmark selects families with more than 1,000 effective sequences. (PDF p. 2).
- Working memory limited analysis to about 500 alignment columns on a standard computer. Direct comparison with message passing covered only two families because of its runtime. (PDF p. 7).
- mfDCA does not recover all biological information in pair correlations. Statistical-coupling analysis identifies functional sectors that differ from direct contact pairs. (PDF p. 6).
- The headline contact rate uses the best-predicted structure per family. I interpret it as performance against a favorable available conformation, rather than a fixed single structure. (PDF p. 3).
