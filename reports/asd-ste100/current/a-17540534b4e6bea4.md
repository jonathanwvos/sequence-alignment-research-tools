## Executive summary

- The authors propose a parallel dynamic programming algorithm for LCS. The C implementation uses OpenMP. (PDF p. 3).
- Tests used four processors and 2 GB RAM. Table 1 reports speedups from 0.0 to 3.23. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to reduce computation time for LCS comparisons of two DNA strings or patterns. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that dynamic programming avoids repeated subproblem calculations that make brute-force subsequence enumeration expensive. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- The authors give space complexity as Θ(MN) for the symbol and weight matrices. Parallel execution does not reduce this storage requirement. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors give serial time as Θ(MN) and parallel time as Θ(MN/Np), where Np is the processor count. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- The sequential description stores LCS lengths in table c and predecessor directions in table b. Traceback uses b to construct a subsequence. (PDF p. 2).
- The proposed parallel procedure assigns matrix blocks to processors. Workers update symbol and weight matrices and return these matrices and a length value to the root. (PDF p. 3).
- The procedure uses OpenMP thread identifiers to distinguish the root from workers. It measures elapsed time with omp_get_wtime. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The authors propose DNA matching and pattern matching as applications. (PDF p. 4).

## Optimization

- The algorithm distributes matrix work across processors through OpenMP. The authors describe the partition as block-stripped. (PDF p. 2).

## Evaluation and testing

- The implementation used C, gcc, Ubuntu 11.10, four processors, and 2 GB RAM. (PDF p. 3).
- Table 1 tests 13 input length pairs. Parent lengths range from 10 to 10000, and child lengths range from 5 to 1500. (PDF p. 3).
- The evaluation reports execution time and speedup. Figures compare serial and parallel timing and plot speedup against matrix size. (PDF p. 4).

## Results

- Table 1 reports 18.90 seconds and a speedup of 3.22 for M=5000 and N=200. It reports 31.67 seconds and a speedup of 3.23 for M=5000 and N=800. (PDF p. 3).
- At M=10000 and N=1500, the reported execution time is 57.99 seconds and the speedup is 2.89. (PDF p. 3).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Reviewer interpretation: the parallel pseudocode does not specify matrix dependency handling or how the root combines partial lengths. It only shows a same-index character comparison before an unspecified matrix update. (PDF p. 3).
- The text identifies M=5000 and N=200 as the highest-speedup case. This conflicts with the larger speedup listed in Table 1. (PDF p. 4).
- Reviewer interpretation: the conclusion that computation speeds up by Np times exceeds the reported four-processor results. (PDF p. 4).
- The supplied evaluation gives no sequence contents, repeated-run statistics, or correctness comparison for the parallel output. (PDF p. 3).
