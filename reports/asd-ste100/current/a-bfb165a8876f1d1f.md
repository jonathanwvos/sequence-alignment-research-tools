## Executive summary

- The authors investigate the physical origin of intrinsic DNA curvature in phased A-tracts using molecular dynamics. (PDF p. 1).
- Curvature emerges spontaneously from localized counterion interactions and sequence-dependent backbone dynamics. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- Determine whether intrinsic DNA bending is caused by counterion localization or base-stacking mechanics. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Unbiased molecular dynamics simulations in explicit solvent reproduce macroscopic curvature observed in experiments. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- Backbone conformational transitions (BI to BII) and bending angles are analyzed across 10-ns trajectories. (PDF p. 10).

## Methods

- Molecular dynamics simulations model DNA oligomers containing phased A-tracts at varying temperatures and ionic strengths. (PDF p. 2).
- Curvature angles are calculated from global helix axis distributions in explicit solvent. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- Understanding sequence-dependent DNA elasticity, chromatin organization, and protein-DNA recognition. (PDF p. 16).

Not stated in the extractable source pages.

## Evaluation and testing

- Simulation bending planes and temperature dependencies are compared against experimental gel mobility data. (PDF p. 13).

## Results

- Demonstrates that counterion localization in the minor groove and differential phosphate shielding govern intrinsic bending. (PDF p. 13).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Ten-nanosecond simulation trajectories remain limited in sampling slow macromolecular conformational relaxation. (PDF p. 10).
