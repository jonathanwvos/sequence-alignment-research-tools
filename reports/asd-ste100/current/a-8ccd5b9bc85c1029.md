## Executive summary

- The authors extend E-score with Ankh embeddings for local protein alignment. They also introduce a framework to evaluate alignment localization and quality. (PDF p. 2).
- The benchmark uses natural and inserted domains from CDD, BAliBASE, and GPCRdb. It reports 2,567,610 tests, counting repeated dataset uses and distance calculations. (PDF p. 3).
- Ankh-score won more domain comparisons than each tested competitor in the reported overall comparison. (PDF p. 10).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to identify the most accurate local protein alignment method and develop a suitable evaluation protocol. (PDF p. 2).
- The study examines how embedding models behave on natural sequences and sequences with inserted domains. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that local alignment evaluation must assess both domain localization and alignment within the selected subsequences. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors argue that previous PEbA tests did not adequately evaluate local alignment. They cite limited flanking sequence, reliance on SP and F1 scores, and unmeasured overhangs. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- The framework uses five distances. Three treat gaps differently: d_ssp ignores gaps, d_seq labels gaps by sequence, and d_pos also labels their positions. (PDF p. 4).
- Relative displacement distance, d_d, measures residue position differences. Closest context distance, d_cc, measures distance to the nearest position with the same context. (PDF p. 4).
- Pairwise method comparisons use Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Benjamini-Hochberg correction. Plots and summaries include only comparisons with corrected p-values below 0.01. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors suggest that unnatural insert sequences can disrupt embedding context. They state that the full explanation of model differences remains unresolved. (PDF p. 12).

## Methods

- E-score computes local alignments by dynamic programming with affine gap penalties. (PDF p. 3).
- Ankh-score is the cosine similarity between Ankh residue embeddings. Gap opening and extension penalties are −1 and −0.1. (PDF p. 4).
- The evaluation algorithm clips a computed alignment to its overlap with the reference domains. It pads missed domain residues with alignments that pair residues only with gaps. (PDF p. 4).
- If either computed subsequence does not overlap its reference domain, the algorithm returns the worst alignment of both complete domains. (PDF p. 4).

## Applications

- The authors expect more accurate local alignments to support remote homology detection, domain identification, and protein annotation. (PDF p. 2).
- The method, protocol, and data are available through the E-score web server and source repository. (PDF p. 13).

## Optimization

- The authors selected Ankh after comparing seven embedding methods on CDD natural data. Ankh performed best under all five distances. (PDF p. 5).
- A constant downward score shift reduces alignment overhangs. The authors report little effect on alignment quality and provide this adjustment to users. (PDF p. 4).
- Gap sensitivity tests covered opening penalties from −1.2 to −0.8 and extension penalties from −0.12 to −0.08. Performance remained stable on CDD natural domains. (PDF p. 5).
- Combining Ankh with other embeddings did not improve results reliably. Ankh alone also exceeded the tested combination of other models. (PDF p. 10).

## Evaluation and testing

- The datasets contain 132 domains and 2631 sequences. Natural datasets use CDD and BAliBASE RV11, RV12, and RV30; insert datasets also include RV911–RV913 and GPCRdb. (PDF p. 3).
- Where possible, domains have at least 30 flanking residues at each end and at least 15 sequences. RV11 and RV12 required exceptions to the sequence-count condition. (PDF p. 3).
- Comparators include BLOSUM matrices, GPCRtm, ProtT5-score, ProstT5-score, DEDAL, and pLM-BLAST. ProtT5-score represents the PEbA scoring approach; vcMSA is excluded because it computes global alignments. (PDF p. 5).
- The framework reports mean distance to reference alignments and the number of tests won for each domain and distance. (PDF p. 4).

## Results

- Across natural datasets and distance metrics, Ankh won 144 domain comparisons against best BLOSUM, which won none. On insert datasets, the counts were 373 and 61. (PDF p. 10).
- Against ProtT5, Ankh won 89 natural-data domain comparisons and lost 11. On insert data, it won 227 and lost 126. (PDF p. 10).
- On natural data, Ankh won 135 domain comparisons against DEDAL, which won nine. It won 145 against pLM-BLAST, which won none. (PDF p. 10).
- ProstT5 won more domain comparisons than ProtT5 on natural data, by 69 to 20. ProtT5 won more on insert data, by 331 to 66. (PDF p. 10).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors collected relatively few suitable natural domains. Some examples still have little flanking sequence relative to total protein length. (PDF p. 12).
- The framework cannot determine the correct overhang length because no gold standard is available for sequence outside the reference alignment. (PDF p. 4).
- Insert-data comparisons were restricted to BLOSUM and the strongest competitors. DEDAL and pLM-BLAST were evaluated only on natural data in the overall comparison. (PDF p. 10).
- The method computes alignments but does not provide database search. The authors identify acceleration for search as unresolved work. (PDF p. 12).

DOI: 10.1016/j.csbj.2025.12.002 Abstract Background: The advent of protein embeddings has revolutionized bioinformatics by providing contextual representations that capture functional and evolutionary patterns. They have become, alongside sequence alignments, the cornerstone of bioinformatics. Embeddings cannot replace alignments but they can greatly help improve their quality. While embedding-based improvements have been considered for global alignments, the more important counterpart, local alignments, has not been studied thoroughly. Our goal is to identify the most accurate local alignment algorithm for protein sequences. Results: We introduce a new scoring function into our previous E-score algorithm by using Ankh embeddings. We prove that the resulting algorithm produces the most accurate local alignments of protein sequences using a new comprehensive framework that enables thorough evaluation of local alignment quality. We design a new algorithm for local alignment extraction, localization and quality evaluation and employ five distance metrics to evaluate the similarity with the true alignment. We also build multiple datasets, using both natural and inserted sequences, from the Conserved Domain Database, BAliBASE, and GPCRdb. We perform over two and a half million tests to compare the new algorithm with the best BLOSUM matrices, specialized GPCRtm matrices, and top programs, such as PEbA, DEDAL, vcMSA and pLM-BLAST. Our testing also reveals interesting insights into the behaviour of various protein language models as some of them perform much better on natural sequences compared to artificial ones obtained by inserting domains into random protein sequences. Also, while some models combine to produce better results, Ankh does not combine well with other embeddings. Conclusions: The new, Ankh-score-based, program is clearly superior to all existing methods. New light shed on the protein embeddings can guide future improvements. In order to facilitate the use of the new method and protocol, they are freely available as a web server at e-score.csd.uwo.ca and as source code at github.com/lucian-ilie/E-score.
