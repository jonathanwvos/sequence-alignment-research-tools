## Executive summary

- The authors introduce the DALI algorithm for comparing 3D protein structures using 2D distance matrices. (PDF p. 1).
- The method identifies contact patterns and aligns them using Monte Carlo optimization. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- Detect topological similarities between protein 3D structures independent of sequence similarity. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- Internal distance matrices are invariant under rigid-body rotation and translation. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- Alignment quality is scored by comparing contact submatrices and penalizing distance deviations. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- The algorithm calculates intra-molecular C-alpha distance matrices for each protein. (PDF p. 2).
- Hexapeptide fragment pairs with similar distance patterns are assembled using Monte Carlo search. (PDF p. 5).

## Applications

- Automated classification of known protein structures and discovery of remote fold similarities. (PDF p. 13).

## Optimization

- Hexapeptides are decomposed into overlapping tetrapeptides to refine fragment boundaries. (PDF p. 5).

## Evaluation and testing

- The authors test DALI on diverse structural pairs including globins and ribonucleases. (PDF p. 13).

## Results

- DALI successfully discovers both known fold classes and non-obvious topological similarities. (PDF p. 14).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The Monte Carlo optimization is heuristic and may fail to identify the globally optimal alignment. (PDF p. 5).
