## Executive summary

- The authors introduce sparse dynamic programming algorithms for sequence alignment and RNA structure with linear gap costs. (PDF p. 1).
- The method computes recurrences over M sparse fragment points in O(n + M log log min(M, n^2/M)) time. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- Exploit sparsity in dynamic programming recurrences when the number of candidate match points M is much less than n^2. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Computing only the active regions of influence avoids evaluating the entire quadratic dynamic programming matrix. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- The authors prove that the total number of active regions created during point insertion is bounded by 2M. (PDF p. 22).

## Methods

- Active search intervals are maintained using priority queues and van Emde Boas data structures. (PDF p. 8).
- Points are processed in topological order, updating active influence regions in O(log log min) time per point. (PDF p. 25).

## Applications

- Wilbur-Lipman sequence alignment and RNA secondary structure prediction with single loops. (PDF p. 3).

## Optimization

- Replacing C-trees with Johnson priority queues improves operation bounds to double-logarithmic time. (PDF p. 26).

## Evaluation and testing

Not stated in the extractable source pages.

## Results

- Solves Wilbur and Lipmans fragment alignment problem in O(m + n + M log log min(M, nm/M)) time. (PDF p. 25).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The double-logarithmic speedup requires strictly linear cost functions. (PDF p. 3).
