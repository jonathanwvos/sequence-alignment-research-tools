## Executive summary

- The authors present nf-core/multiplesequencealign for deploying and comparing protein multiple sequence alignment methods. (PDF p. 3).
- The framework separates guide tree computation, alignment assembly, and evaluation. It can run alternative tools and parameter settings in parallel. (PDF p. 3).
- Nextflow DSL2 modules and controlled software environments support portability, reuse, and integration of new methods. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to provide a common platform for MSA deployment, performance evaluation, and benchmark reporting. (PDF p. 2).
- The framework aims to support both production alignment and benchmark-based selection of methods and parameters. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that variable tool performance across datasets makes method selection difficult for users and comparative evaluation difficult for developers. (PDF p. 1).
- Parameter combinations can invoke different algorithms. The authors therefore treat each configuration as a distinct benchmark procedure. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors argue that evaluation metric selection defines biological optimality and must be considered alongside computational optimization. (PDF p. 7).

## Analysis

- Each toolsheet procedure runs on each samplesheet dataset. This forms a Cartesian product of datasets and configurations. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors identify guide tree computation as a possible bottleneck. An all-sequence distance matrix typically requires O(N²) operations. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors distinguish evolutionary and structural alignment correctness. They state that these criteria are not known to be equivalent. (PDF p. 5).

## Methods

- The six stages validate inputs, compute guide trees, assemble alignments, optionally form a consensus, evaluate alignments, and produce reports. (PDF p. 3).
- Version 1.1.1 includes 13 alignment methods. Structural methods include FoldMason, mTM-Align, and 3D-Coffee. (PDF p. 4).
- M-Coffee builds a consensus from pairwise projections of input alignments. It uses these projections in a consistency-based scoring scheme for progressive alignment. (PDF p. 4).
- A new method requires a Nextflow module and an update to the relevant subworkflow. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- Users can compare guide tree and assembly combinations, then select a procedure for production datasets using external quality evidence. (PDF p. 3).
- The pipeline accepts predicted structures from nf-core/proteinfold for structure-supported alignment. (PDF p. 6).

## Optimization

- Separate guide tree and assembly stages permit systematic exploration of component combinations. (PDF p. 3).
- Toolsheets permit parallel comparison of tool parameters and guide tree choices. (PDF p. 4).
- Available guide tree speed-up options include PartTree, bisecting k-means, and MedoidTree. (PDF p. 5).

## Evaluation and testing

- A small dataset supports continuous integration testing. A full-size dataset supports pipeline testing on AWS. (PDF p. 2).
- T-Coffee computes sum-of-pairs and total-column agreement against user-provided reference alignments. (PDF p. 5).
- Other metrics include TCS, gap counts, and NiRMSD. NiRMSD requires at least two protein structures. (PDF p. 5).
- Nextflow tracing and nf-co2footprint support resource reporting, including CPU use, memory consumption, and runtime. (PDF p. 5).

## Results

- The implemented pipeline supports sequence inputs, structure inputs, or both, according to the selected tools. (PDF p. 3).
- Reports combine resource measurements with alignment quality metrics in CSV output. MultiQC and a Shiny interface support result inspection. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors provide public example outputs from runs on full-size datasets. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The implemented pipeline is explicitly designed for protein sequences. (PDF p. 3).
- The framework does not resolve the lack of clear and unbiased MSA benchmark datasets. (PDF p. 7).
- Iterative alignment procedures remain inside their dedicated software, including iterations that recompute guide trees. (PDF p. 4).
- Further separation of methods into reusable algorithmic components requires continued work by the development community. (PDF p. 8).
