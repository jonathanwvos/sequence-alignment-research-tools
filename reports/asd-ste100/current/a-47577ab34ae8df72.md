## Executive summary

- Boltz-1 predicts 3D biomolecular complexes from protein sequences, ligand SMILES strings, and nucleic acid sequences. Inputs also include MSAs and molecular conformations. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors report accuracy comparable to AlphaFold3 and Chai-1 on a recent PDB test set and CASP15. (PDF p. 18).
- Boltz-1x adds inference-time steering. The authors report improved physical quality while maintaining geometric accuracy. (PDF p. 18).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to make training code, inference code, weights, datasets, and benchmarks available under the MIT license. (PDF p. 2).
- Boltz-steering aims to reduce chain overlap and nonphysical geometry in predicted structures. (PDF p. 11).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that geometric accuracy metrics can fail to penalize physical errors strongly. Such errors can prevent downstream use, including molecular dynamics calculations. (PDF p. 11).
- The authors argue that filtering completed diffusion samples is insufficient when valid conformations are rare. They instead steer the sampling process with physical potentials. (PDF p. 11).

## Analysis

- The trunk uses token-pair representations and axial attention. Its computational complexity grows cubically with input token count. Independence from diffusion time and structure allows one trunk evaluation per complex. (PDF p. 6).
- Greedy symmetry correction first selects chain assignments, then selects atom permutations within residues or ligands. It uses global LDDT and updates only affected distance-matrix rows and columns. (PDF p. 10).
- The authors caution that recycling and sampling ablation metrics are noisy. They advise against strong conclusions from small inconsistencies. (PDF p. 18).

## Methods

- Training structures come from PDB releases before 2021-09-30. Reference polymer sequences are aligned to resolved residues. ColabFold search builds MSAs with MMseqs2 and default parameters. (PDF p. 3).
- The MSA module places PairWeightedAveraging and MSATransition before OuterProductMean. This lets updated single representations feed directly into pair representations. (PDF p. 6).
- Diffusion transformer layers apply attention and transition updates in sequence. Each update has a residual connection. (PDF p. 6).
- Steering uses physical potentials for ligand distance geometry, inter-chain clashes, symmetric chain separation, and covalent links between chains. Ligand geometry bounds come from RDKit. (PDF p. 14).

## Applications

- The model accepts proteins, nucleic acids, ligands, and covalent bonds to predict biomolecular complexes. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors propose the released models as a platform for drug design and structural biology research. (PDF p. 18).

## Optimization

- Structure-independent attention biases and atom representations are computed once. They are reused across samples and reverse diffusion steps. This reduces computation but requires additional memory. (PDF p. 10).
- Chunking reduces inference peak memory in triangular attention and the MSA module. MSA chunking covers transition, pair-weighted averaging, and outer product layers. (PDF p. 10).
- The Triton trifast kernel uses chunked online softmax with an inner bias term. It reduces triangular attention memory complexity from O(n³) to O(n²). (PDF p. 11).
- Symmetry correction limits chain assignment candidates to 100 and atom permutation candidates per ligand to 1,000. (PDF p. 10).

## Evaluation and testing

- The final comparison uses 66 CASP15 structures and 541 PDB test structures. Structures are excluded if any method fails or exceeds memory on an A100 80 GB GPU. (PDF p. 15).
- AlphaFold3, Chai-1, and Boltz models use 200 sampling steps, 10 recycling rounds, and five outputs. They use the same precomputed MSAs with up to 16,384 sequences. (PDF p. 15).
- Accuracy metrics include all-atom LDDT, DockQ success above 0.23, protein-ligand interface LDDT, and pocket-aligned ligand RMSD below 2 Å. (PDF p. 15).
- Evaluation uses OpenStructure 2.8.0 and PoseBusters physical rules. It reports the best sample and the highest-confidence sample from five predictions. Physical checks cover geometry, chirality, stereochemistry, and inter-chain clashes. (PDF p. 16).

## Results

- The authors report comparable results for AlphaFold3, Chai-1, and Boltz-1 across metrics on both benchmarks. (PDF p. 16).
- The authors report that Boltz-1x improves physical quality while maintaining Boltz-1 geometric accuracy. (PDF p. 18).
- With three recycling rounds, increasing sampling from 20 to 200 steps raises top-1 mean LDDT from 0.693 to 0.716. These ablations use MSAs with up to 4,096 sequences. (PDF p. 18).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Architectural changes were tested on smaller models. The authors expect these findings to transfer but provide no direct ablations on the final full-size model. (PDF p. 6).
- The PDB comparison excludes covalently bound ligands because the tested public Chai-1 repository cannot specify them. (PDF p. 15).
- The authors observed overlapping chains, steric clashes, incorrect bond geometry, stereochemical errors, and nonplanar aromatic rings in unsteered predictions. (PDF p. 11).
- The recycling and sampling ablations use different MSA parameters and a different surviving structure set from the main comparison. The authors state that these differences affect direct comparison. (PDF p. 18).

6. Conclusion We introduced Boltz-1, the first fully commercially accessible open-source model to achieve Al- phaFold3-level accuracy in predicting the 3D structures of biomolecular complexes. To accomplish this, we replicated and expanded upon the AlphaFold3 technical report, incorporating several in- novations in architecture, data curation, training, and inference processes. We empirically validated Boltz-1 against AlphaFold3 and Chai-1, the current state-of-the-art structure prediction methods, demonstrating comparable performance on both a diverse test set and the CASP15 benchmark. Further, we introduced Boltz-1x an updated model that leverages Boltz-steering, a new inference time technique, to significantly improve the physical quality of the poses generated while maintaining their geometric accuracy of Boltz-1. The open-source releases of Boltz-1 and Boltz-1x represent significant steps forward in democratiz- ing access to advanced biomolecular modeling tools and improving their applicability across domains. By freely providing the training and inference code, model weights, and datasets under the MIT li- cense, we aim to enable researchers and organizations to experiment and innovate using Boltz-1 and Boltz-1x. We envision Boltz models as a foundational platform for researchers to build upon, foster- ing collaboration to advance our collective understanding of biomolecular interactions and accelerating breakthroughs in drug design, structural biology, and beyond.
