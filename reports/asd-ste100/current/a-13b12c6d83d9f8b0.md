## Executive summary

- Miniprot maps proteins to complete genomes with a seed-chain-extend algorithm and spliced dynamic programming. (PDF p. 2).
- The author reports accuracy comparable to existing tools, with substantially shorter execution times on the tested datasets. (PDF p. 4).

## Objectives

- Develop a fast protein-to-genome aligner that supports gene annotation and growing genome and protein datasets. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The author argues that protein alignment is useful across large evolutionary distances because coding nucleotide sequences can be poorly conserved. (PDF p. 1).
- Efficient protein-to-genome alignment must handle affine gaps, frameshifts, introns, and splice signals together. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- The author expects accuracy comparable to GeneWise and Exonerate under equal parameter settings because the DP resembles their six-state HMM decoding. (PDF p. 3).
- Spaln2 detects more small exons and introns among proteins mapped by both tools. The author suggests that splice modeling and heuristics could explain this difference. (PDF p. 4).
- The author suggests that fast DP through all introns helps miniprot maintain stable performance as sequence divergence increases. (PDF p. 4).

## Methods

- Miniprot samples translated genome 6-mers from ORFs of at least 30 amino acids on both strands. It finds query anchors and chains them. (PDF p. 2).
- Initial chaining uses binned genome positions and a logarithmic gap penalty. A second pass uses all unbinned 5-mers near the top 30 chains. (PDF p. 2).
- Residue DP handles affine gaps, one-base and two-base frameshifts, and three intron phases. Donor and acceptor penalties model splice signals. (PDF p. 3).
- To reduce pseudogene preference, miniprot recalculates scores against translated coding regions without introns. It also penalizes single-exon alignments. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- Miniprot combines approximate mapping and precise splice alignment to assist gene annotation pipelines. (PDF p. 4).
- The author proposes using miniprot to evaluate de novo assemblies when proteins from closely related species are available. (PDF p. 4).

## Optimization

- A reduced amino acid alphabet uses four bits per symbol. Hash sampling retains approximately half of the genome k-mers. (PDF p. 2).
- Genome positions use 256-base bins and 32-bit integers. This avoids the larger index required by 64-bit positions for a human genome. (PDF p. 2).
- Striped DP uses 16-bit scores and eight-way SIMD on SSE2 or NEON. The author reports more than 50-fold acceleration over exact-mode GeneWise and Exonerate. (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- The benchmark maps zebrafish and mouse proteins to human GRCh38, and mosquito proteins to fruit fly BDGP6. (PDF p. 4).
- The full comparison uses miniprot 0.7, Spaln2 2.4.13c, and GeMoMa 1.9. GenomeThreader was excluded after low sensitivity in a preliminary test. (PDF p. 4).
- Junction confirmation requires exact agreement with annotated boundaries. Base sensitivity measures annotated coding coverage. Base specificity measures the fraction of aligned bases within annotated coding regions. (PDF p. 5).
- GeMoMa used a 50 kb intron limit after the 200 kb setting exceeded 320 GB of memory. It crashed on the human–mouse dataset. (PDF p. 4).

## Results

- For zebrafish proteins mapped to human, miniprot with the human splice model took 267 seconds. Spaln2 took 10,708 seconds, and GeMoMa took 8,718 seconds. (PDF p. 5).
- In that comparison, confirmed junction fractions were 92.16% for miniprot, 90.44% for Spaln2, and 75.10% for GeMoMa. (PDF p. 5).
- For mouse proteins mapped to human, miniprot achieved 90.10% base sensitivity and 97.26% base specificity in 164 seconds. (PDF p. 5).
- For mosquito proteins mapped to fruit fly, Spaln2 found 22,606 confirmed junctions versus 19,774 for miniprot. Miniprot took 34 seconds versus 2,528 seconds for Spaln2. (PDF p. 5).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The DP does not score codons split by Phase-1 or Phase-2 introns. (PDF p. 3).
- Miniprot can merge small exons and introns into adjacent alignments. (PDF p. 4).
- Miniprot aligns proteins independently. It cannot merge identical gene models or resolve conflicts at the same locus. (PDF p. 4).
- Its splice model is not trained on the target genome. It also lacks the ab initio prediction component needed to find genes with weak protein homology. (PDF p. 4).
