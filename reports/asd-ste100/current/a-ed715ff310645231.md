## Executive summary

- Reseek aligns protein structures through discrete residue features and local sequence alignment. Its default combined alphabet has 85,899,345,920 states. (PDF p. 3).
- The author reports greater remote homolog sensitivity than DALI, TMalign and Foldseek on SCOP40. Reseek E-values also agreed more closely with measured error rates. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- The method aims to improve remote homolog detection while supporting fast searches and reliable estimates of false-positive counts. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The author argues that small structural alphabets discard useful information. Factoring structural states into separate features permits a large combined alphabet without directly training its full score matrix. (PDF p. 2).
- The author favors coverage versus error plots because they show sensitivity at a specified false-positive count. The author argues that ROC and precision–recall plots do not directly support this search decision. (PDF p. 4).

## Analysis

- False-positive errors per query increase with database size. The author notes that extrapolation to the AlphaFold database also depends on its unknown number of superfamilies. (PDF p. 3).
- The author treats 0.01 false-positive errors per query as the lowest rate that SCOP40 can measure reliably. The reported coverage versus error plot extends to ten errors per query. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- Features describe amino acid type, distances to nearest and reverse Euclidean neighbors, and local backbone conformation. Local conformation uses pairwise distances within a seven-position window by default. (PDF p. 2).
- Scalar features are divided into bins with approximately equal frequencies. Vector features use K-means clusters. Each feature receives its own discrete alphabet and log-odds score matrix. (PDF p. 2).
- Residue-pair scores sum weighted feature log-odds scores. Smith–Waterman or a BLAST-like method constructs local alignments with affine gap penalties. The default representation combines amino acid type with eight 16-letter structural features. (PDF p. 3).
- Alignment quality, AQ, combines LDDT-mu, alignment score, a reversed-query alignment score and length terms. Parameters use SCOP40 training with hold-out validation. An empirical distribution converts AQ to E-values. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- CatE evaluation represents functional category assignment from the top search hit. A correct assignment requires that the hit be a homolog. (PDF p. 3).
- An optional Kabsch transformation produces a rigid structural superposition for visualization of a local alignment. (PDF p. 3).

## Optimization

- Optional filters reject comparisons before alignment construction and AQ calculation. The excerpt does not specify the filter algorithms. (PDF p. 3).
- Separate feature matrices reduce sparse-data problems when estimating scores for rare structural states. (PDF p. 2).
- Feature assignment uses thresholds or nearest cluster representatives. The author suggests that this may be faster than the neural classifier used for Foldseek 3Di assignment. (PDF p. 2).

## Evaluation and testing

- Score matrices were trained on a subset of SCOP40 alignments generated by TMalign, with TM-scores between 0.6 and 0.8. (PDF p. 3).
- All-versus-all testing used SCOP40 v1.75, with 11,206 domains in 1,960 superfamilies and maximum sequence identity of 40%. Domains in the same superfamily were treated as homologs. (PDF p. 3).
- The comparison used Reseek 2.0, DALILite 5, TMalign 2022/4/12 and Foldseek 8-ef4e960. Coverage versus error plots measured sensitivity against false-positive errors per query. CatE plots assessed top-hit assignments. (PDF p. 3).
- E-value calibration compared reported thresholds with measured false-positive errors per query. Agreement requires the observed error count to match the E-value. (PDF p. 4).

## Results

- The author reports that Reseek had higher homolog sensitivity than the tested alternatives, with faster execution and lower memory use than Foldseek in the SCOP40 test. (PDF p. 3).
- At a Foldseek E-value threshold of 0.000001, the measured false-positive errors per query were approximately 0.1. This was five orders of magnitude above the estimate. (PDF p. 4).
- Reseek E-values were in good agreement with measured error rates in the reported SCOP40 evaluation. (PDF p. 4).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The main evaluation uses SCOP40 domains. Scaling its accuracy results to much larger predicted-structure databases requires assumptions about their homolog distribution. (PDF p. 3).
- The excerpt reports hold-out validation but does not give the split design. Detailed filters and empirical E-value fitting are deferred to supplementary material. (PDF p. 3).
