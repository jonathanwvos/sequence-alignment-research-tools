## Executive summary

- SSCLCS seeks a longest common subsequence that contains specified strings as substrings in a fixed order. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors give separate algorithms for constraints that cannot overlap and constraints that may overlap. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- The objective is to combine sequence-inclusion and string-inclusion constraints in one problem. Single-character partitions and a single string partition recover the earlier inclusion variants. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors also aim to improve the time bounds for one or two constraint strings. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- A constraint must be consecutive in the output. Its characters can therefore be matched by direct jumps through the input, which removes unused states from the earlier three-dimensional formulation. (PDF p. 3).
- For a fixed constraint end position, the latest feasible start leaves at least as much prefix space for the remaining solution. Theorem 1 uses these starts to characterize an optimum. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- The non-overlapping correctness proof separates ordinary LCS extensions from completion of a constraint. Invalid or incomplete constraint states retain the value −∞. (PDF p. 5).
- The overlapping proof handles adjacent constraints through equal suffix and prefix strings. Nearest backward matches support the transition between constraint layers. (PDF p. 8).
- For overlapping constraints, each DP cell examines O(l_k) overlap choices for partition k. Summing partition lengths gives O(mnr) DP time. (PDF p. 8).

## Methods

- PrevMatch tables store the previous position of each symbol. Backward lookups construct StartPos tables for feasible constraint endings. (PDF p. 3).
- The single-constraint algorithm uses two DP layers. Layer 0 stores ordinary LCS values, and layer 1 records satisfaction of the constraint. (PDF p. 3).
- The non-overlapping algorithm adds one layer per partition. A completed partition jumps to the preceding layer before its StartPos positions and adds its length. (PDF p. 5).
- The overlapping algorithm computes valid overlap sets W_k. It uses M1 for satisfied constraints and M2 for solutions whose final constraint ends at the output end. (PDF p. 7).

## Applications

- The authors propose distance restrictions between partitions as future work that could support motif finding. (PDF p. 8).

## Optimization

- PrevMatch preprocessing takes O((m + n)|Σ|) time. StartPos preprocessing takes O((m + n)r) time and permits constant-time cells for the single-constraint DP. (PDF p. 5).
- For two constraints with arbitrary order, the authors run the ordered algorithm twice, once for each order. (PDF p. 7).

## Results

- The single-constraint algorithm takes O(mn + (m + n)(|Σ| + r)) time and O(mn + (m + n)|Σ|) space. (PDF p. 5).
- For l non-overlapping partitions of total length r, the reported time and space are O(mnl + (m + n)(|Σ| + r)). (PDF p. 8).
- For overlapping partitions, the reported time is O(mnr + (m + n)|Σ|). Space is O(mnl + (m + n)(|Σ| + r)). (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The overlapping variant requires increasing start and end positions. The authors restrict their treatment to this order condition. (PDF p. 2).
- Distance restrictions between partitions and exclusion of sequential substrings remain proposed extensions. (PDF p. 8).
