## Executive summary

- PLM-GUARD tests whether protein search scores support biological interpretation. It contains six checks across biological fidelity, semantic validity, and manipulation safety. (PDF p. 2).
- Across eight methods, the authors report greater diagnostic stability for alignment-based search than for fully embedding-based search. (PDF p. 6).

## Objectives

- The authors assess score behavior under controlled perturbations. They aim to complement retrieval benchmarks with tests of biological meaning. (PDF p. 7).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that successful retrieval does not establish that a similarity score is reliable biological evidence. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors interpret the intermediate behavior of NEAR as evidence that residue-level correspondence helps preserve biological meaning. They also state that hybrid methods still need diagnostic checks. (PDF p. 7).

## Analysis

- NEAR approximately doubles its score when the query is duplicated because it sums residue-level matches. The authors treat this as a redundancy-stability failure. (PDF p. 5).
- The formal monotonicity loss penalizes score increases after truncation. It assigns zero loss to unchanged scores, so it does not require a strict decrease. (PDF p. 10).
- Data-manipulation AUROC treats all original targets, including nonhomologs, as positives and shuffled decoys as negatives. It measures target-decoy separation rather than homology accuracy. (PDF p. 11).

## Methods

- Evolutionary tests use Pyvolve with the WAG substitution model and ASTRAL40 domains. Mutated queries search the database with the original sequence retained. Analysis keeps the top 1,000 hits. (PDF p. 8).
- Structural tests mutate sequences and relax their structures. Cα-RMSD, Rosetta repulsive energy, and TM-score assess whether structure remains similar. (PDF p. 4).
- Semantic tests append duplicate or shuffled query content and remove sequence content. Truncation retains random contiguous segments of 50% and 25% of query length. (PDF p. 10).
- Manipulation tests use shuffled sequence decoys and perturbed PLMs. Model perturbations permute selected fully connected weight matrices, including internal layers for DCTdomain. (PDF p. 11).

## Applications

- The framework can assess whether search scores support annotation transfer and homology claims. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors propose future use of diagnostic constraints during model training and target-decoy methods for calibrated error control. (PDF p. 7).

## Evaluation and testing

- The evaluated methods are BLASTp, DIAMOND, MMseqs2, NEAR, DCTdomain, DHR, PLMSearch, and TM-Vec. (PDF p. 3).
- Evolutionary evaluation uses AUPRC to assess separation between adjacent mutation stages. Structural evaluation uses AUROC for remote homologs versus nonhomologs. (PDF p. 4).
- Redundancy error uses mean absolute score change normalized by the original-score 99th percentile. Truncation error uses the positive score increase with the same normalization. (PDF p. 10).
- The decoy benchmark defines homologs through shared SCOP family or superfamily labels. Shuffling preserves sequence length and amino acid composition. (PDF p. 10).

## Results

- Alignment methods separated adjacent mutation stages more reliably than fully PLM-based methods. NEAR showed intermediate behavior. (PDF p. 3).
- DIAMOND and MMseqs2 showed the clearest structural consistency. PLM-based methods showed larger score shifts under structure-preserving mutation. (PDF p. 4).
- PLM-based methods showed larger score changes under redundant query augmentation and more monotonicity violations under truncation. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors report that shuffled decoys and perturbed PLMs can produce scores that compete with genuine targets. (PDF p. 6).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The model-manipulation test applies only to methods with learned PLM representations. (PDF p. 11).
- DIAMOND and MMseqs2 are absent from the scored decoy comparison because their prefilters remove decoys before explicit scoring. (PDF p. 5).
- Truncation analysis retains only homologs returned within the top 1,000 hits for both query versions. I interpret this as a limit on coverage of lost retrievals. (PDF p. 10).
- The authors identify sequence length, domain architecture, disorder, taxonomy, and low complexity as factors for future stratified evaluation. (PDF p. 7).

Manipulation safety tests whether high similarity can be hijacked without genuine homology through manipulation of the data or the model. (a) The data manipulation safety test asks whether shuffled decoy sequences can intrude into homolog detection. (b) PLM-based methods rank shuffled decoys much higher relative to targets, as reflected by more dispersed AUROC than BLASTp and NEAR; MMseqs2 and DIAMOND are omitted because their pre- filtering design filters out decoys before explicit similarity scores can be assigned. (f) PLM-based methods rank decoy PLMs much more competitively against targets, as reflected by deviated AUROC under model manipulation; this applies only to PLM-based methods, not to classical search methods.
