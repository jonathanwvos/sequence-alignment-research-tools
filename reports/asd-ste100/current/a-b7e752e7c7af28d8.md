## Executive summary

- Minimap2 maps nucleotide sequences with minimizer seeds, anchor chaining, and optional base-level alignment. (PDF p. 2).
- The same base algorithm supports short reads, long genomic reads, spliced reads, and assembly comparisons through different parameter settings. (PDF p. 4).
- The author reports substantial speed gains in several benchmarks. Accuracy depends on the input type and comparison tool. (PDF p. 6).

## Objectives

- Develop an efficient aligner for long noisy reads, full-length RNA reads, and very long assembled sequences. (PDF p. 1).
- Evaluate speed, accuracy, and versatility with simulated and real datasets. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The author argues that long sequences permit more effective avoidance of repetitive seeds, which can reduce mapping time. (PDF p. 1).
- The author argues that fixed-length hash-table seeds are efficient for long noisy sequences. Multiple seed hits permit repetitive seeds to be skipped. (PDF p. 6).

## Analysis

- Direct chaining takes O(N²) time for N anchors. The stopping heuristic reduces average time to O(hN), with h = 50 used in practice. (PDF p. 2).
- Mapping quality uses an empirical formula based on the primary chain score, its best secondary score, and its anchor count. (PDF p. 2).
- The seed-based divergence estimate is ln(n/m)/k. Its Spearman correlation with divergence from base-level alignments is about 0.9 on the reported long-read datasets. (PDF p. 2).
- The Suzuki–Kasahara recurrence stores score differences bounded by scoring parameters. Suitable parameter bounds permit 8-bit values and 16-way SSE vectorization regardless of peak alignment score. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- Index reference minimizers in a hash table. Find query anchors, chain colinear anchors with dynamic programming, and align between anchors and beyond chain ends. (PDF p. 2).
- Use a two-piece affine gap cost for genomic alignment. The lower extension cost for long gaps helps recover long insertions and deletions. (PDF p. 3).
- Use Z-drop to stop alignment across poorly matching regions while allowing a single long gap. Reverse-complement local alignment can then detect short inversions. (PDF p. 4).
- For spliced alignment, use asymmetric gap costs, constant extension cost of zero for introns, and penalties for noncanonical splice signals. For unstranded reads, test both splice orientations. (PDF p. 4).

## Applications

- Minimap2 supports long-read overlap detection and searches against large multispecies databases. (PDF p. 4).
- For paired-end reads, it first chains the pair as one fragment. It then separates the chains, aligns each read, and pairs consistent hits. (PDF p. 4).
- Assembly alignments can support substitution calling with the supplied paftools.js script. (PDF p. 6).

## Optimization

- Stop predecessor searches after repeated failure to improve a chaining score. The author reports that this usually preserves the optimal chain. (PDF p. 2).
- Homopolymer-compressed seeds improve SMRT overlap sensitivity. They use one-third fewer minimizers in the reported experiment. (PDF p. 3).
- Use difference-based vectorized alignment with antidiagonal computation and a default 500 bp band. (PDF p. 3).
- Remove anchors that imply both an insertion and a deletion greater than 10 bp. Also remove terminal anchors that imply long gaps near chain ends. (PDF p. 4).

## Evaluation and testing

- The long-read simulation used 33,088 reads of at least 1,000 bp from pbsim. The short-read simulation used 10 million 150 bp read pairs from mason2. Both were mapped to GRCh38. (PDF p. 5).
- A simulated read was correct if its longest alignment overlapped at least 10% of the true interval. Accuracy curves varied the mapping-quality threshold. (PDF p. 5).
- Splice tests used SIRV controls with known truth and real mouse reads compared with EnsEMBL release 89. Approximate introns required both ends to be within 10 bp of annotated ends. (PDF p. 5).
- The real short-read test used mixed CHM1 and CHM13 reads, GATK HaplotypeCaller v3.5, and an independent variant truth set derived from assemblies. (PDF p. 6).

## Results

- On simulated long reads, minimap2 took 200 CPU seconds and 6.8 GB peak memory. It was over 30 times faster than the compared tools except minialign and Kart, which had similar runtimes. (PDF p. 5).
- On SIRV controls, 93.8% of predicted splice junctions were exact. This rose to 98.4% with a 10 bp tolerance. (PDF p. 5).
- For short reads, minimap2 was 3–4 times faster than Bowtie2 and BWA-MEM. Simulation accuracy exceeded Bowtie2 and SNAP but was below BWA-MEM. (PDF p. 6).
- A human SMRT assembly aligned to GRCh38 in seven minutes on eight CPU cores. This was over 20 times faster than nucmer from MUMmer4. (PDF p. 6).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The seed-based divergence estimate assumes independent k-mers and homogeneous substitutions. It can be biased and ignores long reference deletions. (PDF p. 2).
- Homopolymer compression reduced sensitivity for the ONT reads available in the study. (PDF p. 3).
- Anchor filters cannot correct all local alignment errors. Spliced alignment is several times slower than genomic alignment because it uses denser seeds, two alignment rounds, and no banding. (PDF p. 4).
- The author could not reliably estimate mapping errors for real human SMRT reads because ground truth was unavailable. (PDF p. 5).
