## Executive summary

- BetaAlign trains transformers to convert unaligned sequences into multiple sequence alignments using simulated training examples. (PDF p. 2).
- The updated algorithm generates alternative alignments and returns the alignment with the highest certainty. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors report the highest DNA alignment accuracy among the tested programs. For proteins, BetaAlign was usually second most accurate. (PDF p. 13).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to learn alignment rules that capture different evolutionary dynamics. (PDF p. 2).
- They assess training size, training duration, transfer learning, transformer architecture, and training within restricted indel-parameter ranges. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that default scoring schemes do not adequately adapt to evolutionary differences between datasets or regions. (PDF p. 1).
- They argue that simulation-based training shifts development effort toward modeling evolutionary dynamics. (PDF p. 13).

## Analysis

- Training experiments compare 50,000, 100,000, and 200,000 protein alignments. They track loss, valid-output coverage, and column score. (PDF p. 10).
- Transfer-learning tests compare initial training, repeated training on the same dataset, and training on more complex datasets before returning to the original dataset. (PDF p. 10).
- Subspace learning specializes a general transformer through further training on restricted evolutionary parameter ranges. (PDF p. 4).

## Methods

- Input sequences are concatenated with separator tokens. The transformer output represents alignment columns and is converted into an MSA. (PDF p. 3).
- SpartaABC generates sequence sets and reference alignments. Simulations can use different insertion and deletion rates and length distributions. (PDF p. 3).
- The encoder produces learned representations. The decoder predicts output tokens sequentially. The models use the Fairseq library. (PDF p. 4).
- If one transformer produces an invalid alignment, the method uses an alternative transformer. Dedicated transformers align segments that are then concatenated to handle long sequences. (PDF p. 4).

## Applications

- BetaAlign aligns DNA and protein sequences. (PDF p. 13).
- Encoder embeddings support ancestral sequence length prediction without computing an MSA. (PDF p. 13).
- The authors propose secondary structure prediction, protein stability prediction, and ancestral reconstruction as further uses of the embeddings. (PDF p. 13).

## Optimization

- A transformer for a given sequence count can start from weights learned for a smaller sequence count. (PDF p. 4).
- The updated inference procedure selects the alternative alignment with the highest certainty to improve accuracy. (PDF p. 2).
- Embedding extraction skips the decoder to reduce runtime. Token averaging compresses embeddings to 1024 × (2n − 1) entries for n sequences. (PDF p. 10).

## Evaluation and testing

- Training and test examples come from simulations with known reference alignments. (PDF p. 3).
- Comparisons use default settings for MUSCLE 3.8.1551, MAFFT 7.475, PRANK 150803, ClustalW 2.1, and DIALIGN dialign2-2. (PDF p. 10).
- Column score measures agreement with reference alignment columns. Coverage measures the fraction of valid output alignments. (PDF p. 10).
- The reported DNA accuracy comparisons use paired t-tests. (PDF p. 13).

## Results

- Training and validation loss decreased as training continued. Valid-output coverage rose from approximately 40% after 20 epochs to 80% after 60 epochs. (PDF p. 10).
- For DNA, BetaAlign achieved a minimum reported error reduction of 12.7% against the compared programs. For 10 sequences, its error reduction relative to PRANK was 13.7%. (PDF p. 13).
- For 10 protein sequences, PRANK reduced error by 5.1% relative to BetaAlign. (PDF p. 13).
- A linear regressor predicted ancestral root length from ND10 embeddings with R² = 0.91. (PDF p. 10).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Transformers can generate invalid alignments. The method therefore needs alternative models as fallbacks. (PDF p. 4).
- The models used in the study support up to 1024 tokens. Attention memory requirements grow quadratically with total input and output length. (PDF p. 13).
- The authors caution that simulation benchmarks can differ from empirical benchmarks because simulations can omit evolutionary events. (PDF p. 2).
- Transfer learning in this study was restricted to models with the same representation and token dictionaries. (PDF p. 10).
