## Executive summary

- Hamming studies binary systematic codes that use extra digits to detect or correct transmission errors. (PDF p. 2).
- The paper presents minimum redundancy constructions for single error detection, single error correction, and single error correction with double error detection. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- The author seeks error correction that permits computers to continue after isolated failures, especially during unattended operation. (PDF p. 2).
- The paper seeks code constructions with minimum redundancy and proofs of optimality under its assumptions. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- The author argues that error detection alone can interrupt computation even when it prevents incorrect results. (PDF p. 2).
- A single computer failure can invalidate later operations because computation repeatedly uses the same equipment along one dependent path. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- For a systematic code, n digits contain m information digits and n − m checking digits. The redundancy measure is R = n/m. (PDF p. 2).
- For minimum distance five, disjoint radius-two spheres give an upper bound of 2^n/[1 + C(n,1) + C(n,2)] code points. (PDF p. 13).
- The author shows a six-digit code with minimum distance three. It corrects one error, although its two independent parity checks cannot locate errors in all six positions. (PDF p. 14).

## Methods

- The single error detection construction places information in the first n − 1 positions. It sets the final digit so that the total number of ones is even. (PDF p. 3).
- The decoder detects a single changed digit through odd parity. The excerpt does not specify an error correction decoder. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The author proposes use during long unattended operation, in tightly connected systems, and for signaling under noise that cannot be reduced economically. (PDF p. 3).

## Optimization

- The optimization target is minimum redundancy. For the even-parity detection construction, R = n/(n − 1). (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- For n = 7 and minimum distance five, the sphere bound permits four code points. The author reports that trial and error gives a maximum of two. (PDF p. 13).

## Results

- The even-parity construction detects every single digit error because each such error changes even parity to odd parity. (PDF p. 3).
- The six-digit example demonstrates the existence of a non-systematic single error correcting code. (PDF p. 14).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The paper does not discuss circuit implementation. Extra encoding and correction equipment also adds cost. (PDF p. 3).
- Redundancy reduces effective channel capacity. The paper discusses transmission efficiency in detail, but gives little economic analysis. (PDF p. 2).
- The author reports no general construction method for optimal codes with minimum distance above four. Whether systematic codes attain the stated bounds remains unresolved. (PDF p. 13).
