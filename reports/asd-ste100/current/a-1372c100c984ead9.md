## Executive summary

- Kabsch derives a direct transformation that minimizes weighted squared deviations between paired vectors under an orthogonality constraint. (PDF p. 1).
- The solution uses eigenvectors and eigenvalues. The author also extends the method to a specified positive definite metric constraint. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- Find an orthogonal matrix U that minimizes E = ½ Σ w_n ||Ux_n − y_n||² for two given vector sets. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The author states that a direct solution is possible despite the nonlinear constraint. He contrasts this approach with an iterative rotation method and factorization of an unconstrained transformation. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- Symmetric Lagrange multipliers give the stationarity equation U(S + L) = R. The minimum condition requires S + L to be positive definite. (PDF p. 1).
- Orthogonality eliminates U and gives (S + L)² = RᵀR. The positive square roots of the eigenvalues determine S + L. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- Form R = Σ w_n y_n x_nᵀ and S = Σ w_n x_n x_nᵀ from the paired vectors. (PDF p. 1).
- Compute normalized eigenvectors a_k of RᵀR and their eigenvalues μ_k. For positive μ_k, set b_k = Ra_k/√μ_k and construct U = Σ b_k a_kᵀ. (PDF p. 2).
- For planar vectors with one zero eigenvalue, complete the bases with a_3 = a_1 × a_2 and b_3 = b_1 × b_2. (PDF p. 2).
- For the metric constraint UᵀU = M, choose a solution B and write U = VB. Transform the input vectors with B, then solve for orthogonal V. (PDF p. 2).

## Applications

- The author motivates the method by fitting atomic arrangements to approximately measured coordinates in crystallography. (PDF p. 1).
- If translation is allowed, shift the vector-set centroids to the origin to remove translation from the problem. (PDF p. 1).

## Optimization

- The derivation replaces an iterative rotation search with a direct eigenvalue construction for the constrained objective. (PDF p. 2).
