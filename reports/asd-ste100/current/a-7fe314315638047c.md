## Executive summary

- The authors introduce ProteinMPNN, a deep learning model for de novo protein sequence design on target backbones. (PDF p. 1).
- ProteinMPNN achieves high sequence recovery and designs sequences that fold reliably into target structures. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- Generate amino acid sequences that robustly encode specific protein backbone geometries. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- Locally connected graph neural networks effectively capture backbone spatial neighborhoods for sequence design. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- Adding coordinate noise during training improves sequence recovery and robustness on predicted structure models. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- An autoregressive message-passing graph neural network maps backbone geometry to amino acid probabilities. (PDF p. 3).
- Sequences are decoded conditioned on target backbone torsion angles and pairwise distance matrices. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- De novo protein design, protein nanoparticle assembly, and therapeutic peptide binder optimization. (PDF p. 7).

## Optimization

- Trained neural network inference replaces computationally intensive physical force field minimization. (PDF p. 7).

## Evaluation and testing

- Designs are validated computationally with AlphaFold and experimentally using X-ray crystallography and cryo-EM. (PDF p. 2).

## Results

- ProteinMPNN achieves 52.4% native sequence recovery and consistently yields soluble, correctly folded designs. (PDF p. 2).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Design relies strictly on fixed input backbone coordinates and cannot model large conformational shifts. (PDF p. 8).
