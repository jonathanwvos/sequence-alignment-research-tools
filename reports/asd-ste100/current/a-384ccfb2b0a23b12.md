## Executive summary

- ProstT5 adapts ProtT5 to translate between amino acid sequences and Foldseek 3Di structure tokens. (PDF p. 1).
- Predicted 3Di sequences enable remote homology searches without first predicting full protein structures. (PDF p. 6).
- The model also generates amino acid sequences from 3Di input and provides embeddings for downstream prediction tasks. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to model protein sequence and structure in one language model. (PDF p. 1).
- They assess whether structure information improves embeddings and enables fast searches for distantly related proteins. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that 3Di tokens make protein structures suitable for language modeling and optimized sequence comparison algorithms. (PDF p. 1).
- They argue that inverse folding should be assessed through structural similarity because different sequences can adopt similar structures. (PDF p. 5).

## Analysis

- The dataset construction combines sequence clustering, structure clustering, and quality filtering. The final set contains 17 million proteins and supplies 34 million directional translation examples. (PDF p. 2).
- Clusters remain within one data split. The validation and test sets each contain 474 representatives after redundancy reduction. (PDF p. 2).
- CATH analysis compares embeddings from amino acids, experimental 3Di, predicted 3Di, and concatenated amino acid and predicted-3Di representations. (PDF p. 6).
- Roundtrip agreement between original and regenerated 3Di sequences correlates with structural similarity at Spearman R = 0.64. (PDF p. 11).

## Methods

- The vocabulary adds 20 lowercase 3Di tokens and two direction tokens. Continued span-denoising training uses both amino acid and 3Di sequences. (PDF p. 2).
- Subsequent training teaches both translation directions. Sequence length increases from 256 to 512 residues during this stage. (PDF p. 3).
- An alternative inference mode uses a two-layer CNN on encoder embeddings to predict 3Di states directly. (PDF p. 6).
- Global Needleman–Wunsch alignment with the Foldseek 3Di substitution matrix measures 3Di sequence similarity during sampling-parameter selection. (PDF p. 5).

## Applications

- Predicted 3Di strings support Foldseek remote homology searches. This capability is available through the Foldseek webserver. (PDF p. 2).
- The model supports inverse folding by generating amino acid sequences from 3Di structure descriptions. (PDF p. 5).
- Embeddings support CATH classification and secondary structure prediction. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors propose future uses in structural phylogenetics, embedding-based alignment, and MSA generation. (PDF p. 11).

## Optimization

- Training uses DeepSpeed stage 2, gradient accumulation, bf16 precision, and torchInductor to increase throughput. (PDF p. 3).
- The encoder-CNN mode avoids sequential decoder generation when predicting 3Di states. (PDF p. 6).
- Sampling settings are selected on validation data. Final test data are used only to report the selected configuration's performance. (PDF p. 5).
- Inverse-folding filtering allows up to 10 attempts to reach 70% roundtrip 3Di identity and retains the candidate with the highest roundtrip accuracy. (PDF p. 9).

## Evaluation and testing

- The SCOPe40 benchmark measures retrieval sensitivity up to the first false positive at family, superfamily, and fold levels. Comparators include experimental-structure Foldseek and MMseqs2. (PDF p. 6).
- The runtime benchmark uses the M. jannaschii proteome subset with sequences shorter than 1000 amino acids. CPU and GPU timings are averaged across five runs. (PDF p. 5).
- Secondary structure tests use biotrainer and FLIP with NetSurfP-2.0 training data. Test sets are CASP12, CASP14, and NEW364. (PDF p. 7).
- Inverse-folding tests compare ESMFold structures of generated sequences with native AlphaFold2 predictions. Metrics include lDDT, RMSD, and TM-score, with bootstrap confidence intervals. (PDF p. 9).

## Results

- Superfamily ROC-AUC was 0.45 for decoder-generated 3Di and 0.47 for encoder-CNN predictions. Experimental-structure Foldseek scored 0.49, and MMseqs2 scored 0.06. (PDF p. 6).
- Proteome 3Di prediction took 40 minutes 6 seconds on the tested CPU and 43.5 seconds on the tested GPU. The cited ColabFold structure workflow took 48 hours. (PDF p. 6).
- Mean CATH accuracy was 73% for ProstT5 amino acid embeddings and 75% for concatenated amino acid and predicted-3Di embeddings. ProtT5 scored 68%, but broad confidence intervals limit comparisons. (PDF p. 7).
- Inverse-folding lDDT was 0.72 for ProstT5, 0.73 after roundtrip filtering, and 0.77 for ProteinMPNN. (PDF p. 9).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Quality filtering removes many disordered proteins and favors short, structured proteins. Strong 3Di token imbalance remains after filtering. (PDF p. 11).
- The authors identify potential circularity and information leakage when structure-derived training data are evaluated on structure-related tasks. (PDF p. 11).
- ProstT5 reduces performance on some function-related tasks, especially subcellular location prediction. The authors therefore do not present it as a general-purpose protein language model. (PDF p. 7).
- Sequential encoder-decoder translation is slower than embedding extraction. Training stopped before clear convergence because further gains did not justify the computing cost. (PDF p. 6).
