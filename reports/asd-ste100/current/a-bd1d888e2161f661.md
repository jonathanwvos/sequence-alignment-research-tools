## Executive summary

- The authors transform constrained LCS problems into instances of the maximum clique problem. (PDF p. 2).
- Exact and heuristic maximum clique solvers are applied to solve repetition-free and arc-preserving LCS variants. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- Develop a unified optimization framework for difficult constrained LCS problem variants. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Representing mutual compatibility as graph edges allows leveraging highly optimized clique solvers. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- Graph reduction effectiveness and solver runtimes are evaluated on synthetic and biological benchmarks. (PDF p. 6).

## Methods

- Problem instances are transformed into conflict graphs where cliques represent mutually compatible subsequence matches. (PDF p. 3).
- Graph reduction rules prune redundant vertices and edges before solver execution. (PDF p. 14).

## Applications

- RNA secondary structure comparison and repetition-free sequence alignment. (PDF p. 2).

## Optimization

- Graph reduction rules eliminate provably dominated match nodes to decrease clique graph density. (PDF p. 14).

## Evaluation and testing

- The method is evaluated using CPLEX and the heuristic solver LSCC-BMS on RFLCS and LAPCS benchmarks. (PDF p. 8).

## Results

- The clique transformation solves large benchmark instances to optimality faster than dedicated problem-specific heuristics. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Dense conflict graphs on very long strings exceed memory capacity and solver limits. (PDF p. 8).
