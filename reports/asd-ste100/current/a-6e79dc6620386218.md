## Executive summary

- The authors compare simulated true alignments with ALIGN pairwise alignments and ClustalW multiple sequence alignments. (PDF p. 2).
- Errors increase with sequence divergence. Local errors from misplaced indels develop into longer segments with many incorrectly placed indels. (PDF p. 3).
- Reconstructed error segments contain fewer gaps and are shorter than the corresponding true segments. (PDF p. 5).

## Objectives

- Identify the sources and characteristics of multiple sequence alignment errors. Measure reconstruction error against simulated true alignments. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that an optimal alignment score does not guarantee historical accuracy. True segments can have lower scores than incorrect segments or share the same optimal score. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors argue that guide-tree errors are not a major source of reconstruction error. Providing the true phylogeny improves alignment accuracy only slightly. (PDF p. 5).

## Analysis

- The analysis divides alignments into alternating correct and erroneous segments. It compares scores, lengths, indel counts, and gap content in corresponding segments. (PDF p. 2).
- Simple pairwise errors involve at most two indels in each corresponding segment. The defined types are shift, split, merge, ex-nihilo, and purge. Other errors are complex. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors explain the sharp increase in complex MSA errors through successive profile alignments. Simple errors from separate steps can combine into complex errors. (PDF p. 5).

## Methods

- ROSE simulated DNA sequences with an average length of 500 on a balanced 16-OTU binary tree. All branches had equal substitution and indel rates. (PDF p. 2).
- The experiment used eight substitution levels and eight indel levels. Each combination had 100 replicates, for 6400 datasets. Pairwise tests used two of the most distant sequences in each dataset. (PDF p. 2).
- ALIGN used default scores of 5 for a match, −4 for a mismatch, −16 for gap opening, and −4 for gap extension. ClustalW used default parameters for MSA reconstruction. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The authors call for methods that identify and manage MSA errors before subsequent analyses. They name HoT and COS as examples. (PDF p. 6).

## Optimization

- Replacing default pairwise penalties with scores based on the true evolutionary parameters improved reconstruction rates by a reported 3–10%. The authors conclude that default penalties are not the main error source. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors link the bias toward shorter alignments to score optimization. With the same number of matches, shorter alignments usually receive higher scores. (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- Column reconstruction uses the CS measure. Residue-pair reconstruction uses the SPS measure. Each error rate is the complement of its reconstruction rate. (PDF p. 2).
- The benchmark spans average pairwise distances of 0.02–0.30 substitutions per site and gap contents of 0.001–0.022 gaps per site. (PDF p. 2).
- A controlled guide-tree test compares standard ClustalW alignments with alignments guided by the true simulation phylogeny. (PDF p. 5).

## Results

- Sub-optimal true segments account for at least half of pairwise errors, even for closely related sequences. (PDF p. 3).
- MSA residue-pair error rates range from approximately 5 ± 2% to 90 ± 7%. Except for very closely related sequences, column error rates exceed 50% and rapidly approach 100%. (PDF p. 3).
- The relative contribution of guide-tree errors to total MSA error peaks at about 10%. (PDF p. 5).
- In one closely related subset, 68% of error segments contain one shifted indel. In an intermediate-divergence subset, only 2.5% contain one misplaced indel, while 90% contain more than three. (PDF p. 5).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Only ClustalW was tested for MSA reconstruction. Simulations included substitutions and indels but excluded variation in rates among sites. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors describe the simulations as a best-case evolutionary setting. Their expectation of higher errors in real sequences is an extrapolation from this setting. (PDF p. 6).
- True evolutionary parameters cannot be supplied in normal alignment tasks because the true alignment is unknown. (PDF p. 2).
