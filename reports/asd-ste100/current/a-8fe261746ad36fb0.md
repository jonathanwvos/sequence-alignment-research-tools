## Executive summary

- The authors compare LCS algorithms through their computation methods and stated time and space costs. (PDF p. 1).
- The comparison covers dynamic programming, divide and conquer, match-point processing, and diagonal edit-graph traversal. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to examine LCS algorithms for approximate string matching. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors motivate dynamic programming through optimal substructure and overlapping subproblems. Stored subproblem results support later computations. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors argue that Wagner–Fischer does not benefit from highly similar inputs because its computation time does not adapt to similarity. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- The paper states O(mn) time and O(m+n) space for Hirschberg's LCS reconstruction method. (PDF p. 2).
- The paper states O((r+n) log n) time and O(r+n) space for Hunt–Szymanski, where r counts match points. It identifies sparse matches as a favorable case. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors argue that Myers' differential algorithm is less suitable when one string is much shorter than the other. They favor its use for similar strings of comparable length. (PDF p. 4).

## Methods

- Hirschberg combines forward and reverse dynamic-programming rows to locate a midpoint on an optimal path. Divide and conquer then supports sequence reconstruction with linear space. (PDF p. 2).
- Hunt–Szymanski uses MATCHLIST to process matching positions and THRESHOLD to track contour crossings. Binary search locates threshold updates. (PDF p. 2).
- The described Myers–Miller method traverses an edit graph by diagonals. Matching diagonal edges have zero cost, while insertion and deletion edges have unit cost. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The authors connect approximate string matching with search engines, database search, spell checking, signal processing, and file comparison. (PDF p. 1).

## Optimization

- Kuo–Cross processes match lists in increasing order. The authors state that this avoids repeated THRESHOLD updates. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors propose reducing finite-automata lookup-table and queue overhead as future work. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors propose changes to computation order to reduce page faults. They give higher priority to algorithms with lower time costs for typical inputs. (PDF p. 5).

## Evaluation and testing

- A worked dynamic-programming example uses GCCCTAGCG and GCGCAATG. The reported LCS length is 5. (PDF p. 1).
- The Hunt–Szymanski and Kuo–Cross illustrations use cbacbaaba and abcdbb to show match lists and threshold behavior. (PDF p. 3).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Interpretation: the stated step from ed >= n-m to ed >= n is not justified by m being much smaller than n. The exact inequality does not follow from that approximation. (PDF p. 4).
- Interpretation: the claim that almost all discussed algorithms take linear time conflicts with the earlier stated quadratic and match-dependent bounds. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors state that string alignment algorithms are outside the work's coverage. (PDF p. 5).
