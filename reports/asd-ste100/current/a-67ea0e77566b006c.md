## Executive summary

- SubseqHash2 produces multiple subsequence seeds in one dynamic programming computation. It targets sequences with high error rates. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors report 10–50-fold acceleration over SubseqHash while retaining nearly the same seed accuracy. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- The study aims to reduce the cost of repeated subsequence seeding while retaining high sensitivity and precision. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- Long substring seeds lose sensitivity on error-prone sequences. Short substring seeds create more chance matches, which reduces precision. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors argue that similar strings retain long shared subsequences despite edits. Repeated subsequence seeding can increase sensitivity with little precision loss. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- A pivot divides each seed into two subsequences. Shared forward, reverse, and pivot score tables define k ABCk orders. (PDF p. 3).
- The k orders share functions and are not mathematically independent. The authors report that their experimental behavior resembles independent SubseqHash repetitions. (PDF p. 3).
- For read length N, window length n, seed length k, and parameter d, the dynamic program takes O(Nnkd) time. SIMD processes independent states along the d dimension. (PDF p. 4).

## Methods

- The dynamic program first solves subproblems by recurrence. It then finds the minimum-ranked subsequence for each order and window, reusing subproblems across windows. (PDF p. 4).
- SubseqHash2r uses symmetric score tables to give a sequence and its reverse complement the same seed sets. (PDF p. 4).
- SubseqHash2w joins a leading substring to each selected subsequence from the following window. (PDF p. 4).

## Applications

- The tested applications are read mapping, pairwise sequence alignment, and read overlap detection. (PDF p. 4).
- The read-mapping experiment uses minimap2 as an initial filter. Subsequence seeds then target difficult fragments that remain unmapped. (PDF p. 6).

## Optimization

- One computation produces up to k seed sets. This removes the repeated dynamic programming cost for t ≤ k repetitions. (PDF p. 8).
- SIMD reduces the stated runtime to O(Nnk) when d states fit the supported parallel computation. The reported supported range extends to d = 32. (PDF p. 3).
- The implementation can generate only the requested t seeds during optimization. Larger k reduces sensitivity and false positives for fixed n. (PDF p. 4).

## Evaluation and testing

- Collision tests used 100,000 random DNA string pairs for each edit distance from one to 10. They compared minimizers, SubseqHash, and SubseqHash2 with and without repetition. (PDF p. 5).
- Read-mapping tests used PBSIM reads from Drosophila chromosome X with an Oxford Nanopore R10 error profile. About 5000 minimap2-unmapped fragments longer than 1000 bases were retained. A true match required over half its aligned characters to agree with simulation truth; sensitivity measured coverage of 200-base segments. (PDF p. 6).
- Pairwise tests used 10 simulated sequence pairs at each error rate of 5%, 10%, and 15%. Initial sequences had one million bases. Metrics measured character coverage by true and false seed matches. (PDF p. 6).
- Overlap tests used 10,000 E. coli reads with unique minimap2 mappings at quality 60. Reference overlap of at least 15 bases defined truth. Additional filtering and verification were omitted to isolate seed performance. (PDF p. 8).

## Results

- SubseqHash2 and SubseqHash had nearly identical collision probabilities at equal repetition counts. SubseqHash2 was up to 50 times faster on the Oxford Nanopore runtime dataset. (PDF p. 5).
- With n = 30, k = 25, d = 31, and 10 repeats, SubseqHash2 took 577.45 seconds. SubseqHash took 15,364 seconds; the tested repeated Strobemer configuration took 145.19 seconds. (PDF p. 6).
- Repeated SubseqHash2 gave a better sensitivity–precision tradeoff on difficult read fragments than the tested substring-based methods. (PDF p. 6).
- SubseqHash and SubseqHash2 detected more true overlaps at comparable precision than substring-based seeds, particularly at high precision. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- SubseqHash2 remains slower than simple substring-based seeding methods. (PDF p. 9).
- Dynamic programming increases memory use. SubseqHash2 stayed below 500 MB in the reported seeding test, while the other methods stayed below 10 MB. (PDF p. 6).
- The read-mapping experiment measures seed quality on selected difficult fragments. It does not establish complete mapper performance. (PDF p. 6).
- Overlap truth depends on selected high-quality minimap2 mappings. The test therefore evaluates reads that already map confidently to the reference. (PDF p. 8).
