## Executive summary

- The authors train HMMs on unaligned sequences to model globins, kinase catalytic domains, and EF-hand motifs. (PDF p. 1).
- The trained models produce multiple alignments and search SWISS-PROT 22 for family members or domains. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors report alignments close to structural alignments and strong discrimination between members and non-members. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- Learn conserved sequence features from protein families and use them for alignment, database search, and model interpretation. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that an HMM can represent conserved positions and position-specific insertion behavior in a protein family. (PDF p. 3).
- They argue that unaligned sequences contain enough statistical information to recover alignments that resemble structural alignments in the three studied families. (PDF p. 28).

## Analysis

- EF-hand experiments compared training and held-out negative log-likelihood scores. Small training sets caused overfitting, which largely disappeared at about 100 sequences. (PDF p. 28).
- The authors use deletion probabilities and amino acid distribution entropy to distinguish conserved and variable regions in the globin model. (PDF p. 21).
- Regularization also counters model bias. Stronger delete-to-match regularization discouraged globin models from representing proteins mainly through insertions and deletions. (PDF p. 17).

## Methods

- The HMM has M match states, M silent delete states, and M + 1 insert states. Match and insert states emit amino acids. (PDF p. 5).
- EM estimates model parameters. Initial probabilities can encode prior knowledge or use the same distributions at equivalent positions. (PDF p. 14).
- Match-state Dirichlet priors were fitted to 5,670 column count vectors from 41 existing alignments. Transition priors used two previously built HMMs. (PDF p. 16).
- A mixture of ten HMMs grouped globin sequences by the component with the lowest negative log-likelihood score. (PDF p. 20).

## Applications

- The method supports multiple sequence alignment and searches for whole protein families or domains within longer proteins. (PDF p. 1).
- The EF-hand search suggested a motif in a conserved intracellular region of L-type calcium channels. The authors present this as a proposed biological interpretation. (PDF p. 30).
- The authors propose connecting domain HMMs into a larger model for automatic protein annotation with Viterbi decoding. (PDF p. 30).

## Optimization

- Noise is added before parameter reestimation and reduced linearly to zero over ten iterations. Repeated runs provide alternative models for selection. (PDF p. 14).
- Model surgery removes positions used as deletions by more than half the sequence paths. It adds positions where more than half the paths contain insertions. (PDF p. 14).
- Insert-state amino acid probabilities are fixed at 1/20. The authors report that this reduces free parameters and tends to improve database searches. (PDF p. 17).

## Evaluation and testing

- Discrimination tests compared HMMs with PROFILESEARCH and PROSITE on globins, kinases, and EF-hands. (PDF p. 1).
- The kinase experiment trained ten HMMs on 193 unaligned sequences. Initial model lengths ranged from 242 to 282 positions. (PDF p. 21).
- The first EF-hand experiment trained five HMMs on 885 motifs from 242 proteins. Database classification used a visually selected Z-score cutoff of 4.75. (PDF p. 25).
- The training-size study estimated 70 EF-hand models and evaluated motifs excluded from each training set. One model per training size also searched SWISS-PROT 22. (PDF p. 28).

## Results

- The authors report that HMMs and PROFILESEARCH outperform PROSITE. They describe the HMM advantage over PROFILESEARCH in false positives and false negatives as slight. (PDF p. 2).
- The globin mixture produced seven nonempty clusters. The authors report correct identification of the main alpha, beta, and myoglobin subfamilies. (PDF p. 20).
- The best kinase model had 254 positions and a mean negative log-likelihood score of 588.39. (PDF p. 21).
- The best initial EF-hand model had 29 positions and a mean negative log-likelihood score of 61.41. At the selected cutoff, it classified 232 database sequences as containing the motif. (PDF p. 25).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- EM can converge to a local optimum. The noise schedule is heuristic and does not guarantee the best model. (PDF p. 14).
- Transition priors came from only two HMMs built with earlier ad hoc regularization. The authors call these values preliminary. (PDF p. 17).
- Training requires enough representative family sequences. Small EF-hand training sets produced overfitting and many false positives. (PDF p. 28).
- The HMM does not readily represent correlations between distant sequence positions that interact in three-dimensional space. (PDF p. 30).
