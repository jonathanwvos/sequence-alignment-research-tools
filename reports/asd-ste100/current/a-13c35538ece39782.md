## Executive summary

- The authors introduce Backward Nondeterministic Dawg Matching (BNDM). It combines suffix automata with bit-parallel simulation. (PDF p. 3).
- BNDM combines the text-skipping ability of BDM with the flexible pattern support of Shift-Or. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to combine fast string matching with support for flexible patterns. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors identify a limitation of earlier bit-parallel methods: they support flexible patterns but cannot skip text characters. (PDF p. 1).
- Simple and regular nondeterministic automata permit search states to be stored as bits. Processor bit operations can then update several states together. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- The authors introduce a suffix automaton for patterns with character classes. They identify its size, minimality, and construction algorithms as subjects for further study. (PDF p. 35).

## Methods

- BNDM simulates a nondeterministic suffix automaton with bit-parallel operations. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors describe extensions for character classes, multiple patterns, and approximate matching. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The authors identify DNA searching as an application where speed and flexible patterns are useful. (PDF p. 35).
- The authors report later extensions for regular expressions and PROSITE protein patterns with character classes and bounded gaps. (PDF p. 35).

## Optimization

- Bit-parallel simulation uses the parallel operations within a computer word. Text skipping reduces the number of text characters that require inspection. (PDF p. 1).

## Results

- The authors report that BNDM is faster than BDM and Shift-Or. Its relative speed against fast Boyer-Moore variants depends on alphabet size. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors report strong performance for character-class searches and competitive performance for searches that permit errors. (PDF p. 1).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors leave a detailed structural analysis of the character-class suffix automaton for future work. (PDF p. 35).
