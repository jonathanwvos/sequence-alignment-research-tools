## Executive summary

- Gotoh modifies the Waterman algorithm for gap costs w(k) = uk + v, with nonnegative u and v. The method requires essentially MN steps. (PDF p. 1).
- The method replaces searches over gap lengths with recursive updates. Temporary scores need two one-dimensional arrays and one variable. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- The author aims to retain gaps of different lengths while reducing computation and supporting computers with limited memory. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- A long gap can result from one mutation. The author uses this argument to support gap costs that are not proportional to gap length. (PDF p. 1).
- The author states that the restricted gap-cost form is suitable for most practical uses. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- Each matrix cell requires a minimum over three candidates for D and a minimum over two candidates for each gap state. This gives MN constant-cost updates. (PDF p. 2).
- For the piecewise-linear extension, the stated computation cost is roughly proportional to (L + 2)MN when N is much larger than the last breakpoint. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- D(m,n) is the minimum of the diagonal score plus the residue-pair cost and the two gap-state scores P(m,n) and Q(m,n). (PDF p. 1).
- P(m,n) = min(D(m−1,n) + w(1), P(m−1,n) + u). Q uses the corresponding recurrence along the other sequence. (PDF p. 2).
- The author describes traceback with a three-bit direction matrix. Two runs exchange the sequence axes, then combine direction bits with logical OR. (PDF p. 2).
- The extension uses piecewise-linear gap costs with decreasing nonnegative slopes. Separate gap states represent the segments. (PDF p. 2).

## Applications

- The method compares protein or nucleic acid sequences. (PDF p. 1).
- The author proposes a gap cost that becomes constant after a threshold for RNA alignments with large gaps. (PDF p. 3).

## Optimization

- Recursive gap-state updates remove the repeated search over all possible gap lengths. (PDF p. 2).
- Temporary score storage uses two arrays and one variable. Traceback additionally uses direction information. (PDF p. 2).
- The author suggests estimating scoring weights and parameters with a dynamic optimization procedure. (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- Figure 1 gives a worked matrix and traceback example. It uses match cost 0, mismatch cost 10, and gap cost w(k) = 10k + 12. (PDF p. 3).

## Results

- The derived recurrence reduces the stated computational order from M²N to MN for the specified gap-cost form. (PDF p. 1).
- In the worked example, the second run changes some direction values from 1 to 5. These changes do not affect the illustrated traceback. (PDF p. 2).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The basic MN-step result requires the specified linear gap-cost form with an added constant. (PDF p. 1).
- The generalized procedure requires queue storage of M × (K_L + 1) cells, plus arrays and variables for the gap states. (PDF p. 3).
- The author states that suitable scoring weights and parameters cannot be determined in advance. (PDF p. 3).
