## Executive summary

- The Learned Alignment Module constructs differentiable multiple sequence alignments with smooth Smith–Waterman. A downstream learning objective can update the alignment. (PDF p. 2).
- SMURF jointly learns alignments and Markov Random Field parameters for contact prediction. The authors report mild improvements. (PDF p. 1).
- Optimizing AlphaFold confidence improved three of four tested structure predictions, but produced inconsistent alignments. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- The study aims to learn alignments jointly with downstream models instead of fixing alignments during preprocessing. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that conventional local substitution and gap scores omit contextual interactions and long-range dependencies. (PDF p. 1).
- A distribution over alignments permits differentiation. The authors also suggest that it helps the model consider several candidate alignments early in training. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- Smooth Smith–Waterman replaces max with logsumexp and argmax with softmax. Higher-scoring paths receive greater probability, and residue-pair alignment probabilities can be expressed as derivatives. (PDF p. 3).
- Learned similarity scores depend on residue context and vary across protein families. They are not fixed functions of residue identities. (PDF p. 3).
- The AlphaFold results show that better downstream predictions need not imply better alignments. The authors interpret the inconsistent learned MSAs as potentially adversarial. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- LAM applies convolutions to one-hot sequences to form 512-dimensional residue vectors. Dot products between reference and sequence vectors define alignment scores. (PDF p. 3).
- Smooth Smith–Waterman aligns each sequence to the same reference. These pairwise outputs form the MSA supplied to the downstream model. (PDF p. 3).
- SMURF first uses BasicAlign to minimize squared differences from an averaged MSA. TrainMRF then uses masked language modeling to update both MRF parameters and alignment convolutions. (PDF p. 3).
- The implementation supports temperature control, affine gap penalties, local and global alignment, and a restrict-turns option to correct diagonal bias. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- SMURF uses unaligned protein or RNA families for unsupervised contact prediction. (PDF p. 2).
- LAM can replace the AlphaFold input alignment and receive gradients that maximize predicted structure confidence. (PDF p. 2).

## Optimization

- The implementation uses JAX compilation and automatic differentiation. Vectorization across sequence length and batch dimensions reduces runtime. (PDF p. 3).
- The AlphaFold experiment tested learning rates of 0.01, 0.001, and 0.0001, with and without cooling. The selected learned MSA maximized pLDDT confidence. (PDF p. 5).

## Evaluation and testing

- Runtime tests compared forward passes and combined forward–backward passes against a smooth Needleman–Wunsch implementation. They also compared vectorized and naive versions of the authors' code. (PDF p. 3).
- The AlphaFold tests compared learned and MMSeqs2 MSAs using pLDDT, structure RMSD, and pairwise alignment scores. Tests removed individual sequences and groups of distant sequences. (PDF p. 5).
- Contact prediction was tested across diverse protein and RNA families. (PDF p. 1).

## Results

- The authors report faster forward and combined forward–backward computation than the smooth Needleman–Wunsch comparator. Vectorization substantially reduced runtime. (PDF p. 3).
- SMURF produced mild contact-prediction improvements with alignments that the authors judged consistent and reasonable. (PDF p. 5).
- Structure prediction improved for T1039-D1, T1064-D1, and T1070-D1, but not T1043-D1. Learned MSAs were less consistent than MMSeqs2 MSAs. (PDF p. 5).
- For T1039-D1, retaining only the query and closest homolog improved the MMSeqs2-based RMSD from 7.62 to 2.79. The learned full-set MSA gave RMSD 2.66. (PDF p. 5).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Maximizing AlphaFold confidence does not require biologically sensible alignments. Conserved motifs can become inconsistently aligned. (PDF p. 6).
- Structure predictions can be highly sensitive to the removal of one sequence. Removing distant sequences helped some targets and harmed others. (PDF p. 5).
- The demonstrated AlphaFold improvement covers only three of four examples. It does not establish a general improvement across targets. (PDF p. 5).
- The model trains a separate network for each family. Training one scoring network across families is proposed as future work. (PDF p. 6).
