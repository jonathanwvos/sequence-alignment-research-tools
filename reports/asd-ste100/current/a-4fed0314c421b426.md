## Executive summary

- The authors use the crystal basis model of the genetic code to derive relations between amino acid physical-chemical properties. (PDF p. 1).
- They compare predicted affinities with experimental property values and report broad agreement with several exceptions. (PDF p. 4).

## Objectives

- The study seeks to explain close property relations between some amino acids and the unusual pattern for codons with G in the second position. (PDF p. 1).
- The abstract states an aim to predict thermodynamic parameters that had not been measured for three amino acids. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The model assumes a hierarchy of codon features that influence amino acid properties. Dinucleotide representation and charge sign have the highest priority. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors explain the second-position G pattern through representation membership and charge Q. No two relevant states share both features. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors suggest that codon multiplets may relate to genetic code evolution. They present this as an argument for future study. (PDF p. 9).

## Analysis

- The representation analysis predicts close properties for His/Gln, Asp/Glu, and Leu/Val. It also predicts affinities for Pro/Ser and Ala/Thr. (PDF p. 3).
- Table III compares amino acid pairs using property means, standard deviations, and relative standard deviations. (PDF p. 8).
- For amino acids with A in the central codon position, the reported affinity estimator eta ranges from 0.34 to 1.37. (PDF p. 9).

## Methods

- The model represents nucleotides as states of the (1/2, 1/2) representation of Uq[sl(2) ⊕ sl(2)] as q approaches zero. Ordered tensor products of three states represent codons. (PDF p. 2).
- The first two nucleotides define a dinucleotide representation. A charge Q separates eight strong dinucleotides associated with codon quadruplets from eight weak dinucleotides associated with doublets or singlets. (PDF p. 2).
- The property hierarchy also uses the vertical third component within a dinucleotide representation and the labels of codon representations. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The authors apply the model to qualitative prediction of amino acid property affinities from codon structure. (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- The comparison covers 10 measures: two conformational measures, polarity, relative hydrophilicity, three thermodynamic activation parameters, two dissociation measures, and the isoelectric point. (PDF p. 4).
- Table I lists property values for the 20 amino acids and gives overall means, standard deviations, and relative standard deviations. (PDF p. 6).
- Table V compares eta with an extended estimator that adds refractivity, bulkiness, molecular weight, surface, and volume data. (PDF p. 9).

## Results

- The authors report very good agreement for His/Gln except for the isoelectric point, and for Asp/Glu except for turn propensity. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors report very good agreement for Leu/Val. For Cys/Tyr, relative hydrophilicity is an exception. (PDF p. 4).
- For Pro/Ser, the authors report very good agreement except for the combined helix-sheet measure and activation enthalpy. (PDF p. 4).
- Table III gives relative standard deviations of 0% for Leu/Val pKb and isoelectric point, at the table's reported precision. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors state that some properties depend mainly on specific amino acid chemical structure and need not follow the model hierarchy. (PDF p. 3).
- Table I lacks all three thermodynamic values for Asp and Glu. It also lacks enthalpy and entropy values for His. (PDF p. 6).
- The proposed connection to evolutionary organization remains a suggestion for later investigation. (PDF p. 9).
