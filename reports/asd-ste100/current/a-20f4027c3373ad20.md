## Executive summary

- GRASPx searches short peptide databases for homologs of a reference protein. It aligns and assembles peptides during the same search. (PDF p. 1).
- GRASPx accelerates GRASP through prebuilt extension links, local seed assembly, and query-level parallelism. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors report more than 30-fold speedup over GRASP with similar accuracy. They also report better homolog detection than the tested BLAST and FASTA programs. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- Improve GRASP computational efficiency while retaining its homolog detection performance. (PDF p. 2).
- Support annotation of incomplete metagenomic proteins through reference-guided assembly and homolog search. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that contig-to-query similarity provides a better measure of homology than individual read-to-query similarity. (PDF p. 2).
- Alignment scores guide graph traversal and prune false paths. The authors state that this can improve detection of low-abundance homologs. (PDF p. 2).
- The gene-centric formulation permits low-similarity reads to join higher-similarity reads in a contig that meets the query similarity threshold. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- The gene-centric problem seeks contigs assembled from input peptides whose similarity to the query exceeds a specified threshold. Reads in accepted contigs form the homolog set. (PDF p. 3).
- Direct alignment is a special case of this formulation in which each candidate contig contains one read. (PDF p. 3).
- For two pathway-related queries missed by PSI-BLAST, BLASTP alignments of GRASPx contigs gave E-values of 2 × 10−94 and 2 × 10−172. The authors use these results to support improved recall. (PDF p. 10).

## Methods

- GRASPx indexes the peptide database once and finds exact k-mer seed matches in a reduced-alphabet space. (PDF p. 5).
- It follows prebuilt links to maximal extension sequences. Banded Needleman-Wunsch alignment scores guide iterative extension and termination. (PDF p. 5).
- Extension stops at a predefined depth. Greedy recalibration merges overlapping contigs by overlap length and recalculates alignment scores and E-values. (PDF p. 5).
- Post-mapping recovers constituent reads from assembled contigs. It requires alignment over at least 60% of read length and permits at most three substitution errors. (PDF p. 5).

## Applications

- GRASPx supports short peptide annotation, reference-based homolog search, and gene-centric assembly. (PDF p. 10).
- The authors used MinPath to reconstruct pathways from homologous reads found in human saliva. Queries comprised all proteins encoded by P. acnes. (PDF p. 10).

## Optimization

- Prebuilt extension links replace repeated suffix-array searches and redundancy removal. Each maximal extension sequence can be retrieved in constant time. (PDF p. 3).
- Extension links are constructed by linear traversal of a suffix array and its longest common prefix array. Construction time is linear in peptide database size. (PDF p. 3).
- Each source read has at most 20 extension links. The authors chose this empirical limit to balance performance and computational efficiency. (PDF p. 3).
- Query-level threads reduce communication. Discarding constituent read information during assembly limits memory use; post-mapping later recovers that information. (PDF p. 5).

## Evaluation and testing

- The benchmark compares GRASPx with GRASP, BLASTP, PSI-BLAST, and FASTM. GRASP is excluded from the large genome-wide benchmark because of its computational cost. (PDF p. 5).
- Simulated data set DS1 uses 20 marine microbial genomes with staggered abundances. WGSIM generated reads at 10-fold coverage, expected length 100 bp, and 1% error rate. (PDF p. 5).
- FragGeneScan identified 6,273,043 peptide reads from DS1. Real data set DS2 comes from a Human Microbiome Project saliva sample. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors compare recall at equal precision and assess runtime. A separate pathway test compares MinPath results from GRASPx and PSI-BLAST predictions at an E-value cutoff of 10−10. (PDF p. 10).

## Results

- The authors report similar accuracy to GRASP. At equal precision, recall was approximately 20% higher than PSI-BLAST and 30% higher than BLASTP and FASTM. (PDF p. 2).
- GRASPx was more than 30 times faster than GRASP and had similar runtime to PSI-BLAST. A search of about 40 million reads took approximately 12 hours with 16 threads. (PDF p. 10).
- For approximately six million reads, GRASPx used about 14 GB with 16 threads, compared with about 13 GB for GRASP. A 198-marker search took about 36 minutes, with post-mapping below two minutes. (PDF p. 5).
- MinPath reconstructed 73 pathways from GRASPx predictions and 71 from PSI-BLAST predictions. The GRASPx set contained all 71 PSI-BLAST pathways plus fatty acid degradation and amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism. (PDF p. 10).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Local assembly can split a complete contig into smaller pieces. Greedy recalibration attempts to repair these breaks. (PDF p. 5).
- The evidence for the empirical 20-link limit is not shown in the paper excerpt. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors state that the biological relatedness of the two validated contigs to P. acnes remains unclear, despite their high query sequence similarity. (PDF p. 10).
- MinPath measures pathway presence or absence and ignores abundance. Improved results with abundance-aware reconstruction tools are proposed, but are not demonstrated here. (PDF p. 10).
