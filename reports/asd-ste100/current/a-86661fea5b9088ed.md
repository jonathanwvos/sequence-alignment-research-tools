## Executive summary

- The authors develop algorithms for concatenated LCS, S-table merging, and incremental character updates using linear-space S-tables. (PDF p. 2).
- Reported time bounds are O(n) for improved concatenation, O(n log n) for merging, and O(n) for one appended-character update. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- Use the first S-table row and changes between consecutive rows to avoid storing the full quadratic-space table. (PDF p. 1).
- Compute LCS lengths for concatenated strings and updated strings while retaining the linear-space representation. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that quadratic-space S-tables become impractical for long sequences. (PDF p. 1).
- The concatenated result contains LCS lengths against every prefix of B, so it can support further concatenations. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- An S-table entry records the earliest endpoint in B that achieves a specified LCS length for a fixed starting position. A missing endpoint is represented by infinity. (PDF p. 3).
- The merge algorithm uses segment-tree range queries and updates. Each operation costs O(log n), giving O(n log n) total time. (PDF p. 13).
- After initialization, each position in the incremental status array is accessed and modified at most twice. The authors derive O(n) time and O(n) working space. (PDF p. 15).

## Methods

- CoLCS takes the S-table for A(2) against B and prefix LCS results for A(1). It computes prefix LCS results for A(1)A(2) against B. (PDF p. 2).
- The merge algorithm computes the first output row, then uses two segment trees to obtain subsequent row changes. One tree maintains range minima of δ; the other maintains range maxima of D(2). (PDF p. 13).
- The incremental algorithm uses next(i), the next occurrence of the appended character in B. It updates the first row and maintains a status array to compute later row changes. (PDF p. 15).

## Applications

- Consecutive suffix alignment supports common substring alignment and cyclic string comparison. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors propose applying these algorithms to compressed-string LCS by constructing linear-space S-tables for substrings and then combining them. (PDF p. 16).

## Optimization

- The improved CoLCS algorithm uses range maximum queries implemented with set union-find. This reduces the initial O(n log n) time bound to O(n). (PDF p. 2).
- Merging operates on compact S-tables and improves the straightforward O(nL) method to O(n log n), where L is the LCS length. (PDF p. 16).
- Incremental updates use linear scans and bounded status changes to avoid reconstructing the complete S-table. (PDF p. 15).

## Evaluation and testing

Not stated in the extractable source pages.

## Results

- The authors provide four algorithms for three problems. These compute LCS lengths using the linear-space representation. (PDF p. 2).
- The stated bounds cover concatenation from supplied tables, merging two supplied tables, and updating a supplied table after appending one character. (PDF p. 16).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The algorithms compute LCS lengths only. They do not reconstruct the subsequence. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors identify compressed-string applications as future work. They note that known lower bounds can make further LCS improvements difficult. (PDF p. 16).
- The article states that no data was used in the research. (PDF p. 16).
