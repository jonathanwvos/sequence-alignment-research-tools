## Executive summary

- The authors introduce TM-Vec 2 and TM-Vec 2s for fast protein structural similarity prediction from sequence alone. (PDF p. 1).
- TM-Vec 2 replaces large language models with compact, efficient transformers to predict TM-scores directly. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- Detect remote structural homology from amino acid sequences without requiring 3D coordinate prediction or heavy PLMs. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Compact transformer models with optimized attention can match the structural representation power of 3-billion-parameter PLMs. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- TM-score prediction correlation and runtime scaling are benchmarked against TM-align and Foldseek. (PDF p. 6).

## Methods

- Efficient transformers (Lobster and Ankh) embed protein sequences into continuous vectors. (PDF p. 3).
- Cosine similarity between sequence embeddings is trained with margin loss to predict TM-align scores. (PDF p. 4).

## Applications

- High-throughput structural annotation of metagenomic dark matter and viral proteomes. (PDF p. 2).

## Optimization

- Vector cosine calculations enable database searching using approximate nearest neighbor indexing. (PDF p. 4).

## Evaluation and testing

- Models are evaluated on CATH S100 and SCOPe 40 datasets against TM-align and Foldseek ground truth. (PDF p. 6).

## Results

- TM-Vec 2 achieves encoding and search throughput up to two orders of magnitude faster than Foldseek with high correlation. (PDF p. 5).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Prediction accuracy decreases on orphan proteins and structural folds underrepresented in training datasets. (PDF p. 8).
