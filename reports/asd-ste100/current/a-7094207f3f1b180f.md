## Executive summary

- Hirschberg presents two algorithms that recover an LCS. Their time bounds depend on the LCS length p for input lengths m ≤ n. (PDF p. 1).
- ALGD takes O(pn + n log n) time. The second algorithm has a stated bound of O(p(m + 1 − p) log n). (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- Recover an LCS with input-dependent bounds that can be below quadratic time. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- A candidate can be discarded if another candidate of the same length has no larger coordinate. The retained candidate can generate every extension of the discarded candidate. (PDF p. 2).
- The author proposes ALGD for inputs whose expected LCS length is small relative to the input lengths. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- Minimal candidates have increasing first coordinates and decreasing second coordinates. At most one minimal candidate exists for each first coordinate and subsequence length. (PDF p. 3).
- The ALGE analysis bounds its main candidate search by O(pe log n). It separately identifies O(n log m) preprocessing for NEXTB. (PDF p. 10).
- The KEEP structure uses O(e²) space. The stated total space is linear in n when e = O(√n). (PDF p. 10).

## Methods

- ALGD represents the LCS dynamic programming matrix by contours. Minimal k-candidates identify the corners between regions with values k − 1 and k. (PDF p. 3).
- The D matrix stores the second coordinate of each minimal candidate, or zero when no such candidate exists. (PDF p. 3).
- ALGE stores feasible candidates in F and G arrays. KEEP records skipped positions so that RECOVER can construct the subsequence. (PDF p. 8).

## Optimization

- Candidate dominance removes states that cannot provide a distinct extension. (PDF p. 2).
- ALGE uses reference counts to release KEEP records after all candidate references have been removed. (PDF p. 8).
- When the skip bound e is unknown, ALGE doubles an initial guess of two until it finds a sufficient bound. The author derives O(p(m + 1 − p) log n) time from this process. (PDF p. 10).

## Results

- The author gives ALGD an O(pn + n log n) time bound for LCS recovery. (PDF p. 3).
- The author states that the second algorithm can take much less than n² time when p is close to m. (PDF p. 1).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The analysis assumes that each alphabet symbol fits in one memory word and that symbol comparison takes unit time. (PDF p. 1).
- The proposed O(pe log log n) improvement is tentative. The author suggests that a priority-queue method might accelerate position finding, but does not establish that improvement here. (PDF p. 10).
