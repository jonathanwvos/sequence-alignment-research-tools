## Executive summary

- Myers and Miller adapt Hirschberg's divide-and-conquer method to Gotoh's algorithm for affine gap costs. (PDF p. 1).
- The method produces one optimal alignment in linear space while retaining O(MN) time. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors provide a portable C implementation that returns the minimum cost and an optimal sequence of editing operations. (PDF p. 6).

## Objectives

- Remove the quadratic traceback storage requirement when only one optimal alignment is needed. (PDF p. 1).
- Make Hirschberg's method more accessible through a direct development for affine gap costs. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that storage often limits alignment computation before running time does. (PDF p. 1).
- They distinguish the storage needed to produce one optimal alignment from the storage used to record traceback choices for all optimal alignments. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- The objective minimizes replacement, insertion, and deletion costs. A gap of length k costs g + hk, with non-negative g and h. (PDF p. 1).
- The dynamic program maintains minimum total cost and costs for paths ending in deletion or insertion. Dependencies permit row-by-row computation. (PDF p. 2).
- Recursive subproblem areas decrease geometrically. The analysis gives a leading time cost below twice the top-level matrix computation. (PDF p. 6).
- The core implementation stores four vectors of length N. The recursion stack adds O(log M) space, and storing the output operations adds M + N words. (PDF p. 6).

## Methods

- The cost-only algorithm updates two row vectors in place. Three scalar values retain the insertion state and adjacent costs needed by the recurrence. (PDF p. 2).
- Forward and reverse cost computations meet at the middle row. Their results identify a point on an optimal conversion. (PDF p. 3).
- The method recursively computes optimal conversions on both sides of the selected midpoint. (PDF p. 3).
- DIFF returns the minimum conversion cost and encodes replacements as zero, deletions as negative lengths, and insertions as positive lengths. (PDF p. 7).

## Applications

- The method computes complete pairwise sequence alignments with affine gap penalties and a supplied replacement-cost table. (PDF p. 1).
- A similarity-maximization problem can be transformed into the cost-minimization form used by the program. (PDF p. 1).

## Optimization

- Recomputing costs during recursive division replaces storage of the full traceback matrix. (PDF p. 3).
- Output operations can be printed immediately to avoid storing the edit script. The replacement table can be reduced to the alphabet size. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors note that DNA input storage can be reduced to two bits per symbol. (PDF p. 6).

## Evaluation and testing

- The C software was written and tested on a Vax 11/780 running 4.3 BSD Unix. (PDF p. 2).
- A worked example aligns agtac with aag using mismatch cost 1 and gap cost 2 + 0.5k. Its unique optimal conversion has cost 4. (PDF p. 3).
- The memory comparison uses four-byte words and reports capacities with and without stored output operations. (PDF p. 6).
- The timing comparison uses a straightforward dynamic program with proportional gap costs as the reference implementation. (PDF p. 6).

## Results

- The implementation took an average of 153MN microseconds on the Vax 11/780. Its execution time was 1.84 times that of the reference program. (PDF p. 6).
- At 1000 K bytes, the table gives equal-sequence length limits of 62,500 without stored operations and 41,666 with stored operations. The compared seven-bit-per-cell method supports length 1,069. (PDF p. 6).
- For affine costs, the authors report that their more general concave-gap software takes 3.0 times as much time and 3.5 times as much space. (PDF p. 2).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The implementation produces one optimal alignment. It does not enumerate all optimal alignments. (PDF p. 1).
- Linear storage does not remove the O(MN) time requirement. (PDF p. 1).
- The memory capacity table slightly overstates practical limits because it omits the recursion stack. (PDF p. 6).
- The reported factor of 1.84 compares against a simpler proportional-gap program. It is not a direct comparison with a full-matrix affine-gap implementation. (PDF p. 6).
