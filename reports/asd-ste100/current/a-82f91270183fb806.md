## Executive summary

- The method computes discrete convolutions with radial kernels in two dimensions. Its Fourier frequencies lie on concentric circles. (PDF p. 1).
- Efficient Bessel Decomposition (EBD) reduces the number of frequency samples. The authors report faster convolution evaluation after longer precomputation. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- The author aims to extend Sparse Cardinal Sine Decomposition from three dimensions to two dimensions using Bessel functions. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- Radial symmetry permits a compact frequency representation. The authors argue that fewer samples can offset the higher cost of a type-3 NUFFT. (PDF p. 3).
- The one-dimensional approximation permits direct error control. The author contrasts this with manual parameter adjustment in Fastsum. (PDF p. 22).

## Analysis

- EBD coefficient calculation requires inversion of a P-by-P matrix and O(P^3) work. The total number of circular-quadrature terms is O(P^2). (PDF p. 19).
- The circular-quadrature error allocation bounds the maximum error of the trigonometric kernel representation by epsilon. (PDF p. 19).
- The stated far-field evaluation bound is O(Nz^(4/3−2η) log(Nz) |log ε|^4). The close-correction bound is O(Nz^(4/3+η)) under the analyzed node assumptions. (PDF p. 20).

## Methods

- EBD approximates the radial kernel on an annulus with a constant and a sum of zero-order Bessel functions. Coefficients minimize a radius-weighted squared derivative error. (PDF p. 3).
- Circular quadratures convert the Bessel representation into a trigonometric sum with frequencies on concentric circles. (PDF p. 3).
- Online evaluation applies a negative-sign NUFFT, multiplies by the kernel weights, and applies a positive-sign NUFFT. (PDF p. 20).
- A fixed-radius neighbor search identifies close node pairs. A sparse matrix corrects the kernel approximation for these pairs. (PDF p. 19).

## Applications

- Boundary integral solutions of the Laplace equation require repeated convolutions with a logarithmic kernel. (PDF p. 2).
- The author identifies iterative solution of large linear systems with radial-kernel matrix entries as a use for faster online evaluation. (PDF p. 22).

## Optimization

- Concentric-circle frequency sampling reduces the number of terms compared with the Cartesian frequency grid used by Fastsum. (PDF p. 3).
- Piecewise polynomial interpolation accelerates evaluation of the smooth Bessel sum used in close corrections. (PDF p. 19).
- The cutoff radius δmin controls the balance between kernel approximation work and close-pair correction work. (PDF p. 22).

## Evaluation and testing

- The numerical comparison with Fastsum records frequency counts, offline time, online time, and error. The supplied tables include r^2 log(r) and 1/r^2 kernels with 10^3 to 10^6 nodes. (PDF p. 22).
- EBD uses Matlab and a FORTRAN NUFFT routine. Fastsum uses C. The author states that this difference mainly affects offline timings. (PDF p. 22).

## Results

- The author reports about 100 times fewer frequency samples in most tested cases. (PDF p. 22).
- Online evaluation is reported to be faster in every tested case, generally by a factor of 2 to 3. Offline computation is 2 to 4 times slower in computationally intensive cases. (PDF p. 22).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Automatic selection of the best δmin remains unresolved when the node distribution is unknown. (PDF p. 23).
- The implementation requires longer precomputation than Fastsum. Repeated convolutions can make this cost easier to recover. (PDF p. 22).
- The author leaves extension to general dimension for future work. (PDF p. 23).
