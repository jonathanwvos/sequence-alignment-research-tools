## Executive summary

- The authors introduce the open-closed mod-minimizer algorithm to sample k-mers from DNA strings at reduced density. (PDF p. 1).
- The method extends the miniception scheme using open and closed syncmers as anchor selections. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- Construct robust, low-density seed sampling schemes that improve upon random minimizers for sequence comparison. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Selecting open and closed syncmers as anchors guarantees lower density bounds across window shifts. (PDF p. 5).

## Analysis

- Density is analyzed theoretically and measured empirically on synthetic random strings and genomic sequences. (PDF p. 16).

## Methods

- The algorithm identifies open and closed syncmers of length t as anchor selections within window w. (PDF p. 6).
- A modular arithmetic condition selects canonical k-mers based on anchor offsets. (PDF p. 12).

## Applications

- Seeding in sequence alignment, genome assembly, and sequence search data structures. (PDF p. 2).

## Optimization

- Independent counting of open and closed syncmers reduces density computation complexity to O(x^5). (PDF p. 10).

## Evaluation and testing

- The method is evaluated against random minimizers, miniceptions, and mod-minimizers across diverse window sizes. (PDF p. 16).

## Results

- The open-closed mod-minimizer achieves strictly lower density than standard minimizers and miniceptions. (PDF p. 16).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Computing exact theoretical density expectations requires higher computational complexity for large window sizes. (PDF p. 10).
