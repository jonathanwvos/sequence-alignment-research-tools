## Executive summary

- Klump calculates codon–anticodon interaction energies and rearranges the codon table to expose thermodynamic symmetry. (PDF p. 3).
- The author proposes that successive transition mutations from 16 equal-energy codons can generate the energy profile of all 64 codons. (PDF p. 6).
- The author claims that discrete mutation energy changes show that the modern genetic code is robust. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- The author separates the development of the codon set from the historical assignment of codons to amino acids. (PDF p. 1).
- The study examines whether thermodynamic effects could produce codon symmetry without pre-existing functional proteins. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- The author proposes that the need for three consecutive complementary base pairs to initiate a stable helix may explain the origin of triplet coding. (PDF p. 2).
- The author argues that hydrogen-bond counts alone cannot explain mutation energy changes. Base stacking and structural context must also contribute. (PDF p. 6).

## Analysis

- The author represents codon octets as cubes. Edges represent single base replacements, faces combine two replacements, and space diagonals combine three. (PDF p. 5).
- Within each cube, three transition mutations connect two codons with interaction energy 3.46 kcal/mol. Their net energy change is zero, regardless of mutation order. (PDF p. 5).
- For transitions from the equal-energy subset, the reported energy changes are smallest at the third position, intermediate at the first, and largest at the second. (PDF p. 6).

## Methods

- The author calculates triplet interaction energies at 298 K with a nearest-neighbour model. It sums free-energy increments from measured duplex formation parameters. (PDF p. 3).
- The codon table uses base order T, C, G, A. The author selects the mammalian mitochondrial code because it has greater symmetry than the universal code. (PDF p. 3).
- The proposed evolutionary analysis mainly uses transition mutations, which replace a purine with a purine or a pyrimidine with a pyrimidine. (PDF p. 2).

## Applications

- The author uses the energy model to examine a possible prebiotic development of the codon set before codons acquired amino acid assignments. (PDF p. 1).

## Results

- The author identifies 16 codons with the same interaction energy of 3.46 kcal/mol. Each base occurs equally often at each position within this subset. (PDF p. 3).
- Complementary codon families share interaction energies. The energy spectrum therefore displays 32 entries for the 64 codons. (PDF p. 5).
- Third-position transitions from the equal-energy subset produce changes of approximately 0.50, 0.84, or 1.17 kcal. The author compares their level spacing with thermal noise. (PDF p. 6).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The author states that the historical origin of the common genetic code cannot be predicted or reconstructed. (PDF p. 2).
- The evolutionary model assumes initially random sequences with equal base probabilities. It mainly considers transitions because transversions can produce changes below thermal noise. (PDF p. 2).
- Interpretation: the reported energy symmetry depends on the chosen thermodynamic model and table arrangement. The author also selects the more symmetrical mitochondrial code for presentation. (PDF p. 3).
