## Executive summary

- The authors present a method to reconstruct phylogenetic trees from amino acid sequence differences. (PDF p. 1).
- The method computes minimum mutation distances between homologous proteins and estimates ancestral branch lengths. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- Reconstruct evolutionary relationships from primary structure differences among homologous proteins. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- Primary structures contain sufficient historical records to determine evolutionary divergence. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- The authors solve sets of linear equations to distribute branch lengths among tree nodes. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- The algorithm computes a minimum mutation distance matrix between pairs of homologous sequences. (PDF p. 1).
- Branch lengths are assigned by minimizing the difference between input distances and tree distances. (PDF p. 2).

## Applications

- Phylogenetic reconstruction of vertebrate and eukaryotic cytochrome c sequences. (PDF p. 2).

## Optimization

Not stated in the extractable source pages.

## Evaluation and testing

- The authors test the algorithm on cytochrome c sequences from 20 eukaryotic species. (PDF p. 2).

## Results

- The reconstructed phylogenetic tree agrees closely with traditional taxonomic classifications. (PDF p. 3).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The method does not correct for back-mutations or multiple substitutions at single sites. (PDF p. 2).
