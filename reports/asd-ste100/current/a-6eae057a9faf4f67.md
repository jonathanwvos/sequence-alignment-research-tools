## Executive summary

- AlphaFold predicts protein atomic coordinates with a neural network that uses sequence, MSA, and structural information. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors report accuracy close to experimental structures in many cases, including targets without known similar structures. (PDF p. 1).
- The system uses an Evoformer to process MSA and residue-pair representations, followed by a module that constructs three-dimensional structure. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- Reduce the gap between known protein sequences and experimentally determined structures through accurate computational prediction. (PDF p. 1).
- Combine evolutionary information with physical and geometric constraints in an end-to-end prediction model. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that physical and geometric constraints in the network help it learn efficiently from limited PDB data. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors hypothesize that MSA information helps establish an early structural estimate. They suggest that later refinement depends less strongly on this information. (PDF p. 6).

## Analysis

- Removing BFD reduced mean CASP14 accuracy by 0.4 GDT. Removing MGnify reduced it by 0.7 GDT, and removing both reduced it by 6.1 GDT. (PDF p. 8).
- Most targets changed little when these databases were removed, but some changed by more than 20 GDT. The authors interpret the database contributions as largely overlapping. (PDF p. 8).
- Predicted confidence correlated with measured accuracy on 10,795 chains. Pearson correlations were 0.76 for pLDDT versus lDDT-Cα and 0.85 for pTM versus TM-score. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- HHblits searches BFD and Uniclust30 for MSA sequences. Jackhmmer searches UniRef90 and MGnify, while HHsearch retrieves templates from PDB70. (PDF p. 8).
- Each Evoformer block transfers information from the MSA to the pair representation through an outer product summed over sequences. (PDF p. 3).
- The structure module represents each residue with a rotation and translation. Invariant point attention combines geometric, single-residue, and pair representations. (PDF p. 8).
- Training uses FAPE and auxiliary losses for distances, masked MSA reconstruction, confidence, and side chains. Fine-tuning adds a structure violation loss. (PDF p. 8).

## Applications

- The authors report use of AlphaFold predictions for molecular replacement and interpretation of cryogenic electron microscopy maps. (PDF p. 6).
- Prediction takes GPU minutes to hours, depending on sequence length. The authors identify this speed as a basis for proteome-scale structure prediction. (PDF p. 6).

## Optimization

- Recycling feeds predicted outputs back through the network. The authors report a marked accuracy gain with little extra training time. (PDF p. 3).
- Initial training uses 256-residue crops and batches of 128 on 128 TPU v3 cores. Fine-tuning uses 384-residue crops, larger MSAs, and a lower learning rate. (PDF p. 8).
- Self-distillation uses predictions for 355,993 sequences. Final training samples these predictions 75% of the time and clustered PDB structures 25% of the time. (PDF p. 8).
- Five models use different random seeds and different template settings to increase prediction diversity. (PDF p. 8).

## Evaluation and testing

- CASP14 provides a blind test with recently solved structures that were not publicly disclosed to participants. (PDF p. 1).
- The reported CASP14 comparison covers 87 domains. Median accuracy confidence intervals use 10,000 bootstrap samples. (PDF p. 2).
- A later PDB test measured full-chain accuracy on 3,144 chains after redundancy and template-similarity filtering. (PDF p. 3).
- The recent PDB set excludes chains longer than 1,400 residues, NMR structures, chains with too few resolved residues, and chains with unknown or ambiguous residues. (PDF p. 12).

## Results

- Median CASP14 backbone r.m.s.d. at 95% residue coverage was 0.96 Å for AlphaFold and 2.8 Å for the next best method. (PDF p. 2).
- Median all-atom r.m.s.d. at 95% coverage was 1.5 Å for AlphaFold and 3.5 Å for the best alternative. (PDF p. 2).
- The filtered recent PDB set had a median full-chain backbone r.m.s.d. at 95% coverage of 1.46 Å. Its 95% confidence interval was 1.40–1.56 Å. (PDF p. 3).
- A prediction made after CASP reproduced domain packing in a 2,180-residue chain, with r.m.s.d.95 of 2.2 Å and TM-score of 0.96. (PDF p. 2).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Accuracy falls substantially below a median effective MSA depth of about 30 sequences. Gains above about 100 sequences are small. (PDF p. 6).
- Accuracy is weaker for proteins whose structure depends mainly on contacts with different chains, rather than internal or homotypic contacts. (PDF p. 6).
- Full-chain r.m.s.d. is sensitive to domain packing. Uncertain packing or packing errors can produce high values even when individual domains are accurate. (PDF p. 3).
