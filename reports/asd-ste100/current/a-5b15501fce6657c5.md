## Executive summary

- The authors develop SAFARI to align ancient DNA reads to pangenome graphs under severe post-mortem damage. (PDF p. 1).
- SAFARI uses a purine/pyrimidine (RY) encoding scheme and an ancillary RYmer index to rescue deaminated reads. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- Prevent mis-mapping and reference bias caused by cytosine-to-thymine deamination in ancient DNA sequences. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Standard minimizer seeds fail to match in ancient DNA due to terminal deamination damage. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- Damage models calculate posterior match probabilities to weight candidate alignments without biasing against damage. (PDF p. 5).

## Methods

- An ancillary RYmer index maps purine/pyrimidine k-mers alongside the standard vg Giraffe minimizer index. (PDF p. 3).
- A modified encoding scheme converts deamination-vulnerable transitions into invariant binary categories. (PDF p. 14).

## Applications

- Pangenome alignment and taxonomic profiling of ancient hominin and environmental metagenomic DNA. (PDF p. 12).

## Optimization

- Compact RYmer indices preserve standard short-read alignment speeds while expanding seeding tolerance. (PDF p. 13).

## Evaluation and testing

- SAFARI is benchmarked against standard vg Giraffe on ancient metagenomic datasets across multiple reference graphs. (PDF p. 12).

## Results

- SAFARI recovers significantly more authentic ancient fragments per taxon without introducing false positives. (PDF p. 12).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The ancillary RYmer index increases memory usage and index building time on large eukaryotic genomes. (PDF p. 13).
