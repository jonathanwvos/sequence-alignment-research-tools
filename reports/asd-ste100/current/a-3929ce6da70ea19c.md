## Executive summary

- MSAProbs combines pair-HMM and partition-function posterior probabilities for multiple protein sequence alignment. (PDF p. 1).
- It uses weighted probabilistic consistency, weighted profile-profile alignment, and iterative refinement. OpenMP parallelism reduces execution time. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to improve multiple protein alignment accuracy while keeping execution time competitive on multicore CPUs. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors motivate the combined probability model with earlier results on long terminal extensions. They suggest that partition-function probabilities can help locate highly similar regions. (PDF p. 2).
- Sequence weights account for unequal sampling of related proteins. Weighted consistency prevents every sequence from having identical influence. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- Arithmetic-mean variants performed best near pair-HMM weights of 0.6 to 0.7 on BAliBASE. The authors report that root mean square combination gave higher accuracy. (PDF p. 6).
- Weighting gave small, dataset-dependent gains. The default combination favored PREFAB accuracy while retaining BAliBASE accuracy, but did not maximize BAliBASE SPS. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors give time complexities of O(N²L²) for posterior computation and O(N²L³) for consistency transformation. N is sequence count and L is average sequence length. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- Forward and Backward algorithms compute pair-HMM probabilities. Dynamic programming computes partition-function probabilities. Corresponding values are combined by root mean square. (PDF p. 2).
- Pairwise global alignment uses posterior probabilities as scores and zero gap penalties. Distance equals one minus the optimal score divided by the shorter sequence length. (PDF p. 2).
- MSAProbs builds a UPGMA guide tree and assigns CLUSTALW sequence weights. It applies two weighted consistency iterations by default. (PDF p. 3).
- Weighted profile-profile alignment follows the guide tree with zero gap penalties. Ten default refinement iterations randomly split the sequences into two groups and realign their profiles. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The implemented application is multiple protein sequence alignment. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors suggest possible uses in motif finding and RNA or protein structure prediction. These are proposed applications, not demonstrated results. (PDF p. 6).

## Optimization

- Sparse matrix multiplication reduces consistency-transformation work because most posterior entries are near zero. (PDF p. 3).
- The implementation precomputes exponential terms for the fixed substitution matrix, gap penalties, and beta parameter. (PDF p. 3).
- OpenMP dynamically assigns sequence pairs to threads for posterior computation and consistency transformation. (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- Accuracy tests used BAliBASE, PREFAB, SABmark, and OXBENCH. Tests ran on Linux with a 2.67 GHz Intel i7 quad-core CPU and 12 GB RAM. (PDF p. 3).
- BAliBASE 3.0 contained 386 reference alignments. Scoring used reliable core blocks. PREFAB 4.0 contained 1681 references and scored structural alignments of the original sequence pairs. (PDF p. 3).
- Comparisons included ClustalW, MAFFT, MUSCLE, ProbCons, and Probalign. The authors excluded T-Coffee based on earlier published comparisons. (PDF p. 1).
- Variant tests changed the probability-combination rule and the weighting of consistency and profile alignment. Weighting tests kept other conditions fixed. (PDF p. 6).

## Results

- On PREFAB, MSAProbs achieved mean Q 70.43 in 3:34:36. ProbCons took 15:32:43 and Probalign took 23:59:22. Reported accuracy comparisons with all five aligners had P-values below 10⁻¹⁰. (PDF p. 5).
- On SABmark, MSAProbs achieved overall SPS 60.51 and CS 40.29 in 70 seconds. The authors report significant improvements over all compared aligners for both scores. (PDF p. 5).
- On OXBENCH, MSAProbs achieved SPS 90.06 and CS 81.70. Its difference from Probalign was not statistically significant. (PDF p. 5).
- Default weighting gave PREFAB Q 70.43 versus 69.36 without either weighting method. BAliBASE SPS was 89.09 with defaults versus 89.23 without either weighting method. (PDF p. 6).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- MSAProbs took longer than MUSCLE, MAFFT, and ClustalW on the large BAliBASE and PREFAB benchmarks. (PDF p. 5).
- Consistency transformation remains computationally expensive despite sparse matrices. (PDF p. 3).
- The two weighting methods do not improve every metric. Their contributions depend on the dataset. (PDF p. 6).
