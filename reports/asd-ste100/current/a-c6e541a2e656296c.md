## Executive summary

- The authors present the Z curve database, providing three-dimensional geometric representations of genome sequences. (PDF p. 1).
- The Z curve uniquely and losslessly reconstructs DNA sequences through coordinates representing nucleotide distributions. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- Provide visual and computational tools for analyzing genomic nucleotide distributions and replication origins. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Geometrical curves in three-dimensional space reveal global compositional asymmetry invisible in raw sequence text. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- Cumulative coordinates reflect purine/pyrimidine, amino/keto, and strong/weak hydrogen bond skews across chromosomes. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- Three independent coordinate variables are calculated cumulatively along sequence length N. (PDF p. 3).
- Web-based software services render genomic curves and calculate localized composition profiles. (PDF p. 7).

## Applications

- Identification of replication origins (oriC), terminus regions, and pathogenicity islands in bacterial genomes. (PDF p. 6).

## Optimization

Not stated in the extractable source pages.

## Evaluation and testing

- The system is evaluated across all publicly available fully sequenced bacterial and archaeal genomes. (PDF p. 2).

## Results

- The database reliably highlights genomic islands, horizontal gene transfers, and replication origins across diverse species. (PDF p. 6).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Visual analysis of 3D curves requires user interaction and is less automated than numerical window profiling. (PDF p. 7).
