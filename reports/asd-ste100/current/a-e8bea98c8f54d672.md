## Executive summary

- Muscle5 generates ensembles of accurate multiple sequence alignments by changing HMM parameters and guide-tree joining order. (PDF p. 1).
- It measures confidence in an inference as the fraction of alignment replicates that support it. (PDF p. 2).
- The author reports that ensemble analysis can expose alignment bias that conventional tree bootstrap values do not detect. (PDF p. 5).

## Objectives

- The method aims to vary alignment bias between replicates while maintaining benchmark accuracy. (PDF p. 1).
- The study assesses confidence in alignment columns and phylogenetic inferences, including RNA virus relationships. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The author argues that a single preferred alignment can hide systematic errors caused by model parameters and guide-tree order. (PDF p. 1).
- The author argues that high bootstrap values can support incorrect tree edges when the input alignment contains bias. (PDF p. 5).
- The author presents ensemble analysis and bootstrapping as complementary assessments of phylogenetic confidence. (PDF p. 5).

## Analysis

- Column Confidence is the fraction of replicates that reproduce a column. Alignment Confidence averages these values within a replicate, and MAC averages Alignment Confidence across the ensemble. (PDF p. 2).
- Edge Confidence and Topology Confidence measure support across replicate trees. These measures do not resample alignment columns. (PDF p. 2).
- For two RNA virus phylum alignments, changing only guide-tree order produced conflicting topologies. All six tree methods agreed within each alignment. The author identifies this as guide-tree bias. (PDF p. 4).
- Ensemble Monophyly averages TP/(TP + FP + FN) across trees. Each tree uses the subtree that minimizes category assignment errors. (PDF p. 7).

## Methods

- A double-affine-gap HMM computes posterior probabilities for residue pairs. The model uses separate states for short and long gaps and BLOSUM62-based protein emission probabilities. (PDF p. 6).
- The parallel ProbCons implementation applies two consistency transformations by default, constructs a greedy maximum expected accuracy guide tree, and performs 100 randomized refinement rounds. (PDF p. 6).
- HMM probabilities change by P → (1 + αδ)P, with δ uniform from −1 to 1. The default α is 0.25. Probabilities are then normalized within mutually exclusive event sets. (PDF p. 7).
- The guide tree is split into three subtrees of approximately equal size. Muscle5 uses the original tree and three joining-order permutations while largely preserving joins near the leaves. (PDF p. 7).

## Applications

- The study applies ensemble analysis to RdRp alignments for four coronavirus genera and five Riboviria phyla. (PDF p. 2).
- Column Confidence identifies a variable surface loop with uncertain alignment in a four-protein Balibase example. (PDF p. 3).
- The author proposes ensemble confidence for secondary structure predictions and paralog or ortholog discrimination. (PDF p. 6).

## Optimization

- Muscle5 parallelizes the ProbCons core. The Super5 divide-and-conquer strategy supports larger datasets. (PDF p. 6).
- The perturbation amplitude was selected by trial and error on a Balibase subset. Individual replicate parameters are not optimized against the input sequences. (PDF p. 7).
- Guide-tree permutations change joins among large groups while preserving much of the order near the leaves. This design seeks diverse biases without reducing accuracy. (PDF p. 7).

## Evaluation and testing

- Accuracy tests use structure-based Balibase protein alignments and Bralibase RNA alignments. Comparisons include Clustal-Omega, MAFFT, and ProbCons. (PDF p. 3).
- The study compares confidence scores with the fraction of correct columns. It also reports a benchmark with 10,000 protein sequences per set. (PDF p. 2).
- RNA virus tests use RAxML, PhyML, IQ-Tree, FastTree, and MEGA neighbour-joining and minimum-evolution methods. Torovirus and reverse transcriptases provide the respective genus and phylum outgroups. (PDF p. 2).
- Palmscan identifies six essential RdRp catalytic residues. A residue is counted as misaligned when its column differs from the majority position. (PDF p. 7).

## Results

- On the 10,000-sequence benchmark, Muscle5 correctly aligned 59% of columns, compared with 52% for Clustal-Omega and 47% for MAFFT. (PDF p. 2).
- Alignment Confidence correlated with correct-column accuracy. Pearson correlations were 0.80 for proteins and 0.84 for RNA. (PDF p. 3).
- MAC was 0.91 for coronavirus genera and 0.18 for Riboviria phyla. The author interprets the lower value as evidence of substantial alignment error. (PDF p. 2).
- Reported Ensemble Monophyly ranged from 41% to 64% across Riboviria phyla. The author interprets the results as evidence against the proposed monophyly of these groups. (PDF p. 5).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Ensemble confidence assumes that replicate variation samples relevant alignment errors. A consistently wrong alignment can violate this assumption. (PDF p. 5).
- Automatic convergence criteria for ensemble generation were proposed but were not implemented in the reported code. (PDF p. 7).
- The author did not validate the method with simulated evolution. He argues that simplified simulation models may not support reliable conclusions about deep evolutionary history. (PDF p. 7).
- Structural similarity does not necessarily establish sequence homology. The author nevertheless uses agreement with structural alignments as evidence of alignment accuracy. (PDF p. 8).
