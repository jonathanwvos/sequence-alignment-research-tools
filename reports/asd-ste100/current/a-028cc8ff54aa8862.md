## Executive summary

- TM-Vec predicts structural similarity from protein sequences. DeepBLAST predicts structural alignments from sequence pairs without structures at inference. (PDF p. 1).
- The proposed pipeline retrieves similar proteins with TM-Vec and aligns them with DeepBLAST. The authors benchmarked the two tools separately. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to support structural similarity search across large sequence databases and alignment of remote homologs. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that direct DeepBLAST searches are impractical for large databases. Each query requires pairwise alignments whose total runtime grows with database size. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors argue that a structural training objective improves protein representations. TM-Vec achieved topology macro AUPR of 0.94, compared with 0.66 for the underlying ProtTrans representations. (PDF p. 5).

## Analysis

- Held-out CATH pairs and domains each had median TM-score prediction error of 0.023. Their correlations with reference scores were 0.936 and 0.901, respectively. (PDF p. 3).
- Performance declined on held-out CATH folds. Median error was 0.042 and correlation was 0.781. The authors interpret these results as evidence of generalization beyond training folds. (PDF p. 5).
- DeepBLAST had lower alignment F1 scores than the tested structural methods. Its TM-score trend on Malidup was similar to Mammoth, but its prediction errors had higher variance. (PDF p. 7).

## Methods

- TM-Vec converts ProtT5 residue embeddings into 512-dimensional protein vectors with a twin neural network. Transformer layers, average pooling, dropout and fully connected layers produce the vectors. Training minimizes L1 error between cosine similarity and reference TM-scores. (PDF p. 12).
- Faiss indexes the protein vectors. A query sequence is encoded once, and cosine similarity selects neighbors with the highest predicted TM-scores. (PDF p. 12).
- DeepBLAST uses convolutional networks to derive match and gap scores from ProtTrans embeddings. Softplus makes match scores positive. Log-sigmoid makes gap scores negative. (PDF p. 12).
- Differentiable Needleman–Wunsch uses logsumexp and softmax to produce an expected traceback matrix. Directional derivatives and local Hessians support training. Cross-entropy compares predicted alignment probabilities with TM-align reference alignments. (PDF p. 13).

## Applications

- The authors applied TM-Vec to BAGEL bacteriocins and used DeepBLAST to align a putative bacteriocin with its three nearest classified neighbors. (PDF p. 9).
- The authors report assigning class or subclass annotations to 28 putative bacteriocins from their nearest embedding clusters. (PDF p. 9).

## Optimization

- Indexed vector retrieval avoids exhaustive DeepBLAST comparisons. The authors report sublinear search scaling with database size. (PDF p. 7).
- The GPU implementation batches differentiable alignments. The authors report runtime about ten times faster than the naive CPU implementation. (PDF p. 13).
- Encoding 50,000 queries took up to 40 minutes on one GPU. Searching their vectors against five million proteins took under 20 seconds. Encoding was the search bottleneck. (PDF p. 7).

## Evaluation and testing

- SWISS-MODEL training data included 141 million chain pairs for training and validation, plus one million held-out test pairs, for chains up to 300 residues. A second dataset contained 320 million pairs for chains up to 1,000 residues. (PDF p. 13).
- The CATH dataset contained 23 million domain pairs. Evaluation separated held-out pairs, held-out domains and held-out folds. Sampling reduced the excess of pairs from different folds. (PDF p. 13).
- DeepBLAST was evaluated against manually curated Malidup and Malisam alignments. Table 2 compares alignment F1 scores with BLAST, HMMER, Needleman–Wunsch, Smith–Waterman and four structural alignment methods. (PDF p. 5).
- CATH retrieval tests compared TM-Vec with FoldSeek, MMseqs2, ProtTucker and other methods. The CATHS20 test measured correct homology retrieval and excluded each query from its lookup database. (PDF p. 5).

## Results

- DeepBLAST achieved F1 scores of 0.265 on Malidup and 0.066 on Malisam. Smith–Waterman achieved 0.114 and 0.031. Dali achieved 0.791 and 0.619. (PDF p. 5).
- On CATHS20, the CATH-trained TM-Vec model achieved 88% homology retrieval accuracy. FoldSeek achieved 85%, ProtTucker 71% and HHBlits 49%. (PDF p. 5).
- On the UniRef50 benchmark, TM-Vec retrieved the same family annotation in its top hit for 92.1% of proteins up to 1,000 residues. Sensitivity among the top 50 hits was 96.9%. (PDF p. 8).
- For BAGEL bacteriocins, 94% of annotated proteins had a nearest neighbor in the same class. TM-Vec separated classes better than TM-align applied to AlphaFold2, OmegaFold and ESMFold predictions in this test. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Alignment F1 uses one curated reference alignment. The authors note that structural alignment becomes ambiguous for remote homologs, and valid alternatives can differ from that reference. (PDF p. 7).
- TM-Vec did not detect structural differences caused by point mutations in the VIPUR benchmark. (PDF p. 9).
- TM-Vec learns global TM-score similarity. It performed below DIAMOND on a family retrieval task that the authors describe as more dependent on local similarity. (PDF p. 9).
- DeepBLAST struggles with large insertions and deletions. The authors propose affine gap costs and shared multitask training as possible improvements, without reporting tests of these changes. (PDF p. 9).
