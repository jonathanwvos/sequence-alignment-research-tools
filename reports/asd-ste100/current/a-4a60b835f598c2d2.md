## Executive summary

- Taylor presents procedures that compute evolutionary distance and an optimal alignment path in one pass of MN iterations. (PDF p. 1).
- The procedures support linear gap weights and gap weights that become constant above a specified length. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- The aim is to reduce alignment computation to MN iterations for both capped and uncapped gap weights. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The author argues that capped gap weights help retain long insertions or deletions that large penalties could otherwise suppress. (PDF p. 5).
- The author warns that sequence similarity can reflect functional convergence instead of common ancestry. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- The distance minimizes the total point-mutation and gap weights. (PDF p. 2).
- The author states that symmetric mutation weights are required for symmetric distance. Variable mutation weights must also satisfy the triangle inequality. (PDF p. 3).
- The author contrasts the proposed MN iterations with Gotoh's two passes of MN steps for linear gap weights. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- Four versions compute a distance matrix. Auxiliary gap states use P(m,n) = min(D(m−1,n) + v, P(m−1,n)) + u and Q(m,n) = min(D(m,n−1) + v, Q(m,n−1)) + u. (PDF p. 3).
- One version stores a single path with codes for diagonal movement, horizontal movement, and vertical gap length. It keeps vertical gap lengths in a separate memory array. (PDF p. 5).
- Another version uses a word array to store path segments. A 16-bit layout permits gaps of up to 127 elements in either sequence and supports enumeration of all optimal paths. (PDF p. 5).
- The program uses FORTRAN IV. The author minimized departures from the standard to support transfer between machines. (PDF p. 8).

## Applications

- The program aligns nucleotide or amino-acid sequences. (PDF p. 1).
- The example output uses RNA sequence portions from the New Jersey and Indiana VSV genomes. (PDF p. 8).

## Optimization

- Known horizontal and vertical gap lengths permit the gap weight to be capped at uL + v for lengths greater than L. (PDF p. 5).
- The single-path encoding reduces disk storage. The author recommends it when disk space is limited. (PDF p. 5).

## Evaluation and testing

- The first three versions were tested on various sequences. The author compared their alignments and evolutionary distances. (PDF p. 5).
- Reported execution times used the first version because the example sequences had only short gaps, described as fewer than about ten elements. (PDF p. 5).

## Results

- The first three versions produced identical alignments and evolutionary distances when their limits were respected. (PDF p. 5).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Several alignments can have the same minimum distance. Different weights can also produce substantially different alignments. (PDF p. 8).
- The author states that alternative alignments and sensitivity to weights tend to increase as the matching percentage decreases. (PDF p. 8).
- Path storage imposes gap-length limits. The illustrated 16-bit representation permits at most 127 elements per gap. (PDF p. 5).
