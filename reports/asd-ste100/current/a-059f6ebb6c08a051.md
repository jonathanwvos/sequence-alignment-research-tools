## Executive summary

- Li and Homer explain alignment indexes, seeding, extension, and their use with different sequencing data. (PDF p. 2).
- Their simulations examine how gaps and paired-end mapping affect alignment and SNP calls. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors conclude that short-read alignment to one reference was no longer the main data-analysis bottleneck at publication. (PDF p. 9).

## Objectives

- Explain recent alignment methods and their applications to sequencing platforms and experimental designs. (PDF p. 2).
- Use simulated data to evaluate the need for gapped alignment and paired-end mapping. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that gapped alignment is essential for variant discovery. Ungapped alignment can produce false SNPs and false structural variation calls. (PDF p. 6).
- Paired-end information can resolve repetitive reads and expose placements that violate the expected relation between mates. (PDF p. 6).

## Analysis

- Trie indexes merge identical reference substrings into one path. This permits shared alignment work that typical hash indexes repeat for each copy. (PDF p. 3).
- Fully sensitive MAQ and SeqMap seed schemes require a number of templates that grows exponentially with the mismatch limit. (PDF p. 2).
- RMAP uses k + 1 templates for k mismatches. Large k reduces seed weight and produces many candidate matches. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- Hash-based methods use seed and extend. BLAST indexes query k-mers, scans for exact matches, extends and joins seeds, and refines alignments with Smith–Waterman. (PDF p. 2).
- Spaced seeds require matches at selected positions. Eland uses six templates to guarantee detection of hits with two mismatches. (PDF p. 2).
- SHRiMP and RazerS use q-gram filtering to support gaps. Extension usually starts from multiple short seed matches. (PDF p. 3).
- QPALMA and TopHat first map reads to identify possible exons. They then enumerate nearby junctions and align unmapped reads to junction sequences. (PDF p. 8).

## Applications

- Alignment supports variant discovery, ChIP-seq, RNA-seq, methylation analysis, and mapping reads back to assemblies. (PDF p. 1).
- Realignment uses reads at the same locus to improve alignment near indels. The authors discuss Realigner, GATK, and SAMtools in this role. (PDF p. 8).

## Optimization

- ZOOM designs spaced seeds for specified read length, sensitivity, and memory. Five weight-14 templates detect all two-mismatch hits in 32-base reads. (PDF p. 2).
- A two-level index hashes a short prefix and then searches within its bucket. This makes peak memory independent of the full seed length. (PDF p. 3).
- SIMD vectorization processes several query positions per CPU cycle. The cited SSE2 Smith–Waterman implementation is reported to exceed a tenfold speedup over the standard algorithm. (PDF p. 3).
- Restricting dynamic programming to regions near seeds reduces unnecessary cell calculations. The authors also cite an O(kL) method for full-query alignment with at most k differences. (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- Figure 2A uses simulated 108-base reads from human genome build36. Mutation rates are 0.085% substitutions and 0.015% indels, with a 2% uniform sequencing error rate. (PDF p. 6).
- The comparison includes gapped paired-end, gapped single-end, ungapped single-end, and BWA-SW single-end configurations of BWA, plus Novoalign paired-end mapping. (PDF p. 6).
- Figure 2A measures misplaced reads against mapped reads across mapping-quality thresholds. Figure 2B measures false SNPs against called SNPs across SNP-quality thresholds. (PDF p. 6).
- For the SNP test, simulated chromosome 6 reads are mapped to the whole genome. SAMtools calls and filters SNPs, and one configuration uses GATK realignment. (PDF p. 6).

## Results

- Paired-end alignment improves sensitivity and specificity over single-end alignment in the reported simulation. (PDF p. 6).
- An ungapped configuration misplaces seven reads near an indel with high confidence. BreakDancer then calls a false translocation that effective gapped aligners avoid. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors report that accurate base-quality scores halve alignment errors. They recommend recalibration when base-calling scores are inaccurate. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors report typical Bowtie and BWA throughput of approximately 7 Gbp per CPU day, compared with approximately 3 Gbp for the Illumina base caller. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The effect of omitting gapped alignment on ChIP-seq and RNA-seq remains an open question in the paper. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors cite a result in which TopHat without known junctions recovers only 72% of splice sites found by ERANGE. (PDF p. 8).
- The authors identify unresolved cloud data-transfer and storage costs, and practical difficulties with human-scale multiple-genome alignment. (PDF p. 9).
