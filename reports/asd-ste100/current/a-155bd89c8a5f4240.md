## Executive summary

- Szymanski presents a maximal common subsequence algorithm for sequences in which each element occurs at most once in the other sequence. (PDF p. 2).
- The stated running time is O(n log n). A restricted integer case permits O(n log log n) time. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- Find a common subsequence of greatest possible length under the stated occurrence restriction. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- The author states that known general algorithms require time proportional to the product of the sequence lengths. The occurrence restriction permits a faster special-case algorithm. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- The restricted problem is equivalent to finding a longest ascending subsequence of a permutation. (PDF p. 3).
- The author also relates the problem to finding the largest linearly ordered subset of a finite collection of vectors in two dimensions. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- For permutations of the first n integers, Algorithm A uses distribution sorting in Step 1 and searches the T array in Step 3. (PDF p. 12).
- Step 3 requires integer insertion, deletion, and predecessor queries. A predecessor query finds the greatest set member below a specified value. (PDF p. 12).

## Applications

- The algorithm addresses the equivalent permutation and two-dimensional ordering problems stated by the author. (PDF p. 3).

## Optimization

- Distribution sorting makes Step 1 take O(n) time. Van Emde Boas techniques make each required Step 3 operation take O(log log n) time. (PDF p. 12).

## Evaluation and testing

- Corollary 10 gives an analytical time bound for two permutations of the first n integers. Its proof uses the costs of sorting and set operations. (PDF p. 12).

## Results

- The abstract states an O(n log n) running time for the restricted common subsequence problem. (PDF p. 2).
- Corollary 10 establishes an O(n log log n) time bound for the permutation case. (PDF p. 12).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The algorithm assumes that each element of either sequence occurs at most once in the other. The stated result does not cover unrestricted repeated matches. (PDF p. 2).
- The supplied proof of the faster bound applies to permutations of the first n integers. (PDF p. 12).
