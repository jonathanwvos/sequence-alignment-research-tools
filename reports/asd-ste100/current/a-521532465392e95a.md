## Executive summary

- The authors study a longest common subsequence of X and Y that contains a required string P as a subsequence. (PDF p. 1).
- They give an O(r min{mN, nM}) time bound. Here m, n, and r are string lengths, and M and N count runs in X and Y. (PDF p. 1).
- The method reduces dynamic programming work by computing faces of matched cuboids instead of their full surfaces. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- Improve the previous O(r(mN + nM))-time algorithm for constrained LCS with run-length encoding. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors identify surface computation for fully matched cuboids as the main cost of the previous method. (PDF p. 2).
- The new method computes faces for both fully matched and partially matched cuboids. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- The standard recurrence forms a three-dimensional DP lattice over prefixes of X, Y, and P. Runs partition this lattice into cuboids. (PDF p. 2).
- Lemma 4.7 uses the number of intervals as a potential function. Both interval insertion and interval merging have constant amortized cost. (PDF p. 10).
- Theorem 4.3 gives O(NMr + r min{mN, nM}) time. My interpretation is that NM ≤ min{mN, nM}, so this agrees with the shorter abstract bound. (PDF p. 10).

## Methods

- Use an inverted index of symbol occurrences to derive recurrence values on cuboid faces. (PDF p. 3).
- Maintain b-interval and s-interval sets to find the critical index for a DP element. (PDF p. 9).
- Algorithm A takes X in RLE form and Y and P in uncompressed form. It initializes boundary values, computes the reduced table, and returns the CLCS length. (PDF p. 10).

## Applications

Not stated in the extractable source pages.

## Optimization

- Compute each critical table element in O(1) amortized time through interval maintenance and critical-index selection. (PDF p. 10).
- Choose whether X or Y supplies the RLE dimension. The smaller matched-face workload gives the min{mN, nM} term. (PDF p. 10).

## Evaluation and testing

- Worked DP examples compare the cells computed by the previous cuboid method with those needed by the proposed method. (PDF p. 2).
- Table 1 illustrates s-interval construction for a specific run and three constraint positions. (PDF p. 9).

## Results

- The authors prove O(NMr + r min{mN, nM}) time for the CLCS problem. (PDF p. 10).
- The displayed Algorithm A explicitly outputs the CLCS length. (PDF p. 10).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The algorithm processes only one of X and Y in RLE form. The authors leave direct processing of both strings, or all three strings, in RLE form as a challenge. (PDF p. 10).
