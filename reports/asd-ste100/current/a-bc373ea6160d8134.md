## Executive summary

- The authors introduce a two-hit extension rule and adaptive gapped extension to improve BLAST speed and sensitivity. (PDF p. 1).
- PSI-BLAST builds a position-specific score matrix from significant alignments and uses it in subsequent database searches. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors report approximately threefold speed improvement for gapped BLAST over the original program. (PDF p. 5).

## Objectives

- The objective is to reduce protein database search time while improving detection of weak sequence similarities. (PDF p. 1).
- PSI-BLAST automates profile construction and repeated search to make sensitive searches easier to use. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- A significant ungapped alignment can contain several nearby word hits. Requiring two hits can avoid many expensive extensions. (PDF p. 2).
- Gapped extension can recover a significant alignment from one detected ungapped region. This permits a higher word threshold during the initial scan. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- BLAST converts raw alignment scores to bit scores using statistical parameters. Expected chance hits depend on bit score and search-space size. (PDF p. 2).
- For the tested settings, two-hit search generated about 3.2 times as many hits but only 0.14 times as many extensions as one-hit search. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors estimate that deciding whether to extend a hit costs about one ninth as much as extending it. The resulting hit-processing stage was about twice as fast. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- The two-hit rule requires two non-overlapping word hits on the same diagonal within distance A. An array stores the most recent hit coordinate for each diagonal. (PDF p. 3).
- Gapped extension uses dynamic programming in both directions from a seed. It retains cells whose optimal local score is no more than Xg below the best score found. (PDF p. 5).
- The seed is the central residue pair in the highest-scoring length-11 segment of the triggering HSP. Shorter HSPs use a central residue pair. (PDF p. 5).
- PSI-BLAST forms a multiple alignment from BLAST results, converts it to a position-specific score matrix, and searches the database with that matrix. (PDF p. 13).

## Applications

- The BRCT search starts with the 215 C-terminal residues of human BRCA1 and searches the NCBI non-redundant protein database. (PDF p. 11).
- The authors use PSI-BLAST to strengthen weak similarities involving GalT proteins and detect a yeast tetraphosphate phosphorylase relationship. (PDF p. 11).

## Optimization

- The two-hit rule lowers the word threshold to preserve sensitivity while avoiding most hit extensions. (PDF p. 3).
- The Xg rule adapts the dynamic programming region to the data. An alignment can cross many diagonals while the active region in each row remains limited. (PDF p. 5).
- The search stage uses a smaller Xg than the final traceback stage. The larger final value improves the reported alignment. (PDF p. 5).
- The output procedure limits alignments per query region instead of imposing one overall limit. This reduces the chance that a frequent domain hides weaker matches elsewhere. (PDF p. 11).

## Evaluation and testing

- The sensitivity test generated 100000 model HSPs for each nominal score from 37 to 92 using BLOSUM-62 and specified amino acid frequencies. (PDF p. 3).
- The test compared length-3 one-hit search at T = 13 with two-hit search at T = 11 and A = 40. It measured the probability of missing each HSP. (PDF p. 3).
- Runtime analysis separates overhead, hit qualification, ungapped extension, and gapped extension. (PDF p. 5).
- The BRCT experiment included alignments with E-values at most 0.01 in subsequent matrices. Iteration stopped at round 16 when no new proteins met that threshold. (PDF p. 12).

## Results

- In the model HSP test, the two-hit method was more sensitive than the one-hit method for scores of at least 33 bits. (PDF p. 3).
- The runtime breakdown assigns 100 relative time units to original BLAST and 33 to gapped BLAST. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors identify seven recent database additions as BRCT proteins. Some did not pass the 0.01 E-value cutoff and required examination of weaker results. (PDF p. 11).
- The authors report that PSI-BLAST can improve sensitivity to weak biological relationships while taking only slightly more time per iteration than gapped BLAST. (PDF p. 13).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The gapped extension remains a heuristic. The Xg cutoff restricts the cells examined and does not guarantee exploration of the full path graph. (PDF p. 5).
- The cited score-distribution theory was not proved for gapped alignments. Gapped statistical parameters require empirical estimation. (PDF p. 2).
- The BRCT search missed some previously described members and produced two likely false positives. (PDF p. 11).
- Inaccurate or unrelated pairwise alignments can affect the evolving multiple alignment. The authors propose additional realignment as a future improvement. (PDF p. 13).
