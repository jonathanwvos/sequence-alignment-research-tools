## Executive summary

- Sellers defines evolutionary distance as the minimum total weight of mutations and deletions that make two sequences alike. (PDF p. 2).
- The author states that the distance is a metric under suitable conditions and can be computed in essentially mn steps for sequence lengths m and n. (PDF p. 2).
- A worked example computes the distance between acbba and abca and traces a best matching through a matrix. (PDF p. 7).

## Objectives

- The paper aims to formalize evolutionary distance and give an efficient algorithm for weighted sequence comparison. (PDF p. 2).
- The author seeks to establish the metric properties needed for applications such as evolutionary tree construction. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- A neutral element represents a deleted term. Representations with the same nonneutral subsequence denote the same evolutionary sequence. (PDF p. 3).
- Minimizing the sum of distances between aligned terms gives the weighted mutation and deletion distance. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- The formal construction starts with a metric on symbols, including the neutral element. Evolutionary distance is the minimum over equivalent sequence representations. (PDF p. 3).
- The stated computation uses essentially mn steps. Each step selects the smallest of three numbers. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- Initialize the first row and column with distances between sequence prefixes and the neutral sequence. Fill the remaining matrix with distances between prefix pairs. (PDF p. 7).
- Each matrix entry selects among a horizontal step with a deletion cost, a diagonal step with a symbol comparison cost, and a vertical step with a deletion cost. (PDF p. 7).
- Recover a best matching by tracing minimum-cost choices backward from the lower-right matrix entry. (PDF p. 7).

## Applications

- The author identifies comparison of DNA sequences as a principal application. (PDF p. 2).
- The author identifies evolutionary tree construction by the Margoliash and Fitch method as an application that requires metric properties. (PDF p. 2).

## Optimization

- The matrix procedure uses previously computed prefix distances to calculate each new entry from three adjacent entries. (PDF p. 7).
- The author claims that the algorithm computes weighted distance more efficiently than the methods then in use. (PDF p. 2).

## Evaluation and testing

- The worked example uses unit costs for distinct symbols. Inspection gives distance 2 through one deletion and one mutation. (PDF p. 7).
- A 6 × 5 matrix computes all 30 prefix-pair cases for acbba and abca. Its final entry agrees with the inspection result. (PDF p. 7).

## Results

- The example gives d(acbba, abca) = 2. Its traceback pairs c with the neutral element and one b with c. (PDF p. 7).
- The author states that this example has a unique best matching because each traceback step has only one choice. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The metric construction assumes that the underlying symbol distances already form a metric, including distances to the neutral element. (PDF p. 3).
