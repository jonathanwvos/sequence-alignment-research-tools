## Executive summary

- The authors present Algorithm SQUARES to locate square occurrences directly in a run-length encoded string with N runs. (PDF p. 6).
- They prove O(N log N) time when squares contained within single runs are reported by expressions instead of explicit enumeration. (PDF p. 7).

## Objectives

- The objective is to locate repeated adjacent nonempty substrings without making the time bound depend on the uncompressed string length. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- Explicit enumeration can require quadratic time because a string of one repeated symbol has Θ(n²) square occurrences. (PDF p. 2).
- Grouping equal-length squares by an interval of ending positions permits constant-time reporting of each group. (PDF p. 2).
- Applying the uncompressed algorithm directly to run symbols misses squares. Run boundaries and partial runs need additional treatment. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- Correctness separates squares into those within one run, those within either recursive part, and those that cross the split. (PDF p. 6).
- Each recursion level uses linear work in the run count. The recurrence T(N) = T(⌊N/2⌋) + T(⌈N/2⌉) + O(N) gives O(N log N) time. (PDF p. 7).
- The authors derive optimality under a model that distinguishes alphabet symbols only through character comparisons. (PDF p. 7).

## Methods

- SQUARES divides the encoded substring evenly by runs. Prefix sums of run lengths convert recursive offsets to uncompressed positions. (PDF p. 6).
- The algorithm tests squares centered at the split separately. It checks matching internal runs, boundary characters, and run-length constraints. (PDF p. 6).
- For crossing squares centered in the right part, the algorithm combines LP and LS arrays with HD and TL adjustments. It reverses both parts to handle left-centered squares. (PDF p. 6).
- Each reported nontrivial family contains an interval of ending positions and a common square length. (PDF p. 2).

## Applications

- The authors identify square detection as relevant to formal language theory, data compression, and computational molecular biology. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors expect particular benefit when many identical characters occur consecutively, since the time bound depends on runs. (PDF p. 7).

## Optimization

- LP and LS are computed in linear time in the encoded substring size. HD and TL then require another linear pass. (PDF p. 7).
- The base case reports within-run squares through parameterized expressions. This avoids work proportional to each run length. (PDF p. 6).

## Evaluation and testing

- A worked example uses u = baba and v = aabaaa. LP and LS identify length-eight squares ending at positions 9 and 10 of the combined string. (PDF p. 3).
- Theorem 1 verifies coverage through the recursive cases and the crossing-square lemmas. (PDF p. 7).

## Results

- The authors establish an O(N log N) algorithm whose time bound is independent of run lengths under the stated output convention. (PDF p. 7).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The bound excludes explicit computation of all within-run squares. These are represented by mathematical expressions. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors leave open whether all distinct squares in a run-length encoded string can be reported in O(N) time. (PDF p. 7).
