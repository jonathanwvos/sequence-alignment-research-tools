## Executive summary

- The authors model protein-decorated DNA minicircles using base-pair level elastic energy minimization. (PDF p. 1).
- They demonstrate how protein binding coordinates DNA supercoiling and mechanical allostery across distance. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- Investigate the cooperative mechanical effects of DNA elasticity and architectural protein positioning in constrained minicircles. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Torsional stress transmitted through the double helix coordinates the spatial binding of distant protein complexes. (PDF p. 15).

## Analysis

- Minicircle energy states are computed across variable helical repeats and superhelical twist densities. (PDF p. 10).

## Methods

- DNA is represented as a discrete elastic rod using six rigid-body base-pair step parameters. (PDF p. 3).
- Constrained boundary conditions enforce covalent circle closure, and elastic energies are minimized numerically. (PDF p. 8).

## Applications

- Chromosome organization, Ter macrodomain condensation by MatP, and synthetic DNA nanostructure modeling. (PDF p. 2).

## Optimization

## Results

- Underwound minicircles bearing protein dimers show significantly reduced elastic energy, stabilizing condensed superhelical fibers. (PDF p. 16).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The coarse-grained base-pair model assumes static elastic potentials and omits explicit solvent fluctuations. (PDF p. 3).

Appendix E. Zajac instability for DNA minicircles The Zajac instability [21, 22] is related to the fact that a twisted elastic ring becomes unstable if the twist density within the ring exceeds a certain value. It is straightforward to transpose this result to our DNA base-pair model (in fact, our mesoscale model together with our ideal force field can be seen as a discretized Kirchhoff elastic rod model). For a DNA fragment of N bp in its rest state, the intrinsic total twist Tw is given by: Tw = N ∗ θ3 360 = N 10.5 , (E.1) where θ3 = 360/10.5 = 34.2857 deg is the intrinsic twist step parameter value. We introduce the excess of twist ∆Tw = Tw − Tw and the stability criterion is written as: |∆Tw| < √ 3 2Γ , (E.2) 24 where Γ is the ratio of the bending and twisting stiffnesses (for our ideal for field we have Γ = 0.715). Since we only consider the case of a planar minicircle we have Tw = Lk = ∆Lk + Lk0 and we recall that Lk0 = [N/10.5] (where the brackets stand for the nearest integer operator). It follows that the criterion can be written as: − √ 3 2Γ −  N 10.5  − N 10.5  < ∆Lk < √ 3 2Γ −  N 10.5  − N 10.5  .
