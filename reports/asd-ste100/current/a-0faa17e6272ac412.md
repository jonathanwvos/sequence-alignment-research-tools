## Executive summary

- The authors parallelize the sequential substring constrained longest common subsequence problem on the CGM model. The result must contain the specified constraint strings in order. (PDF p. 1).
- The proposed algorithm handles overlapping constraint partitions. It partitions the dependency graph in three dimensions and evaluates blocks by diagonals. (PDF p. 7).
- The authors state an execution time of O((mnr + (m + n)|Σ|)/p) with O(p) communication rounds on p processors. (PDF p. 7).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to reduce processor latency through smaller subproblems and fewer communications at each step. (PDF p. 2).
- The scheduling goals are to start processor work early and keep processors active for as long as possible. (PDF p. 4).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that earlier CGM partitioning methods perform poorly on this nonserial dynamic program. Dependencies can require communication of a complete block to all active processors. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors attribute the higher execution times of Ho, Sn, and Ir to communication costs and processor latency. (PDF p. 11).

## Analysis

- The dependency graph contains l node planes of size m × n. Each plane has m + n − 1 diagonals, and nodes at the same level are independent. (PDF p. 4).
- The cited sequential algorithm requires O(mnr + (m + n)|Σ|) time and O(mnl + (m + n)(|Σ| + r)) space. Here, l counts constraint partitions and r is their total length. (PDF p. 4).
- Lemma 1 states a worst-case block evaluation time of O(mnr/p³). The proof uses the block dimensions and the cost of evaluating each node. (PDF p. 7).

## Methods

- PrevMatch tables record earlier symbol occurrences. Reverse searches through these tables produce StartPos tables for constraint partitions. (PDF p. 3).
- For overlapping partitions, preprocessing also finds valid suffix-prefix overlap lengths for adjacent constraints. The dynamic program uses separate states for general solutions and solutions that end at a constraint. (PDF p. 4).
- When p ≤ l, each processor receives one parallel plane. When p > l, a snake-like mapping assigns several processors to a parallel plane. (PDF p. 7).
- Algorithm 2 first computes PrevMatch and StartPos tables. It then evaluates block diagonals and sends computed nodes to processors that need them. (PDF p. 7).

## Applications

- The authors identify bioinformatics as a field that uses SSCLCS. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors propose future use of the method for LCS problems with multiple inclusion or exclusion constraints. (PDF p. 12).

## Optimization

- Three-dimensional partitioning reduces block size. The authors identify l mod p = 0 as the best partitioning case because all parallel planes have equal size. (PDF p. 7).
- When l > p, a processor sends only the last node plane of a computed block to the next processor. It does not send the complete block. (PDF p. 7).
- Independent blocks on a diagonal can execute in parallel. The schedule keeps all processors active from step p through step 2p − 1. (PDF p. 7).

## Evaluation and testing

- Tables 3–5 compare the sequential implementation and the 3D, Ho, and Sn methods. They report execution time, speedup, and efficiency. (PDF p. 11).
- The tests vary n from 512 to 4096, p from 4 to 64, and l from 64 to 1024. Separate comparisons vary one parameter while fixing the others. (PDF p. 11).
- Table 6 compares 3D with Ir using one or two fragmentations. It fixes p = 64 and l = 256 and varies n from 512 to 4096. (PDF p. 12).

## Results

- Table 5 reports a 3D execution time of 10.15 seconds at n = 2048, l = 256, and p = 64. Speedup is 59.70 and efficiency is 93.29%. (PDF p. 11).
- At the same Table 5 setting, Ho and Sn take 585.93 and 599.16 seconds. Their reported speedups are 1.03 and 1.01. (PDF p. 11).
- Table 6 reports a 3D speedup of 60.92 at n = 4096. At n = 512, it reports a speedup of 74.07 and efficiency of 115.73% on 64 processors. (PDF p. 12).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- When p > l, the authors state that snake-like mapping reduces idle time but does not balance processor workloads. (PDF p. 7).
- Processors become idle during the final schedule steps. The number of idle processors increases after each step. (PDF p. 7).
- My interpretation is that the abstract's speedup of up to 59.7 is not the maximum across all reported tests. Table 6 includes a speedup of 74.07. (PDF p. 12).
