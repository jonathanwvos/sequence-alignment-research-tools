## Executive summary

- RoseTTAFold exchanges information between sequence alignments, residue-pair geometry, and three-dimensional coordinates in a three-track neural network. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors report better CASP14 performance than their two-track models, but lower accuracy than AlphaFold2. (PDF p. 4).
- The network also predicts protein complexes directly from sequence information. (PDF p. 8).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to improve protein structure prediction for structural biology research and protein design. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- The authors propose that direct exchange between all three representations can better connect sequence information, residue geometry, and atomic coordinates. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors interpret the controlled comparison with two-track models as evidence that joint processing improves extraction of sequence–structure relationships. (PDF p. 4).

## Analysis

- RoseTTAFold accuracy has a weaker correlation with MSA depth than trRosetta and other tested CASP14 methods. (PDF p. 4).
- More paired sequences improve complex predictions. The authors suggest that residue co-evolution helps determine the relative positions of protein chains. (PDF p. 8).

## Methods

- Attention operates on the MSA, distance map, and backbone coordinates. Information passes between the three tracks. (PDF p. 4).
- Training uses discontinuous crops with two sequence segments and 260 residues in total. Final prediction combines and averages crop features and geometric predictions. (PDF p. 3).
- One output route uses predicted distance and orientation distributions in pyRosetta to make all-atom models. The other uses a final SE(3)-equivariant layer to predict backbone coordinates. (PDF p. 3).
- Complex prediction uses paired sequence alignments and predicts the chains together. The tested network was trained on monomeric proteins. (PDF p. 8).

## Applications

- RoseTTAFold models enabled molecular replacement for four crystallographic datasets that had resisted solution with available PDB models. (PDF p. 5).
- A predicted four-chain IL-12R/IL-12 complex fits the experimental cryo-EM density. The model suggests an additional role for the receptor N-terminal peptide in binding. (PDF p. 8).

## Optimization

- For proteins longer than 400 residues, the pyRosetta route requires 8 GB of GPU memory instead of 24 GB for the end-to-end route. It adds CPU structure-modeling work. (PDF p. 4).
- Repeated prediction with predicted templates and random MSA subsamples produces some better models. The accuracy predictor cannot reliably select them. (PDF p. 5).
- An experimental Perceiver approach updates seed MSAs of up to 100 sequences through cross-attention to thousands of extra sequences. Its initial results require more training for rigorous comparison. (PDF p. 5).

## Evaluation and testing

- CASP14 comparisons include three-track and two-track models with identical training sets, similar attention architectures, and similar inference operations. (PDF p. 4).
- CAMEO provides blind tests on structures submitted to the PDB. The reported test period covers 69 medium and hard targets from 15 May to 19 June 2021. (PDF p. 4).
- Complex tests use paired alignments for known structures with two or three chains and assess predictions with TM-score. (PDF p. 8).

## Results

- The three-track models outperform the listed CASP14 server and human-group comparators, including the BAKER group. They remain below AlphaFold2. (PDF p. 4).
- RoseTTAFold outperforms all other servers in the reported CAMEO test period. (PDF p. 4).
- After sequence and template search, backbone prediction for proteins shorter than 400 residues takes about 10 minutes on an RTX2080 GPU. (PDF p. 4).
- Many tested complex models achieve TM-score above 0.8. Complex prediction takes about 30 minutes on a 24 GB TITAN RTX GPU. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- GPU memory limits prevent direct training on large proteins and require sequence crops. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors suggest that incomplete optimization and missing side-chain information reduce end-to-end accuracy relative to the pyRosetta route. (PDF p. 4).
- The reported RoseTTAFold implementation uses only the top 1,000 MSA sequences because of memory limits. (PDF p. 5).
- The complex tests may contain training bias in individual monomer structures. The authors state that the complexes themselves were not in training. (PDF p. 8).
