## Executive summary

- The authors present sparse dynamic programming algorithms for computing the LCS from fragment matches. (PDF p. 1).
- The algorithm runs in O(M log M) time and O(M) space, where M is the number of fragment pairs. (PDF p. 23).

## Objectives

- Efficiently compute longest common subsequences on long strings when matching substrings are sparse. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Restricting dynamic programming evaluation to fragment boundaries avoids filling the full matrix. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- Time complexity is bounded by charging search tree operations to fragment endpoints. (PDF p. 23).

## Methods

- Balanced search trees maintain threshold vectors of non-dominated match points. (PDF p. 23).
- Points are processed in topological order, updating the sparse dynamic programming boundary in logarithmic time. (PDF p. 23).

## Applications

- Software duplication detection and molecular sequence comparison. (PDF p. 3).

## Optimization

- Balanced search tree lookups avoid redundant checks across dominated match fragments. (PDF p. 23).

## Evaluation and testing

Not stated in the extractable source pages.

## Results

- The algorithm strictly improves upon the classic Hunt-Szymanski algorithm for sparse fragment sets. (PDF p. 2).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Fragment weights must be less than the cost of an insertion plus a deletion to preserve correctness. (PDF p. 24).
