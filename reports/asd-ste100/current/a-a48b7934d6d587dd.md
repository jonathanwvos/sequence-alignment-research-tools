## Executive summary

- PLMSearch uses protein language model embeddings and Pfam clans to search for homologous proteins from sequence input. (PDF p. 6).
- PLMAlign adds local or global alignments for retrieved pairs. It uses residue embeddings to calculate substitution scores. (PDF p. 7).
- The authors report high remote homology recall and short search times on SCOPe40-test and Swiss-Prot. (PDF p. 4).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to improve sequence search sensitivity while retaining broad applicability and computational efficiency. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that training on structural similarity lets SS-predictor estimate useful similarity scores without structure input. (PDF p. 3).
- Pfam clan matching gives higher recall than Pfam family matching. The authors therefore prefer clans for initial filtering. (PDF p. 6).
- Large Pfam clans produce many irrelevant candidate pairs. The authors use SS-predictor to rank these candidates. (PDF p. 4).

## Analysis

- The remote homology analysis defines remote pairs by sequence identity below 0.3 and TM-score above 0.5. It compares recalled and missed pairs using these two measures. (PDF p. 4).
- An ablation test uses 110 queries with no detected Pfam domain. PLMSearch then gives the same results as SS-predictor because the clan filter retains all pairs. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors report stronger correlation with TM-score for SS-predictor than for direct embedding similarity measures. (PDF p. 4).

## Methods

- PfamScan identifies query domains. PfamClan retains targets with a shared clan, then PLMSearch ranks retained pairs by predicted similarity. Queries with no usable clan match retain all targets. (PDF p. 6).
- ESM-1b residue embeddings undergo average pooling. A bilinear projection predicts TM-scores. The final score equals cosine similarity above 0.995; otherwise, it equals predicted TM-score multiplied by cosine similarity. (PDF p. 7).
- SS-predictor training uses PyTorch 1.7.1, ADAM, mean squared error, batch size 100, learning rate 1e-6, and 200 epochs. TM-align supplies training targets from SCOPe40-train and CATHS40. ESM-1b parameters remain fixed. (PDF p. 9).
- PLMAlign calculates residue substitution scores with dot products. It uses Smith–Waterman or Needleman–Wunsch with a linear gap penalty. The main experiments use global alignment scores. (PDF p. 7).

## Applications

- The web server supports searches against Swiss-Prot and UniRef50, followed by PLMAlign alignment of the top 10 targets. (PDF p. 4).
- The pipeline also provides optional sequence-based Needleman–Wunsch alignment and structure-based TM-align for retrieved pairs. (PDF p. 6).

## Optimization

- Target embeddings are generated and loaded into memory in advance. This avoids repeated language model inference and disk loading during search. (PDF p. 4).
- Aligning only pairs with PLMSearch similarity above 0.3 reduces alignment time by nearly 16 times in the reported comparison, with comparable precision. (PDF p. 4).
- PLMAlign uses dot product scores and a linear gap penalty. The authors give its alignment complexity as O(mn), where m and n are sequence lengths. (PDF p. 7).

## Evaluation and testing

- SCOPe40-test contains 2,207 proteins and 4,870,849 all-versus-all pairs. The benchmark compares PLMSearch variants with five sequence search methods and seven structure search methods. (PDF p. 3).
- The Swiss-Prot test uses 100 queries and 430,140 targets, giving 43,014,000 pairs. Six methods were tested at this scale. (PDF p. 4).
- Metrics include AUPR, MAP, P@K, and a measure called AUROC in the paper. This AUROC is mean query sensitivity before the first false positive, excluding self-hits. Swiss-Prot positives require TM-score above 0.5. (PDF p. 9).
- Alignment evaluation uses Malidup and Malisam structural references. Malidup contains homologous domains within a chain; Malisam contains analogous motif pairs. (PDF p. 9).

## Results

- On SCOPe40-test, reported AUROC values for PLMSearch versus MMseqs2 are 0.928 versus 0.318 at family level, 0.826 versus 0.050 at superfamily level, and 0.438 versus 0.002 at fold level. (PDF p. 4).
- PLMSearch recalls 1,087 of 1,105 remote homology pairs. MMseqs2 recalls 183, and Foldseek recalls 934. (PDF p. 4).
- Reported SCOPe40-test search times are 4 seconds for PLMSearch, 10 seconds for SS-predictor, and 11,303 seconds for TM-align on the same 56-core server. (PDF p. 4).
- For queries without detected Pfam domains, PLMSearch achieves MAP 0.612, P@1 0.845, and P@10 0.712. PLMAlign reranking improves precision. (PDF p. 4).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Search timing excludes target preprocessing. The reported search speed depends on target embeddings prepared in advance. (PDF p. 4).
- PLMSearch predicts pair similarity but does not produce alignments. PLMAlign supplies this additional output. (PDF p. 6).
- SS-predictor focuses on global similarity. Its training lacks pairs with extremely high sequence identity, which motivates the cosine similarity rule. (PDF p. 7).
- The similarity threshold of 0.3 used before alignment causes a small recall decrease. (PDF p. 4).
