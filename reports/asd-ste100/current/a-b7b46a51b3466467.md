## Executive summary

- BWA maps short reads to a reference with BWT backward search. It allows mismatches and gaps and supports single-end and paired-end reads. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors report accuracy similar to MAQ with substantially shorter runtime. BWA also produces mapping qualities and SAM output. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to accelerate large-scale short-read mapping while supporting gaps that MAQ cannot handle in single-end reads. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- BWT search represents repeated reference substrings through shared trie paths. The authors identify this reuse as a main source of efficiency. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors argue that mapping counts alone do not measure accuracy. Counts must be assessed together with alignment errors or other evidence of correct placement. (PDF p. 6).

## Analysis

- Exact backward search counts matches in O(m) time for a read of length m. It updates suffix array intervals without storing the explicit prefix trie. (PDF p. 2).
- The recursive inexact algorithm finds all intervals within a specified edit limit. Practical BWA adds scoring and search restrictions, so this completeness statement applies to the theoretical algorithm. (PDF p. 3).
- The lower-bound array D prunes search states that cannot fit the remaining difference budget. A zero bound leaves a search exponential in the number of differences. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- Inexact search explores matches, mismatches, insertions, and deletions through suffix array intervals. A reverse-reference BWT computes D in linear time in read length. (PDF p. 3).
- BWA stores partial hits in a score-prioritized heap-like structure. It uses separate mismatch, gap-open, and gap-extension penalties and searches both read orientations. (PDF p. 3).
- Paired-end mapping sorts candidate coordinates and scans them to form pairs. Smith–Waterman alignment rescues unmapped reads whose mates map reliably. (PDF p. 4).
- For SOLiD reads, BWA searches a color-space reference index. Dynamic programming then decodes mapped reads into nucleotide sequences. (PDF p. 4).

## Applications

- BWA supports human resequencing with Illumina and SOLiD reads. (PDF p. 1).
- SAM output enables downstream sorting, visualization, SNP calling, and indel calling through SAMtools. (PDF p. 4).

## Optimization

- BWA stores occurrence counts every 128 positions and reconstructs intervening values from the BWT. (PDF p. 3).
- BWA stores suffix array values every 32 positions and reconstructs other values. Caching large intervals halves the reported pairing time. (PDF p. 4).
- Restricting a 32-base seed to two differences made simulated 70-base read alignment 2.5 times faster. The error rate rose from 0.08% to 0.11%. (PDF p. 3).
- By default, BWA skips suboptimal intervals for repetitive best hits. For a unique best hit with z differences, it searches only up to z + 1 differences. (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- wgsim generated one million read pairs at each length of 32, 70, and 125 bases. Simulations used 0.09% SNPs, 0.01% indels, and 2% uniform sequencing errors. (PDF p. 5).
- Comparisons included MAQ, SOAPv2, and Bowtie. Confident mappings used tool-specific uniqueness rules or mapping quality thresholds. Runtime used one 2.5 GHz Xeon E5420 core. (PDF p. 5).
- The real-data test mapped about 12.2 million pairs of 51-base NA12750 reads to human genome build 36. Pair consistency provided evidence of alignment accuracy. (PDF p. 5).
- A second real-data test added the chicken genome to the human reference. Reads placed on chicken sequences served as an error indicator. (PDF p. 6).

## Results

- For simulated 70-base paired-end reads, BWA took 1,619 seconds. It mapped 96.2% confidently, with 0.11% errors among confident mappings. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors report broadly similar simulated accuracy for BWA and MAQ. SOAPv2 was faster than BWA under the tested settings. (PDF p. 5).
- On real reads, BWA took 4.0 hours, compared with 94.9 hours for MAQ. BWA mapped 88.9% confidently, with 98.8% of confident mappings in consistent pairs. (PDF p. 6).
- BWA used 2.3 GB for single-end mapping and about 3 GB for paired-end mapping. Memory use did not depend on the read count. (PDF p. 5).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Runtime increases with the mismatch rate. Long reads with high sequencing error rates reduce performance. (PDF p. 6).
- BWA requires alignment of the complete read. Structural variation or reference misassembly can therefore prevent alignment of long reads. (PDF p. 6).
- BWA can overestimate mapping quality. In one simulation, Q60 mappings had an observed error rate of 7 × 10^-6 rather than 10^-6. (PDF p. 4).
- Real-data accuracy proxies have limits. Pair consistency assumes reliable mate information and rare structural variation. Hybrid-reference errors can be undercounted or overcounted. (PDF p. 6).
