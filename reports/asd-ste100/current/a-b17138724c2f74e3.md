## Executive summary

- The authors extend a quaternionic genetic-code representation to include chirality. Conjugate quaternions represent the two chiral systems. (PDF p. 1).
- The protein model combines integer amino acid type quaternions with real order quaternions. It constructs alpha-carbon traces for L-proteins and their D-protein mirror images. (PDF p. 11).
- The authors fit three small protein structures with particle swarm optimization and report alpha-carbon RMSDs below 0.17 Å. (PDF p. 13).

## Objectives

- Extend the earlier mathematical representation to include chirality while retaining the genetic-code structure. (PDF p. 2).
- Use amino acid quaternions to construct spatial coordinates for homochiral protein chains. (PDF p. 11).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that quaternion representations support protein modelling because quaternions can describe spatial rotations. (PDF p. 11).
- The proposed evolutionary diagrams assume that the D-base/L-amino-acid and L-base/D-amino-acid systems evolved independently. They also assume that code changes were largely independent of chirality. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- The specified set of eight norm-7 integer quaternions is divided into two sets of four. Corresponding elements are conjugates and represent the two base systems. (PDF p. 2).
- The amino acid assignments satisfy conjugacy between corresponding L and D type quaternions. Glycine has the same real quaternion in both lists. (PDF p. 11).
- The authors derive a transformation of order quaternions that reflects generated coordinates across the plane x = 0. The resulting L and D chains are mirror images. (PDF p. 13).

## Methods

- Each amino acid receives an integer type quaternion and a real order quaternion. The type quaternion derives from the codon representation. (PDF p. 2).
- For each residue, the algorithm sums order quaternions weighted by dot products between its type quaternion and all other type quaternions. It normalizes this sum to obtain a rotation quaternion. (PDF p. 13).
- The rotation quaternion maps the north-pole unit vector to a point on a sphere. A subsequent iterative expansion produces the alpha-carbon chain. (PDF p. 13).

## Applications

- The representation models the standard genetic code and a hypothetical code with the opposite chiral combination. It preserves the degeneracy of the corresponding codes. (PDF p. 11).
- The authors apply the folding procedure to glucagon, osk1 toxin, and a type III antifreeze protein. (PDF p. 13).

## Optimization

- Particle swarm optimization fits the unknown real order quaternions. The fitness measure is alpha-carbon RMSD against reference coordinates, calculated with an implementation of the Kabsch algorithm. (PDF p. 13).
- The revised model replaces ordinary quaternion products with dot products. The authors state that this supports a common order-quaternion construction for L and D proteins and reduces the number of fitting solutions. (PDF p. 15).

## Evaluation and testing

- The examples are 1GCN with 29 amino acids, 2CK5 with 31, and 1HG7 with 66. L-protein references come from the PDB. D-protein references are reflections of those structures. (PDF p. 13).
- Interpretation: these RMSDs measure fits to reference structures used by the optimizer. The described experiment does not establish prediction accuracy on unseen structures. (PDF p. 13).

## Results

- Reported L-protein RMSDs are 0.103 Å for 1GCN, 0.091 Å for 2CK5, and 0.163 Å for 1HG7. (PDF p. 13).
- The L and D constructions produce mirror-image structures through related order quaternions. (PDF p. 15).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The implemented structural target is the alpha-carbon trace. The procedure does not directly determine all atomic coordinates. (PDF p. 11).
- D-protein targets are assumed mirror images of experimental L-protein structures. They are not independent experimental D-protein references. (PDF p. 11).
- The authors leave open whether one set of order quaternions can describe the folding of all proteins. (PDF p. 15).
