## Executive summary

- pLM-BLAST detects protein homology through direct comparison of ProtT5 residue embeddings. It produces local and global alignments. (PDF p. 2).
- On the hard homology benchmark, the authors report accuracy similar to HHsearch. EBA performs best. (PDF p. 5).
- In the database search benchmark, pLM-BLAST is the second-fastest method. The authors report average speeds about twice those of HHsearch and three times those of EBA. (PDF p. 7).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to detect distant protein homology and produce precise alignments through comparisons of single-sequence representations. (PDF p. 2).
- Local alignment aims to detect conserved subdomain fragments that can indicate distant evolutionary relationships. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that raw embeddings can support homology detection without training a specialized model on labelled protein pairs. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors argue that averaging residue embeddings into one protein vector loses information needed to detect local similarity. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors propose that some high-scoring links across ECOD groups could indicate previously unrecognized homology. They present this as a hypothesis. (PDF p. 7).

## Analysis

- Residue similarity depends on sequence context because scores come from embeddings rather than a fixed substitution matrix. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors attribute EBA's stronger homology results to signal enhancement. They also note that domain benchmarks can favor global alignment. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors suggest that raw embeddings retain useful homology signals that TM-Vec's predicted structural similarity does not preserve as effectively in this benchmark. (PDF p. 5).

## Methods

- The method normalizes each residue embedding by its Euclidean norm. Pairwise cosine similarities form a sequence-specific substitution matrix. (PDF p. 2).
- Local scoring uses a modified Smith-Waterman procedure without gap penalties or zero truncation. Traceback starts from sequence boundaries to generate multiple candidate paths. (PDF p. 2).
- A moving average scans candidate paths for high-scoring segments. The default cutoff is twice the average standard deviation of the substitution matrix. (PDF p. 3).
- Global mode uses Needleman-Wunsch with the embedding-based substitution matrix and a zero gap penalty. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The tool supports custom databases and batch searches as a standalone package. Its web server searches precomputed databases. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors use local matches to investigate possible shared ancestral fragments across protein folds, including a beta-hairpin in cupredoxin and immunoglobulin domains. (PDF p. 7).

## Optimization

- A prefilter compares embedding windows by cosine similarity. The default window covers 30 residues and all 1024 ProtT5 embedding dimensions. (PDF p. 5).
- Only sequence pairs with a window similarity above a user-set threshold proceed to alignment. The speed benchmark uses a cutoff at the 90th percentile. (PDF p. 5).

## Evaluation and testing

- The hard ECOD set contains 1500 domains from 300 H-groups, with no pair above 30% sequence identity. The easy set contains 1032 domains in 103 clusters, targeting 50%–70% similarity. (PDF p. 3).
- For the hard set, same-H-group matches are positives. Matches across H-groups within one X-group are neutral. Matches across X-groups are negatives. Missing scores are set to zero for precision-recall calculations. (PDF p. 3).
- Alignment quality uses 444 structure-based reference alignments. The reported metrics are mean residue-pair precision and sensitivity. (PDF p. 5).
- Speed tests use five queries against ECOD70 on a six-core CPU and one GPU. Timings include query preparation and database search. Four runs are averaged. (PDF p. 5).

## Results

- On the hard benchmark, EBA performs best. HHsearch and pLM-BLAST show similar accuracy, while TM-Vec and BLAST perform worse. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors report accuracy comparable to HHsearch for both highly similar and strongly divergent sequences. (PDF p. 1).
- Local pLM-BLAST search times range from 54 seconds to 2 minutes 30 seconds across five queries. TM-Vec takes 15 seconds for each query. (PDF p. 7).
- HHsearch is faster than pLM-BLAST for the 55-residue coiled-coil query: 38 seconds versus 54 seconds. (PDF p. 7).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Searching redundant databases with millions of sequences remains computationally costly. (PDF p. 7).
- Local mode typically produces precise but shorter alignments than HHsearch. The evolutionary meaning of these shorter regions requires further study. (PDF p. 7).
- The authors caution that benchmark homology labels reflect hypotheses. HHsearch-informed reference relationships may omit homology detectable by embedding methods. (PDF p. 7).
- TM-Vec alignment quality was not evaluated because the authors could not run the required DeepBLAST model. (PDF p. 5).
