## Executive summary

- NGS-Logistics queries sequence variants across multiple centers without transferring raw genomic data to the user. (PDF p. 3).
- Users receive detailed results for authorized samples and summary information for other samples. Pseudonymous identifiers support requests for further access. (PDF p. 10).
- A demonstration analyzed approximately 1,500 exome samples held at KU Leuven and UZ Leuven. (PDF p. 5).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to support population-level mutation questions and detailed analysis of individual genomic positions across distributed data sources. (PDF p. 3).
- They aim to balance useful analysis with data confidentiality and controlled access to personal genomic data. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors prefer queries on aligned reads because variant calls can change as calling methods evolve. (PDF p. 2).
- They argue that uniform analysis of BAM files improves comparability across samples. Reference genotyping also distinguishes homozygous reference positions from positions without coverage. (PDF p. 9).
- They argue that analysis without copying remote datasets can reduce transfer time and local storage costs. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- Remote hosts process queries and return results to the main server. The main server applies privacy restrictions before users view results. (PDF p. 3).
- In the SMARCA2 example, rs281875187 is absent from the active dataset but occurs in one heterozygous sample in the whole population. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors interpret two variants shared in a heterozygous state by the same 22 individuals as being on the same allele. They then reject the variant combination as the disease cause in three patients. (PDF p. 8).

## Methods

- The application has administration, query manager and user interface modules. Its three tiers handle presentation, application processing and data management. (PDF p. 3).
- Administrators manage users, sample assignments and access rights. They can restrict query types and query volume. (PDF p. 3).
- Remote centers query BAM files with GATK and export results in a VCF-like format. Users can select HaplotypeCaller or UnifiedGenotyper. (PDF p. 5).
- Point queries specify a chromosome and position. Area queries specify a genomic interval or gene name. Each request receives an identifier for status tracking. (PDF p. 5).

## Applications

- Users can search for rare variants in other samples and contact the responsible principal investigator to request further access. (PDF p. 5).
- Point queries support investigation of variant occurrence and coverage at a selected genomic position. (PDF p. 3).

## Optimization

- Queries execute where the data are stored. This design avoids exchange of raw data between centers and the main system. (PDF p. 3).

## Evaluation and testing

- The demonstration uses approximately 1,500 exome samples across two centers and illustrates area and point queries. (PDF p. 5).
- The SMARCA2 use case tests retrieval of mutations by sample and retrieval of samples by mutation. (PDF p. 5).
- For the two-position example, variants with Phred-scaled genotype likelihood below 70 are filtered before polymorphic samples are counted. (PDF p. 8).
- The application was tested in Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox and Safari. (PDF p. 10).

## Results

- The authors report successful analysis of samples held at both demonstration centers. (PDF p. 5).
- The SMARCA2 query identifies a heterozygous variant carrier outside the active dataset through whole-population results. (PDF p. 5).
- The two-position example identifies the same 22 heterozygous carriers for both variants. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Query duration cannot be estimated because each center has different resources and workloads. The interface reports progress by center. (PDF p. 5).
- Further sample access requires review of consent and applicable institutional requirements. The authors state that this process cannot be automated. (PDF p. 9).
- The whole population mixes cases and controls, and clinical information is not supported. These limits prevent association scoring. (PDF p. 9).
- Samples from the same patient can occur at different centers without a shared identity record. Such duplication can distort population statistics. (PDF p. 9).
