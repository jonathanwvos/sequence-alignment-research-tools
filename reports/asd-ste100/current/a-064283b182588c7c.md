## Executive summary

- The paper seeks a longest string that is a subsequence of every X input and a substring of every Y input. (PDF p. 1).
- Algorithm A uses a multidimensional table. The author states time and space bounds equal to the product of all input lengths. (PDF p. 17).

## Objectives

- The author extends the two-string subsequence-and-substring problem to s subsequence inputs and t substring inputs. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- A nonempty common suffix requires the final symbols of all Y prefixes to match. If these symbols differ, the corresponding state is empty. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- Each state represents a longest string that is a subsequence of the selected X prefixes and a suffix of every selected Y prefix. (PDF p. 3).
- The author attributes correctness to Claims 1–5 and states O(m1 × … × ms × n1 × … × nt) time and space complexity. (PDF p. 17).
- Interpretation: the stated product bound grows rapidly as the number of input strings increases. (PDF p. 17).

## Methods

- Nested loops visit combinations of X and Y prefix endpoints. The table entry is zero when the Y endpoint symbols differ. (PDF p. 16).
- When all endpoint symbols match, the recurrence decrements every index and adds one to the previous length. (PDF p. 16).
- When the common Y symbol differs from X endpoint symbols, the recurrence decrements the mismatching X indices and retains the Y indices. (PDF p. 16).
- The algorithm tracks the largest table value and its endpoint in Y1. It returns a substring of Y1 from that information. (PDF p. 17).

## Applications

- The introduction identifies biological sequence similarity as an application of conventional longest common subsequence and substring lengths. (PDF p. 2).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Interpretation: the pseudocode has inconsistent notation. It mixes loop indices θ and τ with i and j, and uses x[t, τt] when defining the common Y symbol. (PDF p. 16).
- Interpretation: the return statement leaves the endpoint convention unclear. Under inclusive indexing, a substring of maxLength ending at lastIndexOnY1 starts at lastIndexOnY1 − maxLength + 1. (PDF p. 17).
