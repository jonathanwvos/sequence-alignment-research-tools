## Executive summary

- The authors map DNA codons to stabilizing free energies of antiparallel codon–complementary codon duplexes. (PDF p. 2).
- They report correlations between duplex stability, codon usage frequency, and codons for ancient amino acids. (PDF p. 7).
- They propose that differential stability helped shape genetic code evolution through linked thermodynamic cycles. (PDF p. 7).

## Objectives

- The authors seek a thermodynamic contribution to genetic code evolution and molecular Darwinism. (PDF p. 2).
- They propose genome energy maps to connect sequence, structure, and biological function. (PDF p. 7).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that trimeric duplexes provide sufficient coding diversity and stability. They suggest that longer, more stable interactions could reduce translation rates. (PDF p. 2).
- They hypothesize that stable genetic domains can support persistence of traits alongside selection for phenotypic advantages. (PDF p. 2).
- They propose that stable prebiotic codons produced the remaining code through transition and transversion mutations influenced by interlocking energy cycles. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- The energy map represents 64 DNA codons as 32 complementary trimeric duplexes. The annotated code is the human mitochondrial code. (PDF p. 3).
- The hypercube connects eight codon cubes. Transition mutations connect codons within a cube, and transversion mutations connect different cubes. (PDF p. 6).
- Interpretation: The reported correlations support the proposed thermodynamic scenario, but do not establish a historical evolutionary pathway. The authors describe reconstruction of earlier pathways as speculation. (PDF p. 7).

## Methods

- Duplex free energies use previously measured calorimetric nearest-neighbor parameters. (PDF p. 2).
- The calculation averages hypothetical terminal base pairs and stacks to reduce end effects. This correction reduces stability variation and the influence of the central base. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors compare stability with amino acid coding properties and examine mutation paths from stable codon groups. (PDF p. 6).

## Applications

- The authors propose comprehensive genome energy maps to examine known functional domains and regions with unknown functions. (PDF p. 7).
- They suggest further analysis of DNA coding and signal domains, with parallel energy analysis for RNA. (PDF p. 6).

## Optimization

- The authors propose that evolution can favor relative stability and reaction rates alongside functional advantages. This is an evolutionary hypothesis. (PDF p. 1).

## Evaluation and testing

- The authors test robustness by reversing codon interaction polarity to form parallel couplets. They compare energy spectra, stability ranks, and mutation-associated energy changes. (PDF p. 6).
- They assess the end-effect correction. Removing it preserves stability rank order and general correlations in their selected parameter dataset. (PDF p. 3).
- They compare conclusions with alternative nearest-neighbor datasets. Some numerical values and stability ranks differ. (PDF p. 3).

## Results

- Reported stabilizing free energy values range from 2.8 kcal per mole triplet for ATA/TAT to 5.8 for GCG/CGC. (PDF p. 3).
- Stable codon families encode several amino acids considered ancient. The authors report that the remaining 40 codons can arise from the 24 stable codons through at most three transition mutations. (PDF p. 6).
- Parallel couplets have different energy spectra, stability rankings, and mutation energy changes from antiparallel couplets. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors report correlations between antiparallel duplex formation free energies and codon usage frequencies. (PDF p. 7).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The proposed differences between coding-domain and signal-domain energy profiles require further validation. (PDF p. 6).
- Alternative nearest-neighbor datasets change some numerical values and stability ranks. The authors recommend measurements under different counterion and cation conditions. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors state that surviving evolutionary outcomes make earlier precursors and pathways difficult to determine with certainty. (PDF p. 7).
