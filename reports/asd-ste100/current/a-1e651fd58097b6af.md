## Executive summary

- DHR retrieves protein homologs through learned sequence embeddings and dot-product similarity. (PDF p. 2).
- DHR supplies candidate sequences to JackHMMER, which constructs the MSA used for downstream structure prediction. (PDF p. 3).

## Objectives

- Detect remote protein homologs with high sensitivity and low search time. (PDF p. 2).
- Reduce MSA construction time and supply additional homologs for protein structure prediction. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that learned embeddings can retain structural and evolutionary information that sequence similarity searches miss. (PDF p. 2).
- Separate query and candidate encoders allow the same sequence to receive different representations for its two search roles. (PDF p. 2).
- Retrieval avoids pairwise alignment over the full database. JackHMMER then operates on the smaller retrieved set. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- Across SCOPe queries, average TM-score decreases and average structural RMSD increases as DHR rank becomes worse. The authors interpret this as evidence of structural information in the embeddings. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors distinguish MSA diversity from alignment quality. They assess downstream structure prediction because Meff primarily measures diversity. (PDF p. 7).
- Average MSA log Meff and average structure TM-score increase together across tested configurations. The authors caution that this relation does not explain every protein case. (PDF p. 7).

## Methods

- Both encoders start from ESM-1b parameters. The model uses the first output vector as a fixed-length sequence representation. (PDF p. 14).
- Training samples positive query–homolog pairs from MSAs. Other candidates in the batch act as negatives. A softmax contrastive loss favors the paired candidate. (PDF p. 14).
- Candidate embeddings are computed offline and stored. FAISS retrieves the top-K candidates by dot-product similarity to the query embedding. (PDF p. 14).
- JackHMMER builds MSAs from the retrieved candidates with AF2 search settings. The experiments vary the number of search iterations. (PDF p. 14).

## Applications

- DHR supports homolog detection and MSA generation for AF2 structure prediction. (PDF p. 3).
- DHR-derived MSAs can be merged with AF2 or MMseqs2 MSAs to increase sequence diversity and improve structure predictions. (PDF p. 12).

## Optimization

- Offline database encoding removes repeated candidate-encoding work from individual searches. Dot products permit fast FAISS retrieval. (PDF p. 14).
- In-batch negatives reduce negative-sampling work. Cross-batch negatives extend the effective batch size to 544. (PDF p. 14).
- The MSA experiments test retrieval sizes of 100,000, 200,000, 400,000, and one million sequences, with one to three JackHMMER iterations. (PDF p. 5).
- The distillation procedure reruns JackHMMER on its previous hit set to obtain another MSA. (PDF p. 15).

## Evaluation and testing

- SCOPe evaluation uses 5065 family queries and 2065 superfamily queries. Metrics include top-200 recall and AUC before the first false positive. (PDF p. 5).
- Comparators include PSI-BLAST, MMseqs2, DIAMOND, HMMER, and Foldseek. Hard cases have MMseqs2 sensitivity below 0.34. (PDF p. 4).
- The CASP13DM structure test uses UniRef90 without template search. It selects the highest-confidence result from five AF2 models and evaluates TM-score and global lDDT. (PDF p. 7).
- The New-Protein test contains 650 proteins from structures released during 2022. A 70% similarity filter excludes proteins represented in the training and search sets. (PDF p. 12).

## Results

- DHR achieves about 93% top-200 family sensitivity on SCOPe, compared with 91% for HMMER. Its mean sensitivity exceeds 80% on the defined hard cases. (PDF p. 4).
- DHR achieves AUC-1FP scores of about 89% at family level and 80% at superfamily level. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors report up to a 93-fold speed increase for DHR plus JackHMMER MSA construction relative to the compared default pipeline. (PDF p. 2).
- Combining DHR and MMseqs2 MSAs gives the highest reported prediction accuracy on the 650 New-Protein targets, with a TM-score of 0.909. (PDF p. 12).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors report poorer retrieval for long sequences and attribute this to ESM constraints. The stated threshold is 1000 nt, although the task concerns proteins; the unit is unclear. (PDF p. 12).
- Training-data construction is described inconsistently. This page first specifies HHblits, but later attributes MSA construction to JackHMMER. (PDF p. 14).
- Database encoding is time consuming for millions of sequences, although it can be completed offline and reused. (PDF p. 14).
- A merged MSA worsens RMSD by 11 Å on T0990-D2. The authors propose an encoding or pipeline effect, but do not establish the cause. (PDF p. 7).
