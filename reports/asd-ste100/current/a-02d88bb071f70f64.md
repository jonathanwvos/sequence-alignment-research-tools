## Executive summary

- The authors develop SAUTE and SAUTE_PROT to assemble target nucleotide and protein sequences directly from short reads. (PDF p. 2).
- The tool uses target sequences as seeds to construct localized de Bruijn graphs without whole-genome assembly. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- Recover targeted gene sequences, isoforms, and antimicrobial resistance determinants from short-read libraries. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- Targeted local assembly avoids the computational burden and misassemblies of de novo whole-genome assembly. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- Assembly recovery rates and chimera counts are evaluated across simulated and empirical read sets. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- Reads matching target sequences are harvested using k-mer lookups and exact suffix matching. (PDF p. 5).
- Local assembly graphs are resolved to output full-length target contigs and sequence variants. (PDF p. 18).

## Applications

- Antimicrobial resistance gene identification and isoform discovery in Drosophila and human datasets. (PDF p. 16).

## Optimization

Not stated in the extractable source pages.

## Evaluation and testing

- SAUTE is evaluated against SKESA and SPAligner across 763 microbial read sets from FDA-ARGOS. (PDF p. 3).

## Results

- SAUTE recovers higher percentages of complete target genes than standard de novo assemblers. (PDF p. 17).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Assembly quality degrades in regions with very low coverage or extreme divergence from target references. (PDF p. 20).
