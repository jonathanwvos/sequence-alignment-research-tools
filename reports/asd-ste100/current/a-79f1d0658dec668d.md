## Executive summary

- The authors compare hexameric glutamate dehydrogenases from P. furiosus and T. litoralis to identify structural causes of thermostability above 100°C. (PDF p. 1).
- The combined T138E/D167T substitutions increased T. litoralis enzyme stability at 104°C by about fourfold. Each single substitution reduced stability. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors conclude that ion-pair networks can improve thermostability. They stress that the local structural environment controls the effect of a residue change. (PDF p. 4).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to test a causal link between intersubunit ion-pair networks and high thermostability by modifying the less stable T. litoralis enzyme. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors propose that loss of the ion-pair network around residue 138 contributes to the lower stability of T. litoralis GluDH. (PDF p. 2).
- They argue that combinations of mutations may need screening. A beneficial combination can contain single mutations that reduce stability. (PDF p. 5).

## Analysis

- The mature P. furiosus and T. litoralis sequences contain 419 and 418 amino acids. The authors report 87% identity and an unambiguous alignment. (PDF p. 2).
- The alignment marks intersubunit contacts below 3.7 Å in the P. furiosus hexamer. Both selected mutation sites occur at intra-trimer subunit contacts. (PDF p. 3).
- Sequence comparison identified replacement of Glu-138 by Thr in T. litoralis. The authors associate this change with loss of three ion pairs. (PDF p. 2).
- Structural comparison identified small shifts near residues 138 and 164–166, with partial disorder of Lys-166. This led the authors to examine residue 167. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- The authors used homology-based modeling with the P. furiosus crystal structure to identify differences in ion-pair networks. (PDF p. 2).
- They compared the T. litoralis structure at 2.5 Å resolution with the P. furiosus structure. Domain comparisons gave RMSD values of 0.38 Å and 0.55 Å. (PDF p. 2).
- They made T138E, D167T, and T138E/D167T variants by site-directed mutagenesis. Mutated genes were sequenced and expressed in E. coli BL21. (PDF p. 2).
- Recombinant enzymes were purified by heat treatment, anion exchange chromatography, and affinity chromatography. Purity was assessed with SDS/PAGE. (PDF p. 2).

## Applications

- The authors propose comparative sequence and structural analysis as a basis for rational design of more stable multisubunit enzymes. (PDF p. 5).
- They suggest that ion-pair modification could adapt hyperstable enzymes for industrial biocatalysis while retaining catalytic properties. (PDF p. 5).

## Optimization

- The authors combined T138E with D167T to modify the local ion-pair environment. The double variant raised the melting temperature by 8°C relative to T138E alone. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors associate successful stabilization with preservation of the local net charge found in P. furiosus GluDH. (PDF p. 4).

## Evaluation and testing

- Thermostability tests used 1.0 mg/ml protein in 700 mM KCl and 50 mM diglycine at pH 7.0. Incubation was at 104°C. Residual activity determined enzyme half-life. (PDF p. 2).
- Differential scanning microcalorimetry used a DASM4 instrument from 40°C to 130°C at 1°C/min. Control scan rates from 0.5 to 2°C/min had little effect on the transitions. (PDF p. 2).
- Catalytic activity was measured through glutamate-dependent reduction of NADP+ at 80°C. (PDF p. 2).

## Results

- At 104°C, P. furiosus GluDH had a half-life of 4.6 hours. T. litoralis wild-type GluDH had a half-life of 0.3 hours. Their melting temperatures were 114.5°C and 109.0°C. (PDF p. 3).
- The double mutant had a half-life of 1.1 hours at 104°C and a melting temperature of 111.5°C. (PDF p. 3).
- T138E had a half-life below 0.01 hours and a melting temperature of 103.5°C. D167T had a reported half-life of 0.3 hours and a melting temperature of 108.5°C. (PDF p. 3).
- Mutant and wild-type T. litoralis enzymes had the same reported specific activity of 150 units/mg at 80°C. (PDF p. 3).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The structural data show static molecules in crystal lattices at temperatures well below normal enzyme operating temperatures. The authors state that temperature-dependent conformational changes remain poorly understood. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors could not exclude an unfavorable electrostatic interaction involving Asp-167 as a cause of T138E destabilization. (PDF p. 4).
- Both single mutations reduced stability. The authors state that rational design of ion-pair interactions is difficult without comparative sequence and structural information. (PDF p. 5).
