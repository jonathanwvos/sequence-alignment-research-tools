## Executive summary

- The authors analyze a general evolutionary algorithm framework for the longest common subsequence problem. The framework uses the coding of Julstrom and Hinkemeyer and three fitness functions. (PDF p. 16).
- They prove exponential lower bounds on expected optimization time. The bounds apply even to instances with two binary sequences. (PDF p. 16).

## Objectives

- The study seeks theoretical runtime guarantees for evolutionary algorithms after earlier studies reported good empirical performance on LCS. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors use specific difficult instances to assess worst-case performance. They state that the results can also give large average-case lower bounds under a uniform distribution over inputs of equal length. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors argue that useful future results should identify standard evolutionary algorithms that perform well without extensive tuning for LCS. (PDF p. 16).

## Analysis

- Theorem 7 bounds the probability of finding an optimum within t fitness evaluations by t·exp(−Ω(n)). It covers all crossover probabilities and polynomial parent and offspring population sizes. (PDF p. 15).
- The restricted construction uses four letters and limits each uninterrupted run of one letter to constant length. These restrictions do not remove the worst-case difficulty. (PDF p. 15).

## Methods

- The framework combines elitist plus-selection, uniform crossover, and standard bit mutation. Population sizes and crossover probability define different algorithm instances. (PDF p. 2).
- The framework includes the (1+1) evolutionary algorithm and steady-state genetic algorithms. The analysis considers the fitness functions fJH, fMAX, and fLCS. (PDF p. 15).

## Applications

- The authors identify bioinformatics and pattern finding in text as applications of LCS. (PDF p. 1).

## Optimization

- The optimization target is a longest subsequence shared by all input sequences. The analysis also considers solutions with a specified approximation factor. (PDF p. 2).

## Evaluation and testing

- The evaluation uses mathematical runtime lower bounds on constructed LCS instances. (PDF p. 3).
- The restricted-instance tests cover two binary sequences and four-letter sequences with constant-length runs of identical letters. (PDF p. 16).

## Results

- Worst-case expected optimization time is exponential for the analyzed algorithm class and coding. (PDF p. 16).
- The worst-case runtime remains exponential when the target is a (2−ε)-approximation instead of an optimum. (PDF p. 16).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors do not identify nontrivial instance classes with polynomial expected runtime. They also provide no positive approximation result. (PDF p. 16).
- The authors state that earlier empirical results cannot be reproduced from the available documentation. Even the successfully solved instances are unclear. (PDF p. 16).
