## Executive summary

- AlphaFold predicts residue distance distributions from sequence and multiple sequence alignment features. These distributions define a protein-specific structural potential. (PDF p. 2).
- The described system optimizes backbone torsion angles with L-BFGS and repeated initializations to generate structures. (PDF p. 3).
- CASP13 submissions combined several systems that shared neural-network distance predictions. They were not all produced by the described gradient descent system. (PDF p. 8).

## Objectives

- Predict accurate protein structures by learning residue distances and minimizing the resulting potential. (PDF p. 2).
- Optimize whole protein chains without first assigning domain boundaries. (PDF p. 8).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that distance distributions provide more structural information and a richer training signal than binary contact predictions. (PDF p. 2).
- They argue that a differentiable learned potential permits structure generation with limited sampling. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- Predicted distance accuracy correlates with final structure TM score. Removing the distance potential reduces the reported TM score to 0.266. (PDF p. 3).
- Reducing distograms below six bins lowers accuracy. Removing the torsion term, reference correction, or steric term has smaller effects. (PDF p. 3).
- Integrated Gradients identifies input residue pairs that affect distance predictions. The authors interpret the structured attribution patterns as evidence of information transfer between related residues. (PDF p. 8).

## Methods

- HHblits searches Uniclust30 and constructs MSAs. Input features include sequence profiles, gaps, deletions, and regularized Potts-model coupling parameters. PSI-BLAST supplies additional profiles. (PDF p. 6).
- A two-dimensional convolutional network with 220 residual blocks predicts 64 distance bins across 2–22 Å. Dilations cycle through 1, 2, 4, and 8. (PDF p. 6).
- Splines fitted to negative log distance probabilities form the distance potential. A sequence-independent reference correction, a torsion potential, and a Rosetta steric term complete the objective. (PDF p. 3).
- Full-chain predictions combine distograms from full-sequence and sliding-window MSAs. Window sizes are 64, 128, and 256 residues, and averaging weights depend on MSA sequence counts. (PDF p. 8).

## Applications

- The authors report improved functional interpretation, protein interface prediction, binding pocket prediction, and molecular replacement from the predicted structures. (PDF p. 8).

## Optimization

- L-BFGS minimizes the potential over backbone torsion angles. Initial angles are sampled from predicted distributions, and noisy restarts perturb low-potential structures. (PDF p. 3).
- Training and inference use 64 × 64 crops to limit memory use and reduce overfitting. Off-diagonal crops capture interactions between distant sequence regions. (PDF p. 6).
- MSA augmentation samples half the aligned sequences before feature calculation. Ten samples per domain help training on shallow alignments. (PDF p. 6).
- The network uses synchronized stochastic gradient descent with cross-entropy loss. Training takes about five days for 600,000 steps. (PDF p. 6).

## Evaluation and testing

- CASP13 was a blind assessment with 98 groups, 84 target sequences, and 104 scored domains. Groups could submit up to five structures per target. (PDF p. 1).
- The data split contains 29,427 training domains and 1,820 test domains. Homologous superfamilies stay within one partition. A 377-domain subset supplies the reported internal tests. (PDF p. 6).
- Early stopping uses 27 CASP11 free-modelling domains. Model selection uses 27 CASP12 free-modelling domains. (PDF p. 6).
- Contact evaluation converts distograms to contact probabilities by summing probability below 8 Å. Comparisons use submissions from groups 032 and 498. (PDF p. 6).

## Results

- AlphaFold achieved TM scores of at least 0.7 for 24 of 43 FM and FM/TBM domains. The next-best method achieved this threshold for 14 domains. (PDF p. 1).
- The best-of-five summed z-score for the FM category was 52.8, compared with 36.6 for the next group. (PDF p. 1).
- Noisy restarts increased mean test-set TM score from 0.636 to 0.641. Final Rosetta relaxation added an average of 0.007. (PDF p. 3).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors state that accuracy on the internal test subset is higher than on CASP13 targets. (PDF p. 6).
- CASP13 results include fragment assembly and gradient descent systems. The submission procedure changed during the assessment. (PDF p. 8).
- Free-modelling accuracy remains below template-based modelling accuracy. The authors state that difficult predicted structures cannot reliably support detailed structural interpretation. (PDF p. 8).

Interpretation of distogram neural network. We have shown that the deep distance prediction neural network achieves high accuracy, but we would like to understand how the network arrives at its dis- tance predictions and—in particular—to understand how the inputs to the model affect the final prediction. This might improve our un- derstanding of the folding mechanisms or suggest improvements to the model. However, deep neural networks are complex nonlinear functions of their inputs, and so this attribution problem is difficult, under-specified and an on-going topic of research. Even so, there are a number of methods for such analysis: here we apply Integrated Gradients49 to our trained distogram network to indicate the location of input features that affect the network’s predictions of a particular distance.
