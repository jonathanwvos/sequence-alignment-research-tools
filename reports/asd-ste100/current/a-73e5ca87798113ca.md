## Executive summary

- The authors developed a BOINC system that distributes NGS read alignment across client computers. It supports BWA, Novoalign, Mosaik, and SHRiMP. (PDF p. 1).
- Novoalign tests with four clients gave an average speed-up of 3.550. The authors also tested parameter selection and compared NGS expression with microarray expression. (PDF p. 4).

## Objectives

- The main objective is to reduce alignment computation time through distributed computing. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors aim to select alignment parameters for different sequencing platforms and compare RNA-Seq expression with microarray expression. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that reads can be aligned independently after the input file is split. This permits parallel execution of existing aligners. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors argue that sequencing platforms require different alignment parameters because read lengths and error profiles differ. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- Post-alignment analysis counts ambiguous and unambiguous reads. Ambiguous reads map to multiple reference locations. Unambiguous reads map to one location. (PDF p. 3).
- Parameter analysis separates uniquely correct mappings, uniquely incorrect mappings, multiple hits, and unmapped reads. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors attribute the difference between the observed speed-up and the ideal factor of four to data processing and distribution overhead. (PDF p. 4).

## Methods

- The system has a web interface, a BOINC server, and client computers. A SQL database stores input file references, aligner settings, and job status. (PDF p. 2).
- A work generator splits reads into files of a user-specified size. Each work unit contains a read file and a parameter file. The client runs the selected aligner through system calls. (PDF p. 2).
- A validator checks result format. An assimilator concatenates results from all work units for a job. (PDF p. 2).
- The expression comparison uses GEO dataset GSE22004 and a human RefSeq reference. Probe alignment and filtering establish one-to-one probe-to-gene mappings. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The system supports parameter experiments with simulated Illumina, Helicos, Roche, and ABI reads. The simulations use platform-specific lengths and error profiles. (PDF p. 3).
- The biological case study compares Illumina gene read counts with Affymetrix expression for HeLa cells transfected with miR-155 duplex at 12 hours. (PDF p. 5).

## Optimization

- The authors first isolate parameters that strongly affect accuracy. They then optimize the remaining parameters together. (PDF p. 4).
- For Novoalign, they vary the threshold first. They assume that it has little interaction with gap open and gap extension penalties. Mapping statistics select a threshold of 120. (PDF p. 4).
- They then vary gap penalties together around default values of 40 and 15. The step sizes are three for gap open and one for gap extension. The reported optimum combines a high extension penalty with a low open penalty. (PDF p. 4).

## Evaluation and testing

- The timing benchmark compares one machine with four BOINC clients for two Novoalign parameter sets. It uses RefSeq gene sequences and GEO reads from SRR057531. (PDF p. 4).
- The expression comparison includes 731 genes that satisfy the probe-to-gene filtering criteria. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors use expression correlation as evidence that distributed data transfer and result collection work correctly. This is an indirect validation measure in my interpretation. (PDF p. 5).

## Results

- The two timing runs decrease from 1770.5 to 505.8 seconds and from 5941 to 1650 seconds. Their speed-up factors are 3.500 and 3.600. (PDF p. 4).
- For the tested Novoalign settings, the authors report no appreciable effect of parameter choice on the speed-up factor. (PDF p. 4).
- The expression correlation coefficient increases from 0.71595 at threshold 80 to 0.7503 at threshold 120. (PDF p. 5).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The timing test uses only four clients. Similar timing behavior for the other aligners is reported, but their results are not shown. (PDF p. 4).
- The parameter procedure does not guarantee equal accuracy across aligners. Final accuracy depends on the sequence data and alignment software. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors acknowledge known limitations of microarrays. They use microarray expression only as a comparison reference. (PDF p. 5).
