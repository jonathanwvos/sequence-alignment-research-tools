## Executive summary

- Altschul proposes multiple-alignment gap costs that share a rationale with the substitution costs. (PDF p. 1).
- Quasi-natural gap costs approximate natural gap costs while reducing the alignment history needed by dynamic programming. (PDF p. 12).

## Objectives

- The objective is to extend useful pairwise gap costs to multiple alignments while keeping optimal alignment computationally practical. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Gap and substitution costs jointly determine the optimal alignment. The author therefore argues that they must have a common rationale. (PDF p. 2).
- The author argues that no single gap-cost definition is suitable for every substitution-cost definition. (PDF p. 13).

## Analysis

- Pairwise cost combines aligned-element substitution costs with separate gap costs. Letter-to-null costs are included in the substitution term. (PDF p. 3).
- Sum-of-the-pairs substitution cost adds the pairwise substitution costs within each alignment column. (PDF p. 3).
- For natural tree-alignment gap costs, relevant history depends on the start order of null runs in adjacent tree sequences. (PDF p. 12).

## Methods

- A pairwise projection selects two padded sequences and removes columns that contain only nulls. (PDF p. 2).
- The proposed costs connect multiple-alignment cost to pairwise projection costs. This connection supports existing optimal-alignment algorithms. (PDF p. 2).
- For quasi-natural tree and star gap costs, the history consists only of the null pattern in the preceding column. (PDF p. 12).

## Applications

- The cost definitions apply to optimal multiple alignment of biological sequences, including tree and star alignments. (PDF p. 12).
- The author links the definitions to algorithms that can align up to six sequences of average protein length. (PDF p. 2).

## Optimization

- Quasi-natural costs reduce the number of histories needed to evaluate gap costs during dynamic programming. (PDF p. 12).
- The author states that quasi-natural costs permit smaller dynamic programming search lattices when combined with recent alignment algorithms. (PDF p. 13).

## Evaluation and testing

- Table 4 compares relevant history counts for natural and quasi-natural costs in tree and star alignments with three to six input sequences. (PDF p. 12).

## Results

- For five input sequences, natural tree costs require 9164 histories, compared with 248 for quasi-natural costs. (PDF p. 12).
- For six input sequences, natural star costs require 3430 histories, compared with 126 for quasi-natural costs. (PDF p. 12).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The analysis restricts attention to constant gap costs, assessed separately from null costs. The author avoids additional length-dependent parameters. (PDF p. 3).
- Natural gap costs cause rapid growth in required histories. For natural tree costs with more than five input sequences, history counts also depend on tree topology. (PDF p. 12).
- Quasi-natural costs are a compromise with the natural definition, rather than an exact implementation of it. (PDF p. 13).
