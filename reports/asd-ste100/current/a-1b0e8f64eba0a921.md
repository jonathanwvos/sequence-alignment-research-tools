## Executive summary

- BAli-Phy uses MCMC to jointly estimate alignment, phylogeny, and evolutionary parameters from unaligned sequences. (PDF p. 1).
- Version 3 adds faster algorithms for large trees, ancestral reconstruction, and additional evolutionary models. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- The author aims to account for alignment uncertainty and resolve the circular dependence between alignment and tree estimation. (PDF p. 1).
- The real-data analysis tests runtime improvements over an earlier version. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- The author argues that a posterior distribution of alignments accounts for uncertainty that a single alignment estimate omits. (PDF p. 1).
- The author argues that shared indels provide evidence for grouping taxa in addition to substitutions. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- The real-data analysis uses 68 individuals and three partitions: ITS1, 5.8S, and ITS2. Their approximate lengths are 223, 156, and 172 columns. (PDF p. 3).
- The analysis fixes the 5.8S alignment and estimates both ITS alignments. The ITS partitions share a substitution model but have separate indel models. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- MCMC samples contain a tree, an alignment, and numerical parameters. Alignment proposals operate with a probabilistic indel model. (PDF p. 1).
- Subtree-prune-and-regraft proposals consider attachment to every branch in O(n) time, where n is the number of leaves. Slice sampling updates numerical parameters. (PDF p. 2).
- Each sampled alignment includes ancestral sequences with gaps. A marginal reconstruction averages over alignment and topology uncertainty. (PDF p. 2).
- Version 3 supports codon, covarion, and Markov-modulated models. Model modifiers allow users to construct codon models from nucleotide models. (PDF p. 2).

## Applications

- Users can analyze multiple partitions and independently fix or estimate each partition's alignment. (PDF p. 2).
- The software reconstructs ancestral DNA and amino acid sequences, including gaps. (PDF p. 2).

## Optimization

- Updated algorithms avoid recomputing the full alignment matrix and cached conditional likelihoods after partial alignment changes. Their dependence on leaf count decreases from O(n²) to O(n). (PDF p. 2).
- Likelihood rescaling prevents numerical underflow for large trees. Automatic slice-sampling window adjustment aims to improve mixing. (PDF p. 2).

## Evaluation and testing

- The scaling benchmark measures the time for 10 MCMC iterations across different taxon counts. It compares versions 2.3.8 and 3.4.1. (PDF p. 2).
- The real-data benchmark runs four unheated chains for 24 hours with versions 3.5 and 2.3.8 in the same cluster environment. (PDF p. 3).
- Fixed parameter values enable likelihood unit tests and comparisons with theoretical values or other software. The excerpt describes this capability without reporting test outcomes. (PDF p. 2).
- The bp-analyze tool reports convergence diagnostics. It projects Robinson–Foulds distances between sampled trees into two or three dimensions with principal components analysis. (PDF p. 2).

## Results

- The scaling benchmark shows quadratic runtime growth with taxon count for version 2.3.8 and linear growth for version 3.4.1. (PDF p. 2).
- After 24 hours, version 3.5 reached ASDSF 0.007 and MSDSF 0.076. Version 2.3.8 reached 0.017 and 0.186, respectively. (PDF p. 3).
- Version 3.5 completed 10,591–12,669 iterations per chain. Version 2.3.8 completed 4,823–5,688. The author reports roughly twice the speed on the 68-taxon dataset. (PDF p. 3).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Runtime remains O(L²) in sequence length L for each partition. (PDF p. 3).
- Iteration counts cannot be compared directly with MrBayes because BAli-Phy updates more components per iteration. (PDF p. 3).
- The comparison with a 15-day analysis from 2009 includes improvements in hardware as well as software. (PDF p. 3).
