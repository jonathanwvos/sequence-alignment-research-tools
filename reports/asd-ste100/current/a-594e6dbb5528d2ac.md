## Executive summary

- The authors introduce Sassy, a SIMD-accelerated algorithm for exact approximate string matching on DNA. (PDF p. 1).
- Sassy reports all occurrences of a pattern within a text under edit distance without requiring an offline index. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- Accelerate exact approximate string matching for short patterns in large genomic databases. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- Approximate string matching is distinct from semi-global alignment and requires exact match reporting. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- Runtime is analyzed across varying edit distance thresholds k and pattern lengths. (PDF p. 8).

## Methods

- The algorithm uses SIMD-parallel bit-parallel dynamic programming over 4-letter DNA alphabets. (PDF p. 3).
- Candidate diagonal filtration identifies potential hit regions before fine-grained verification. (PDF p. 6).

## Applications

- CRISPR off-target search, read demultiplexing, and genome polishing. (PDF p. 4).

## Optimization

- SIMD vectorization processes multiple text windows concurrently. (PDF p. 1).

## Evaluation and testing

- Sassy is benchmarked against Edlib, SWOffinder, and CHOPOFF on human genome CHM13. (PDF p. 8).

## Results

- For k >= 4 differences, Sassy is more than 100x faster than SWOffinder and 4x faster than CHOPOFF. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The algorithm is optimized for short query patterns and is less efficient on long queries. (PDF p. 4).
