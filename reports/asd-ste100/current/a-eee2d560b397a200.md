## Executive summary

- The authors refine protein residue embedding similarities with Z-score normalization, K-means clustering, and two dynamic programming alignments. (PDF p. 2).
- The method improves structural similarity scores and annotation transfer without additional model training. (PDF p. 11).

## Objectives

- Test whether unsupervised refinement can reduce noise in embedding similarity matrices for remote homology detection. (PDF p. 2).
- Evaluate structural similarity, component contributions, CATH annotation transfer, and residue alignment quality. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that averaged protein embeddings can lose residue alignment information. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors motivate sequence methods that capture structural similarity without explicit structure prediction because structures are unavailable for most proteins. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- For sequence lengths u and v and embedding dimension d, similarity construction costs O(uvd). Each alignment costs O(uv). Clustering costs O(K(u+v)dT), where T is the iteration count. The authors report overall scaling of O(uvd). (PDF p. 4).
- Removing refinement reduces ProstT5 correlations from 0.93/0.87 to 0.91/0.84. Removing normalization reduces them to 0.58/0.20. These ablations support a larger contribution from normalization. (PDF p. 8).
- Interpretation: the Stage 3 ablation measures clustering and the second alignment together. It does not isolate their separate contributions. (PDF p. 8).

## Methods

- The method uses residue embeddings from ProtT5, ProstT5, or ESM-1b. Their dimensions are 1024, 1024, and 1280, respectively. (PDF p. 2).
- Each residue pair receives exp(−Euclidean distance) as its initial similarity. The method then averages the row and column Z-scores for each matrix entry. (PDF p. 3).
- A first Needleman–Wunsch alignment uses zero gap penalties. K-means groups the combined residue embeddings from both sequences into 20 clusters. (PDF p. 3).
- For aligned residue pairs, the update is 0.8s + 0.2sW. W is 1 for a shared cluster and 0.5 otherwise. Other pairs remain unchanged. A second Needleman–Wunsch alignment produces a score divided by the shorter or longer sequence length. (PDF p. 4).

## Applications

- The method estimates structural similarity between proteins with low sequence similarity. (PDF p. 4).
- CATH annotations are transferred from the most similar lookup protein at the Class, Architecture, Topology, and Homology levels. (PDF p. 9).

## Optimization

- A sensitivity study tests K values of 20, 50, and 100 and blending factors of 1.0, 0.8, and 0.6. K = 20 and a blending factor of 0.8 give the highest reported correlations. (PDF p. 9).
- With embeddings already available, the full pipeline takes 0.044 seconds per pair on a CPU. The version without Stage 3 takes 0.021 seconds. (PDF p. 8).
- Including GPU embedding generation, mean runtime is 0.119 seconds with ESM-1b, 0.126 seconds with ProtT5, and 0.130 seconds with ProstT5. (PDF p. 9).

## Evaluation and testing

- PISCES contains 19,599 protein pairs with at most 30% sequence similarity and a minimum chain length of 75 residues. Evaluation uses Spearman correlation with TM-align scores normalized by shorter and longer sequence lengths. (PDF p. 4).
- CATH evaluation uses lookup and test sets with HVAL < 0. Each test protein has a lookup protein with the same annotation at the tested level. Accuracy measures correct annotation transfers. (PDF p. 4).
- HOMSTRAD evaluation selects the first and last sequence from each of 1032 families. Predicted residue pairs are compared with curated structural alignments. (PDF p. 4).
- HOMSTRAD precision and sensitivity are averaged across families. Competitor scores come from prior published results, and both the proposed method and EBA use ProstT5 embeddings. (PDF p. 5).

## Results

- On PISCES, correlations for shorter/longer normalization are 0.93/0.87 with ProstT5, 0.91/0.84 with ProtT5, and 0.89/0.81 with ESM-1b. EBA gives 0.92/0.86, 0.90/0.84, and 0.87/0.80, respectively. (PDF p. 5).
- CATH accuracy is 91% for Class, 85% for Architecture, 80% for Topology, and 89% for Homology. Class accuracy is similar to EBA; the other levels improve by 1–2 percentage points. (PDF p. 9).
- HOMSTRAD precision is 0.864 and sensitivity is 0.860. EBA gives 0.861 and 0.859. DALI has the highest precision at 0.913, and TM-align has the highest sensitivity at 0.877. (PDF p. 10).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Computational constraints limit testing to ProtT5, ProstT5, and ESM-1b. The authors leave newer models, retrieval baselines, and detailed error analysis for future work. (PDF p. 11).
- Interpretation: PISCES and CATH comparisons use published competitor scores rather than a common rerun of all methods. (PDF p. 4).
- The reported fourfold speed advantage over TM-align assumes that embeddings are already available. (PDF p. 9).
