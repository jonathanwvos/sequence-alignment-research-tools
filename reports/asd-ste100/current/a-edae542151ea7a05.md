## Executive summary

- COFFEE scores a multiple sequence alignment by its consistency with a library of pairwise alignments. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors use the SAGA genetic algorithm to optimize this score. (PDF p. 1).
- The reported comparisons favor SAGA-COFFEE for sequences with low identity. (PDF p. 9).
- Local COFFEE scores also indicate alignment reliability. (PDF p. 10).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to improve multiple alignment accuracy through consistency with pairwise reference alignments. (PDF p. 2).
- They also seek to identify reliable portions of a multiple alignment. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that progressive alignment can preserve early errors because later steps do not correct them. (PDF p. 2).
- They propose that pairwise libraries filter noise while retaining information for global optimization. (PDF p. 14).
- They present the library as an interface that can transfer different pairwise alignment methods to multiple alignment. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- COFFEE evaluation is quadratic in sequence count and linear in alignment consensus length. (PDF p. 8).
- The authors cannot predict the number of generations needed for an optimum. Sequence length, similarity, gap patterns, and genetic operators affect this number. (PDF p. 8).
- Direct DALI evaluation is quadratic in alignment length. The authors present COFFEE as a less costly approximation when structural libraries are available. (PDF p. 14).

## Methods

- The experiments use one pairwise alignment for each of the N(N−1)/2 sequence pairs. Libraries come from default ClustalW alignments or FSSP structural alignments. (PDF p. 3).
- Evaluation compares residue pairings in the multiple alignment with those in the library. Residues are identified by sequence position, and residue-gap pairings are included. (PDF p. 3).
- The library is stored in a lookup table for evaluation. (PDF p. 3).
- COFFEE and the ClustalW library construction procedure were implemented in ANSI C and integrated into SAGA version 0.93. (PDF p. 8).

## Applications

- COFFEE can combine pairwise structural alignments into a multiple sequence alignment. (PDF p. 12).
- Sequence and residue scores can help identify alignment regions that are likely to be correct. (PDF p. 10).

## Optimization

- For the main comparison, SAGA optimizes COFFEE with ClustalW libraries. The highest-scoring alignment from ten runs is retained. (PDF p. 8).
- The authors test optimization with artificial libraries derived from reference multiple alignments. These libraries have a known maximum COFFEE score of 1. (PDF p. 8).

## Evaluation and testing

- The benchmark contains 11 selected 3D_ali cases and two FSSP cases. The 3D_ali selection excludes four easy cases and three cases with extreme divergence and long insertions or deletions. (PDF p. 7).
- Comparators are ClustalW 1.6, SAGA-MSA, PRRP 2.5.1, PILEUP in GCG 9.1, and SAM 2.0. (PDF p. 7).
- Accuracy is the fraction of reference residue pairs recovered, excluding gaps. A second measure uses only helix-to-helix and strand-to-strand pairs, called E+H. (PDF p. 7).
- For structural-library cases, trimdali evaluates each pairwise projection. The authors average DALI score, superimposed consensus length, and RMS deviation. (PDF p. 7).

## Results

- With artificial libraries, SAGA reaches the known optimum in an average of 5.4 of ten runs. The authors also report scores above 0.94 when the optimum is not reached. (PDF p. 8).
- Against ClustalW, SAGA-COFFEE improves overall accuracy in nine of 11 cases and E+H accuracy in ten. Reported decreases are below 2%. (PDF p. 8).
- SAGA-COFFEE recovers 63.3% of E+H pairs in the 0–20% identity group and 76.2% in the 20–40% group. These are the highest reported values in those groups. (PDF p. 9).
- Sequence score has a correlation of r = 0.65 with E+H accuracy. The authors report accuracy predictions within ±10% for more than 85% of sequences. (PDF p. 10).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors state that COFFEE performance depends on the quality of the pairwise library. (PDF p. 3).
- Structural references can contain locally arbitrary alignments where structures cannot be superimposed. (PDF p. 7).
- Interpretation: benchmark exclusions limit evidence for very easy cases and for highly divergent sequences with long insertions or deletions. (PDF p. 7).
- The authors describe SAGA-COFFEE as extremely slow and propose better seeding to improve efficiency. (PDF p. 14).
