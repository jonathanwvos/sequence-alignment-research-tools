## Executive summary

- Masek and Paterson present an algorithm for minimum-cost string editing with insertion, deletion, and replacement of individual symbols. (PDF p. 1).
- For lengths n > m, the authors give a time bound of O(n max(1, m/log n)). The bound requires a finite alphabet and edit costs that are integer multiples of one positive real number. (PDF p. 1).
- The algorithm can produce an optimal edit sequence. Longest common subsequence computation is a special case. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- The aim is to improve the asymptotic time of the Wagner–Fischer algorithm, whose time is proportional to the product of the string lengths. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that precomputation over small submatrices can accelerate edit-matrix computation, following the Four Russians technique. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- The baseline recurrence takes the minimum cost of replacement, deletion, or insertion from three adjacent matrix entries. Each entry takes constant time, giving O(|A||B|) time. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors use an example with irrational costs to show why discreteness is necessary for efficient use of Algorithm Y. (PDF p. 13).
- In that example, the number of distinct step sizes between adjacent diagonals grows linearly with string length. This prevents effective reuse of preprocessing. (PDF p. 13).

## Methods

- Edit distance is the minimum total cost over edit sequences that transform one string into the other. Each operation has a nonnegative cost. (PDF p. 2).
- The proposed method applies the Four Russians approach to Wagner–Fischer edit matrices. It uses small submatrices and precomputed computations. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- The method computes optimal edit sequences and longest common subsequences for string pairs. (PDF p. 1).

## Optimization

- Precomputation reduces the stated time bound for two length-n strings from quadratic time to O(n²/log n), under the alphabet and cost restrictions. (PDF p. 1).

## Evaluation and testing

- The authors test the need for discrete costs through a mathematical counterexample. All other stated conditions hold, but Algorithm Y is inefficient. (PDF p. 13).

## Results

- The authors establish an O(n²/log n) bound for equal-length strings with a finite alphabet and discrete edit costs. (PDF p. 13).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The stated speed bound requires a finite alphabet and costs that are integer multiples of a common positive real number. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors leave open the search for a faster algorithm for finite alphabets without the discreteness condition. (PDF p. 13).
- At publication, the authors report a linear lower bound and a quadratic or O(n²/log n) upper bound, depending on the cost condition. (PDF p. 13).
