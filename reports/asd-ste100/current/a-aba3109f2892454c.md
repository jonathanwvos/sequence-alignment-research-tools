## Executive summary

- AlphaFold 3 predicts joint structures containing proteins, nucleic acids, ligands, ions, and modified residues. (PDF p. 1).
- The model reduces MSA processing, uses a pairformer, and generates atomic coordinates with diffusion. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors report improved accuracy over specialized comparators for several interaction types, including protein–ligand and antibody–antigen interactions. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to predict complexes with many molecular types in one deep-learning framework. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that direct coordinate diffusion accommodates general chemical components with less special handling than residue frames and torsion angles. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors interpret improved interaction predictions as evidence that missing evolutionary information between entities does not prevent progress. (PDF p. 7).

## Analysis

- During initial training, intrachain metrics reach 97% of their maximum within 20,000 steps. Protein–protein interface LDDT reaches this level only after 60,000 steps. (PDF p. 4).
- Despite reduced MSA processing, AF3 retains a similar dependence on MSA depth to AlphaFold-Multimer v.2.3. Shallow MSAs give lower protein accuracy. (PDF p. 5).
- Antibody prediction improves with more model seeds up to 1,000. Increasing diffusion samples alone does not explain this improvement. (PDF p. 7).

## Methods

- Four MSA blocks use pair-weighted averaging. Later processing retains the pair representation but discards the MSA representation. The pairformer has 48 blocks. (PDF p. 2).
- The diffusion model learns to recover true coordinates from noisy coordinates. Inference repeatedly removes noise from an initial random sample. (PDF p. 3).
- Cross-distillation adds AlphaFold-Multimer predictions to training. Their extended loops reduce false compact structures in disordered regions. (PDF p. 4).
- A shortened diffusion rollout generates structures during training for confidence supervision. The confidence module predicts pLDDT, PAE, and distance errors. (PDF p. 4).

## Applications

- Inputs include polymer sequences, residue modifications, and ligand SMILES. These support prediction across several biologically relevant complex types. (PDF p. 4).
- Examples include a ribosomal complex, a glycosylated protein, an antibody-bound peptide, and proteins bound to small molecules. (PDF p. 4).

## Optimization

- Training adjusts dataset sampling probabilities and uses a weighted metric for early stopping. These measures address uneven learning and possible overfitting. (PDF p. 4).
- Training starts with crops of 384 tokens. Two fine-tuning stages increase crop size to 640 and 768 tokens. (PDF p. 9).
- Standard inference selects the highest-confidence prediction from five seeds with five diffusion samples each. Ranking includes confidence and penalties for clashes. (PDF p. 9).

## Evaluation and testing

- The recent PDB test set contains 8,856 complexes released from 1 May 2022 to 12 January 2023. Chains and interfaces are scored separately and aggregated by cluster. (PDF p. 9).
- PoseBusters contains 428 protein–ligand structures. Success requires pocket-aligned ligand RMSD below 2 Å. (PDF p. 4).
- Evaluation uses DockQ for protein interfaces, interface LDDT for protein–nucleic acid contacts, and LDDT for monomers. Figure 1 reports confidence intervals and paired statistical tests where applicable. (PDF p. 2).
- Low-homology evaluation applies sequence-identity filters at 40%. RoseTTAFold2NA comparisons use the same MSAs as AF3. (PDF p. 9).

## Results

- AF3 outperforms AutoDock Vina on PoseBusters without structural inputs. The reported Fisher exact test gives P = 2.27 × 10^-13. (PDF p. 4).
- AF3 exceeds RoseTTAFold2NA on protein–nucleic acid complexes and RNA structures. It does not reach the best human-assisted CASP15 RNA result. (PDF p. 5).
- Protein interface success, antibody interface success, and protein monomer LDDT improve significantly over AlphaFold-Multimer v.2.3. (PDF p. 5).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- PoseBusters predictions retain a 4.4% chirality violation rate despite ranking penalties. Clash penalties reduce but do not eliminate overlapping atoms or chains. (PDF p. 5).
- AF3 can predict false structural order in disordered regions. Such regions usually have low predicted confidence. (PDF p. 5).
- Conformational coverage is limited. In the cereblon example, both apo and ligand-bound predictions adopt the closed conformation. (PDF p. 7).
- The excerpts give inconsistent PoseBusters training cutoffs. Page 5 states 30 September 2019, while page 9 states filtering after 30 September 2021. The intended training cutoff is uncertain. (Page not verified).
