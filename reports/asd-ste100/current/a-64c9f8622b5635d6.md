## Executive summary

- Foldseek converts protein structures into sequences over a 20-state structural alphabet called 3Di. It searches these sequences to find structural matches. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors report large runtime reductions relative to conventional structural aligners, with some loss of search sensitivity relative to Dali and TM-align. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to make sensitive structure searches practical for databases with millions of protein structures. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors identify two causes of slow structural alignment: insufficient prefiltering and non-local similarity scores that require iterative or stochastic optimization. (PDF p. 1).
- They argue that tertiary interaction states contain more useful information than local backbone states. The 3Di states have weaker adjacent dependencies and more even frequencies. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- Foldseek-TM exceeded TM-align in precision-recall performance on SCOPe. The authors attribute this result to prefilter removal of high-scoring false positives. (PDF p. 2).
- Inspection of apparent false positives found correctly aligned structural segments with different relative orientations. The authors argue that Foldseek can detect relationships missed by global superposition. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- A modified VQ-VAE learns 20 3Di states from ten geometric features. Each state describes a residue and a neighbor selected through virtual center distance. (PDF p. 2).
- The prefilter finds two similar spaced k-mers on one diagonal. It uses k equal to 6 or 7 and passes ungapped alignments with at least 15 bits. (PDF p. 5).
- SIMD Smith–Waterman alignment combines amino acid and 3Di substitution scores with weights 1.4 and 2.1. Foldseek-TM instead applies TM-align after the initial search. (PDF p. 5).
- Hit ranking multiplies the alignment bit score by the geometric mean of alignment LDDT and TM-score. Reverse-query score subtraction and composition correction suppress false positives. (PDF p. 5).

## Applications

- The webserver supports searches across predicted and experimental structure databases. In a demonstration, all ten highest-ranked matches to SARS-CoV-2 RdRp were known RdRp homologs. (PDF p. 3).
- The described webserver predicts structures for submitted sequences of up to 400 residues through the ESM Atlas API. This enables sequence-to-structure searches. (PDF p. 9).

## Optimization

- Prefiltering reduces full alignments by several orders of magnitude. Multithreading and SIMD vector units provide further speed gains. (PDF p. 5).
- LDDT computation uses spatial hashing with 15 Å grid cells to find nearby query residues. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors tuned substitution weights and gap penalties on about 1% of SCOPe40 pairwise alignments. (PDF p. 5).
- Precomputed database indexes allow the webserver to keep search data in RAM. Batch searches adapt memory use to available RAM. (PDF p. 9).

## Evaluation and testing

- SCOPe40 contains 11,211 structures clustered at 40% sequence identity. All-versus-all tests measured sensitivity before the first false positive at family, superfamily, and fold levels. (PDF p. 2).
- The multidomain benchmark used 100 queries from 34,270 AlphaFold clusters. LDDT thresholds of at least 0.6 and below 0.25 defined true and false matches. (PDF p. 7).
- HOMSTRAD tests used the first and last members of 1,032 families. Reference residue pairs defined precision and sensitivity. Prefilters and E-value thresholds were disabled for selected tools. (PDF p. 8).
- Runtime tests used a 64-core AMD EPYC 7702P server with 1,024 GB RAM. SCOPe runtimes for TM-align, Dali, and CE were estimated from 10% of queries. (PDF p. 7).

## Results

- On SCOPe40, Foldseek was over 4,000 times faster than TM-align and Dali, and over 21,000 times faster than CE. (PDF p. 2).
- On the multidomain test, Foldseek alignments were more accurate and sensitive than TM-align. They were 13% less precise but 15% more sensitive than CE. (PDF p. 3).
- On HOMSTRAD, Foldseek alignment quality was slightly below CE, Dali, and TM-align. (PDF p. 3).
- The RdRp search took 6 seconds with the Foldseek webserver, 33 hours with TM-align, and 10 days with Dali. (PDF p. 3).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- SCOPe tests only single-domain proteins. HOMSTRAD also consists mostly of single-domain proteins. These tests cannot adequately assess local similarities between multidomain proteins. (PDF p. 9).
- The reference-free multidomain benchmark requires selected thresholds for true and false positives. (PDF p. 9).
- The final 3Di alphabet was trained on all SCOPe40 structures used for the reported benchmark. The authors report no observed overfitting in their comparison. (PDF p. 7).
- Some comparisons used incomplete data or different execution settings. Dali conversion failed for some structures, one CE query was excluded, and Geometricus ran on one core. (PDF p. 8).
