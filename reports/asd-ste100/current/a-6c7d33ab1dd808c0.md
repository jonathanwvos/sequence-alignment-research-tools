## Executive summary

- FoldMason constructs multiple protein structure alignments with a progressive algorithm that uses the Foldseek 3Di+AA alphabet. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors report accuracy comparable to established structure aligners, with substantially shorter runtimes on their benchmarks. (PDF p. 1).
- FoldMason provides LDDT alignment scores, optional refinement, and interactive result views. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to align large collections of divergent protein structures produced by structure prediction and clustering. (PDF p. 1).
- They test alignment accuracy, scaling, tolerance of structural flexibility, and use in structural phylogeny. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that conserved protein structure permits comparisons after amino acid sequence similarity becomes weak. (PDF p. 1).
- They argue that existing accurate structure aligners require too much computation for rapidly growing structure databases. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- On the AFDB benchmark, structure aligners generally scored better on gapless cores. Sequence aligners generally scored better on full alignments. The authors link this difference to structural flexibility. (PDF p. 2).
- Refinement that maximized full-alignment LDDT reduced core LDDT. Refinement that maximized core LDDT reduced full-alignment LDDT. (PDF p. 3).
- The authors attribute FoldMason's performance on flexible proteins to its use of local structural interactions. (PDF p. 4).

## Methods

- FoldMason encodes structures as 3Di+AA strings and computes ungapped alignments for all pairs. It sorts pair scores to construct a minimum spanning guide tree. (PDF p. 2).
- Progressive alignment merges structure profiles from the leaves to the root. Independent alignments at each tree rank run in parallel. (PDF p. 2).
- FoldMason computes LDDT on pairwise subalignments. It maps residue scores to alignment columns, averages each column, and averages the column scores. (PDF p. 2).
- Optional refinement randomly splits an alignment into two groups, removes gap-only columns, and realigns the groups. It retains a change only when LDDT increases. (PDF p. 2).

## Applications

- FoldMason aligned flexible regions in calmodulin, globins, and odorant-binding proteins that caused problems for mTM-align. (PDF p. 4).
- FoldMason alignments supported Flaviviridae glycoprotein phylogenies. Quartet similarities to earlier manually constructed results were 88.33%, 87.44%, and 82.81% for E, E1, and E2. (PDF p. 4).
- The easy-msa module accepts PDB and mmCIF files. It produces matching amino acid and 3Di FASTA alignments, with optional guide trees and HTML views. (PDF p. 8).

## Optimization

- FoldMason uses striped accelerated ungapped alignment for initial comparisons and parallel progressive alignment for profile merging. (PDF p. 1).
- Optional linear-time clustering forms preliminary groups to improve scaling. (PDF p. 1).
- The refinement benchmark used 1,000 iterations and fixed random seed 48335597. Separate runs optimized LDDT with and without gappy columns. (PDF p. 9).

## Evaluation and testing

- Tests compared four structure aligners and four sequence aligners using default parameters. The server had one AMD EPYC 7702P CPU with 64 cores and 1,024 GB RAM. Tools with multithreading used all cores. (PDF p. 8).
- The Homstrad test used 1,032 manually curated families from the May 2024 release. T-Coffee measured sum-of-pairs scores in both directions, total column scores, and column scores. (PDF p. 8).
- The AFDB test selected 1,000 clusters with high predicted confidence and length constraints. Each cluster supplied 20 divergent members. Full and gapless-core LDDT provided reference-free scores. (PDF p. 2).
- The scaling test used subsets of 10 to 100,000 structures from cluster A0A258JAY9. It compared FoldMason with FAMSA2 and mTM-align. (PDF p. 9).

## Results

- FoldMason achieved a Homstrad F1 score of 0.894. Only MUSTANG and mTM-align scored higher. (PDF p. 2).
- FoldMason had the highest full-alignment LDDT among tested tools on the AFDB benchmark. Its core LDDT exceeded sequence aligners and Caretta-shape. (PDF p. 2).
- FoldMason was 722 times faster than MUSTANG and seven times slower than FAMSA2 on average. With 1,000 refinement iterations, full and core LDDT improved by 1.74% and 2.51%, respectively. (PDF p. 3).
- For 100,000 inputs, FoldMason required 92,899.10 seconds and FAMSA2 required 147.49 seconds. mTM-align did not finish 500 structures within 214 hours. (PDF p. 3).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Homstrad mainly contains small groups of relatively short proteins. The authors state that these groups do not fully represent most protein families. (PDF p. 2).
- FoldMason's core LDDT was lower than Matt, mTM-align, and MUSTANG by 1.72%, 4.03%, and 4.61%, respectively. (PDF p. 3).
- Scaling remained substantially slower than FAMSA2. FoldMason required about 630 times longer for the 100,000-input test. (PDF p. 3).
- FoldMason did not lead every flexibility test. Caretta-shape achieved 2.7% higher LDDT for the globin multiple alignment. (PDF p. 4).
