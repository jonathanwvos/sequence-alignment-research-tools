## Executive summary

- The authors present HPRC2 with 460 haplotypes, three pangenome alignment representations, and matched transcriptome and epigenome data. (PDF p. 35).
- They introduce graph coordinate systems that describe variation absent from a chosen linear reference. (PDF p. 15).

## Objectives

- Represent population variation through haplotype assemblies, alignments, and functional annotations. (PDF p. 5).
- Preserve nested variation within insertions when reporting variants outside a linear reference. (PDF p. 15).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that projection onto a reference without an insertion hides variation within that insertion. (PDF p. 15).
- They suggest that improved eQTL associations support future use of pangenome methods in genome-wide association studies. (PDF p. 33).

## Analysis

- Panacus calculates sequential growth curves from a histogram of sequence occurrence counts in HPRC1 and HPRC2. The histogram records the number of bases for each count pair. (PDF p. 56).
- The authors estimate alignable sequence from collinear multi-MUM blocks. Adjacent matches must occur in every assembly, remain less than 100 bp apart, and have consistent orientation and order. (PDF p. 56).
- Multi-MUM coverage was measured across 10 random subsets at different collection sizes. Pairwise matches to T2T-CHM13 were merged for this analysis. (PDF p. 56).

## Methods

- The tree coordinate strategy constructs a minimum spanning tree over the graph. It indexes edges to describe off-reference variants in formats such as VCF. (PDF p. 15).
- GRef adds contigs for novel insertion sequences to a chosen reference. A vertex-disjoint path cover spans the graph, with each added contig taken from a haplotype assembly. (PDF p. 15).
- The eQTL callset merge identifies matching variants with RTG vcfeval or normalized allele identity. Matched pairs are redundant when genotype or dosage correlation has r² ≥ 0.5. (PDF p. 78).

## Applications

- PanGenie, danbing-tk, and EdgeDepth supplied pangenome variants for eQTL mapping in 430 samples with genome and RNA sequencing data. (PDF p. 33).
- The authors integrated HPRC2 into vg-DeepVariant and Sentieon pipelines to evaluate variant calling. (PDF p. 35).

## Optimization

- The authors report that optimized sample selection covers over 99% of common variants observed in the All of Us v8 cohort. (PDF p. 35).

## Evaluation and testing

- Variant calls from HG002 reads on four sequencing platforms were compared with GIAB-v5.0-GRCh38. The evaluation measured error reductions against corresponding linear-reference pipelines. (PDF p. 35).
- The eQTL comparison used the same 430 samples with merged calls and with 1000G calls alone. TensorQTL beta-approximate permutation p-values controlled for different multiple-testing burdens. (PDF p. 79).
- Improvement was the percentage increase in the negative base-10 logarithm of the corrected lead-marker p-value for each eGene. (PDF p. 79).

## Results

- The authors report half as many structurally unreliable regions and individual base errors per haplotype as in HPRC1. (PDF p. 5).
- The tree coordinate analysis counted 2.6 million off-reference SNPs, 2.3 million indels, and 80,000 structural variants. (PDF p. 15).
- GRef identified 162,807 off-reference alignable sequences of at least 50 bp. Their total length was 231 Mb. (PDF p. 15).
- Pangenome-specific variants supplied 20.3% of lead markers at 10,169 eQTLs. Association strength improved by at least 20% for 893 eGenes. (PDF p. 33).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Diversity-based sample selection violates the random-sampling assumption of the coverage analysis. The authors state that this can overestimate population variation and underestimate coverage. (PDF p. 56).
- The off-reference calls captured only a minority of variation seen in assemblies. The authors expect further methods, probably involving long reads, to be necessary. (PDF p. 33).
