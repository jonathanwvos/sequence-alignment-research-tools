## Executive summary

- mapquik maps accurate long reads with seeds made from k consecutive sampled minimizers. It indexes only seeds that occur once in the reference. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors combine these seeds with a linear-time pseudochaining heuristic. They report high sensitivity and specificity on human and maize genomes. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- The study tests whether unique k-min-mer seeds can accelerate long-read mapping while retaining accurate mappings. (PDF p. 3).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that short seeds create many candidate locations. Longer exact seeds reduce these matches but can lose sensitivity. (PDF p. 3).
- They propose long minimizer-space seeds to obtain specific matches without requiring a long contiguous exact match. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- A pseudochain requires matches to agree with an anchor on strand, reference order, and bounded query–reference gap differences. Its matches need not all be pairwise colinear. (PDF p. 10).
- For each reference, the algorithm selects an anchor and checks the other matches in linear time. Summing these scans gives O(n) time for n maximal matches. (PDF p. 10).
- Mapping accuracy increases with pseudochain score and plateaus near scores of nine to 11. The default score threshold is 11. (PDF p. 8).

## Methods

- Adjacent k-min-mer matches are extended when the next query and reference k-min-mers match. (PDF p. 10).
- For each reference, the algorithm anchors one candidate pseudochain at the maximal match with the most matching k-min-mers. It retains the candidate with the highest total score. (PDF p. 10).
- The score counts matching k-min-mers. A mapping is accepted if its score or length meets a user threshold, and its coordinates are extended to the query length. (PDF p. 10).

## Applications

- Current uses include locating HiFi reads, sorting reads by genomic position, separating reads by chromosome, and filtering nonhuman reads. (PDF p. 8).
- The authors propose remapping reads that mapquik misses with minimap2 or Winnowmap2 to increase sensitivity. (PDF p. 8).

## Optimization

- Unique long seeds reduce ambiguous matches. Pseudochaining avoids the dynamic programming procedure used for conventional chaining. (PDF p. 10).
- The authors tested multiple-anchor heuristics with O(cn) and O(n²) runtime. The single-anchor method ran about 20–50% faster, with little loss of accuracy. (PDF p. 10).

## Evaluation and testing

- The seed study used human HiFi reads with a mean error rate of 0.1%. It counted seed occurrences in CHM13v2.0 as a proxy for candidate mapping locations. (PDF p. 3).
- The indexing benchmark compared mapquik, minimap2, mm2-fast, Winnowmap2, and BLEND on CHM13v2.0. Each tool used 10 threads and a warm cache. (PDF p. 8).

## Results

- The authors report sensitivity above 96% and near-perfect specificity. Reported speedups over minimap2 were 37-fold on human reads and 410-fold on maize reads. (PDF p. 2).
- Human reference indexing took 3.4 seconds and 12.1 GB with mapquik. Minimap2 took 33 seconds and 10.1 GB. (PDF p. 8).
- The genomic regions missed by mapquik and minimap2 overlapped by more than 90%. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Some reads lack an indexed seed or a sufficiently strong pseudochain. These failures occur mainly in low-complexity regions. (PDF p. 8).
- Performance declines markedly below 97% read–reference identity. Below 93% identity, fewer than 1% of reads map at Q60. (PDF p. 8).
- Base-level alignment is a proposed extension. The current method does not provide the complete variant-calling pipelines discussed by the authors. (PDF p. 8).
