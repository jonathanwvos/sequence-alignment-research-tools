## Executive summary

- Mugsy aligns closely related whole genomes without a reference genome. It accepts mixtures of complete genomes and assembled draft genomes. (PDF p. 1).
- The method combines Nucmer pairwise alignments, an alignment graph, locally collinear block identification, and SeqAn::T-Coffee multiple alignment. (PDF p. 2).
- Mugsy aligned 31 S. pneumoniae genomes in under two hours and 57 E. coli genomes in about 19 hours on one CPU. (PDF p. 5).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to compare many long, similar genomes efficiently while handling rearrangements, duplications, and sequence gain or loss. (PDF p. 1).
- The tool avoids a reference requirement for both alignment calculation and output interpretation. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that segment graphs are compact for highly conserved genomes. Long ungapped regions become single vertices, so graph size depends on genetic diversity. (PDF p. 2).
- Longer aligned blocks simplify ortholog identification and annotation mapping. They also support inspection of sequence gain and loss without a reference genome. (PDF p. 6).

## Analysis

- In comparisons of 11 complete S. pneumoniae genomes, Mugsy had precision and recall of 0.99 and 0.99 against TBA. Against Mauve, these values were 0.97 and 0.99. (PDF p. 6).
- These precision and recall values measure agreement with another tool treated as hypothetical truth. They do not establish accuracy against independently known alignments. (PDF p. 6).
- The authors suggest that short anchors may explain Enredo's fragmented output in their comparison. Some supplied anchors were only one base long. (PDF p. 6).

## Methods

- Nucmer searches all genome pairs. Delta-filter retains matches in the highest-scoring chains of both sequences for multiple alignment. Scores combine match length with squared sequence identity. (PDF p. 3).
- A modified delta-filter reports duplicated segments found in the chain of only one sequence. Mugsy saves these separately in MAF format. (PDF p. 3).
- The alignment graph stores non-overlapping ungapped segments, homology edges, and relative orientations. Greedy traversal of consistent edges builds multi-genome anchors. (PDF p. 3).
- An anchor graph identifies collinear paths and merges regions after short-block filtering. Maximum-flow minimum-cut separates paths that violate distance constraints. SeqAn::T-Coffee aligns the resulting blocks. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- Tests included complete and draft genomes from N. meningitidis, S. pneumoniae, and E. coli, plus four human chromosome 1 sequences. (PDF p. 5).
- Human alignments supported extraction of substitutions, insertions, and deletions. SNVs were compared with published UCSC and dbSNP variants. (PDF p. 7).
- The authors identify species pan-genomes and other highly conserved within-species comparisons as suitable uses. (PDF p. 8).

## Optimization

- Mugsy uses suffix-tree-based Nucmer searches and compact segment graphs to reduce work on highly conserved sequences. (PDF p. 8).
- Longer blocks are processed first. Anchors are removed as they are aligned, so each anchor belongs to exactly one block. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors propose parallel pairwise searches and parallel alignment of separate blocks as future work. Parallel processing was not presented as an implemented optimization. (PDF p. 8).

## Evaluation and testing

- Runtime tests used one Intel Xeon 5570 CPU and 16 GB RAM. Reported times were the shortest successful runs from three tests. (PDF p. 5).
- TBA received the same filtered Nucmer alignments as Mugsy. Mauve received genome FASTA files directly. (PDF p. 5).
- MLAGAN, Pecan, and FSA received blocks defined by Mugsy. These tests compared alignment components rather than independent whole-genome workflows. (PDF p. 5).
- Output comparisons used complete genomes and the compare utility for precision, recall, and agreement. Enredo and Mugsy also received the same anchors for block comparisons. (PDF p. 5).

## Results

- For 31 S. pneumoniae genomes, pairwise search took 44 minutes and Mugsy took another 56 minutes. For 57 E. coli genomes, these stages took 435 and 720 minutes. (PDF p. 6).
- For 31 S. pneumoniae genomes, Mugsy produced 2394 blocks and a gap-free core of 1590820 bases. Mauve produced 1366 blocks and a core of 1568715 bases. (PDF p. 6).
- Four human chromosome 1 sequences required 1138 minutes of pairwise search and 37 minutes of Mugsy processing. TBA required 71 minutes after the same pairwise searches. (PDF p. 6).
- The human alignment produced 526 blocks and covered 99.86% of the reported hg19 chromosome 1 sequence. (PDF p. 7).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- More divergent genomes shorten conserved segments and enlarge the graph. Processing 57 E. coli genomes required slightly more than 12 GB RAM. (PDF p. 8).
- Greedy anchor construction can introduce branches and fragmentation. Draft contig boundaries also increase block counts. Larger distance settings produce longer blocks with slight sensitivity loss. (PDF p. 7).
- Parameter selection becomes important at longer evolutionary distances. The authors identify automatic selection and user guidance as areas needing improvement. (PDF p. 8).
- Human consensus sequences built with a reference may underrepresent variation. Mugsy does not use underlying read quality or composition when deriving variants. (PDF p. 7).
