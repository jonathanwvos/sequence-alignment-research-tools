## Executive summary

- The authors present a BSP/CGM parallel algorithm for all-substrings longest common subsequence (ALCS). It computes LCS lengths for A and every substring of B. (PDF p. 1).
- The algorithm also reconstructs an actual longest common subsequence of A and B. (PDF p. 32).

## Objectives

- The authors seek an efficient parallel ALCS algorithm with reduced computation, storage, and communication costs. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that solving ALCS can be efficient even when only basic LCS is required. They report linear speed-up over the usual sequential LCS algorithms. (PDF p. 33).

## Analysis

- For string lengths na and nb and p < sqrt(na) processors, the stated bounds are O(na*nb/p) time, O(nb*sqrt(na)) space per processor, and O(log p) communication rounds. (PDF p. 33).
- The LCS reconstruction theorem retains these bounds. Each processor transfers O(nb) data per communication round. (PDF p. 32).
- Finding crossing vertices during reconstruction takes O(nb) parallel time per step and O(nb log p) time across all steps. (PDF p. 32).

## Methods

- The algorithm uses totally monotone matrices and similarities between DG matrix rows and consecutive MD[i] matrices to remove redundant computation. (PDF p. 33).
- Each processor reconstructs a subpath in the grid directed acyclic graph and extracts part of the LCS. The parts can be joined in one communication round. (PDF p. 32).
- The appendix reduces a totally monotone matrix while preserving column minima. It then solves even columns recursively and uses those results to determine odd-column minima. (PDF p. 34).

## Applications

- The authors identify approximate tandem-repeat detection, circular alignment, and alignment against strings with a shared substring as ALCS applications. (PDF p. 1).

## Optimization

- Compact data structures reduce both stored information and message sizes. (PDF p. 33).
- For reconstruction within basic grid graphs, the authors prefer a sequential linear-space LCS algorithm. Retaining the earlier ALCS data would require O(na*nb/p) space. (PDF p. 32).

## Evaluation and testing

Not stated in the extractable source pages.

## Results

- The authors claim the best BSP/CGM bounds then known for LCS and ALCS. The supplied result is an asymptotic guarantee. (PDF p. 2).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The stated parallel bounds require p < sqrt(na). (PDF p. 32).
- The parallel space bound is O(nb*sqrt(na)) per processor, compared with O(nb) space for the cited sequential ALCS algorithm. (PDF p. 2).
