## Executive summary

- The authors construct the transitive closure of an acyclic directed graph in O(n³/ln n) operations. (PDF p. 2).
- The method uses vertex ranks and a lemma that accelerates products of multivalued mappings through precomputed subset images. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- The authors seek a lower operation count for transitive closure than the cited O(n³) bound. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors cite a reduction that uses the acyclic Herz graph to obtain closure for a general directed graph. The stages before and after acyclic closure each take O(n²) operations. (PDF p. 1).
- A partition into ranks turns closure construction into a triangular sequence of mapping products. (PDF p. 1).

## Analysis

- The mapping lemma gives O((p + q)qr/ln q) operations for βα, where α: A → B, β: B → C, and the set sizes are p, q, and r. (PDF p. 1).
- The proof bounds the product for rank i by O(n²n_i/ln n), where n_i is its size. Summation over the ranks gives the total bound. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- Start with vertices that have no predecessors. Add each later rank from vertices whose predecessors are all in earlier ranks. (PDF p. 1).
- Combine the product γ_iG_(i−1) with the direct mapping γ_i to obtain the closure mapping into rank i. (PDF p. 1).
- Divide the intermediate set B into blocks with at most ln q elements. Enumerate each block's subsets and their images under β. (PDF p. 1).

## Applications

- The acyclic algorithm provides the central closure step in the cited reduction for general directed graphs. (PDF p. 1).

## Optimization

- Use binary subset indices to construct each nonsingleton image from two previously constructed images. All block tables cost O(q²r/ln q) operations. (PDF p. 1).
- After table construction, select and combine the required block images in O(pqr/ln q) operations. (PDF p. 2).
