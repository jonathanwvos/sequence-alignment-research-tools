## Executive summary

- POASTA computes optimal sequence-to-graph alignments with gap-affine penalties. It uses A* search, greedy exact matching, and topology-based pruning. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors report an average 4.1-fold speed gain over SPOA on bacterial gene datasets. They also construct alignments of 342 sequences with lengths near one megabase. (PDF p. 7).

## Objectives

- The authors aim to reduce runtime and memory use while preserving optimal sequence-to-graph alignment. (PDF p. 2).
- They aim to make longer partial order alignments practical for applications such as pangenome construction. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- Conventional gap-affine POA needs time and memory proportional to graph nodes multiplied by query length. The authors estimate about 3 TB for a 500 kbp query and 500,000 graph nodes. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors argue that SIMD accelerates full dynamic programming without removing its memory demand. Adaptive banding reduces computation but loses the optimality guarantee. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- Alignment is a minimum-cost path problem in the product of the query and POA graph. Match, mismatch, insertion, and deletion edges encode alignment operations. (PDF p. 3).
- The admissible A* heuristic bounds the remaining gap cost from below. It compares unaligned query length with minimum and maximum path lengths to the graph end. (PDF p. 3).
- The heuristic requires O(V + E) preprocessing and O(V) extra memory. The authors emphasize this low cost because each added sequence changes the graph. (PDF p. 7).
- Graph branches prevent direct use of the pairwise wavefront diagonal formulation. POASTA instead uses superbubble topology to remove unnecessary states. (PDF p. 7).

## Methods

- POASTA builds an MSA progressively by aligning each query to a character-labeled directed acyclic graph representing the current alignment. (PDF p. 2).
- A reverse topological scan computes shortest and longest paths to the end node. These lengths support the minimum remaining gap cost heuristic. (PDF p. 3).
- A depth-first component follows exact matches greedily. Adjacent insertion and deletion states are queued when a mismatch is encountered. (PDF p. 3).
- Superbubble pruning compares best-case paths from a candidate state with costs available from previously reached bubble exits. States that cannot improve the solution are removed. (PDF p. 5).

## Applications

- The authors constructed long M. tuberculosis alignments and examined the rpoB drug-resistance locus. (PDF p. 6).
- POASTA is provided as a standalone utility and a Rust crate for use in other software. (PDF p. 8).

## Optimization

- The combined heuristic, exact-match traversal, and superbubble pruning reduce the number of computed alignment states without sacrificing sequence-to-graph optimality. (PDF p. 2).
- The authors propose dual gap-affine penalties, bidirectional A*, stronger heuristics, and GPU execution as future improvements. These are not reported as implemented features. (PDF p. 8).

## Evaluation and testing

- The gene benchmark used 13 datasets drawn from five housekeeping genes in 40,188 complete bacterial genomes. Sets contained 140–2,385 sequences, with mean lengths of 1–4 kbp and pairwise ANI of 82%–97%. (PDF p. 5).
- The authors measured complete MSA runtime and memory for POASTA and SPOA. They also compared abPOA, which uses heuristic banding. (PDF p. 5).
- Long-sequence tests used 342 M. tuberculosis genomes at approximately 250, 500, and 1,000 kbp. The authors excluded 28 genomes with large inversions to preserve colinearity. (PDF p. 6).
- The benchmark suite is written in Rust and Python. Its repository includes the data underlying the article. (PDF p. 8).

## Results

- POASTA was faster than SPOA on 12 of 13 gene datasets. The average speed gain was 4.1-fold, and the maximum was 9.8-fold. SPOA used 2.6 times more memory on average. (PDF p. 5).
- abPOA was 3.5 times faster than POASTA but used more memory on nearly all gene datasets. (PDF p. 5).
- The 250, 500, and 1,000 kbp tests took 5.3, 24, and 69 hours. Peak memory was 63.8, 120, and 231 GB. SPOA and abPOA exceeded the available 240 GB. (PDF p. 6).
- At rpoB codon 450, graph paths represented all observed codons. Edge counts matched counts derived independently from gene annotations. (PDF p. 7).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Optimal sequence-to-graph alignment does not guarantee a globally optimal MSA under the sum-of-pairs objective. (PDF p. 1).
- POASTA requires zero match cost and nonnegative other costs. Its gap-affine model also requires the gap-open cost to exceed the gap-extension cost. (PDF p. 3).
- High sequence diversity reduces exact-match runs and can make POASTA slower than SPOA. A* also has less predictable memory access than contiguous dynamic programming. (PDF p. 7).
- The long-sequence tests used highly similar, largely colinear genomes. POA cannot accurately model large inversions or translocations, and the gene tests contained few large indels. (PDF p. 6).
