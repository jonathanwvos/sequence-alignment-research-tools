## Executive summary

- The author reviews developments in multiple sequence alignment algorithms, focusing on consistency and template methods. (PDF p. 1).
- Template-based alignments incorporate structural data or homology predictions to guide progressive computation. (PDF p. 2).

## Objectives

- Review modern multiple sequence alignment strategies that move beyond pure sequence-based progressive alignment. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- Pure sequence information is insufficient to resolve difficult alignment regions below the twilight zone. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- The author analyzes consistency evaluation metrics to filter out unreliable alignment regions. (PDF p. 2).

## Methods

- Consistency-based methods combine multiple pairwise local and global alignments into a primary library. (PDF p. 3).
- Template-based approaches use predicted RNA secondary structures or tertiary protein models as alignment constraints. (PDF p. 3).

## Applications

- Multiple alignment of divergent protein families and non-coding RNA molecules. (PDF p. 3).

## Optimization

Not stated in the extractable source pages.

## Evaluation and testing

- Methods are evaluated on standard benchmark databases comparing predicted alignments to structural superpositions. (PDF p. 2).

## Results

- Template and meta-method approaches significantly increase alignment accuracy in distantly related sequences. (PDF p. 2).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Incorporating external templates increases computational complexity and depends on database annotation quality. (PDF p. 2).
