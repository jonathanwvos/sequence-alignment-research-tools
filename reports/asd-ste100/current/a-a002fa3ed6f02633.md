## Executive summary

- The authors calibrate protein retrieval thresholds to control a selected loss at a user-defined risk level. (PDF p. 2).
- The framework supports functional annotation, enzyme classification, and database filtering before structural alignment. (PDF p. 8).
- The statistical guarantees depend on exchangeability and are marginal rather than conditional. (PDF p. 8).

## Objectives

- The objective is to select proteins for further characterization with interpretable scores and controlled retrieval risk. (PDF p. 2).
- For structural search, the objective is to reduce the lookup database while retaining most high-scoring DALI hits. (PDF p. 7).

## Arguments

- The authors argue that sequence and structural similarity alone do not establish shared function. Raw similarity scores can also be difficult to interpret. (PDF p. 2).
- They propose calibration as a way to use existing search models without assumptions about model structure. (PDF p. 2).

## Analysis

- Retrieval includes lookup proteins whose similarity scores exceed a calibrated threshold. The selected loss can measure false negatives or false discoveries. (PDF p. 2).
- The CLEAN analysis uses hierarchical EC loss from 0 for an exact match to 4 for a different enzyme class. The authors state that these penalties can be changed. (PDF p. 11).
- Interpretation: calibration at 1% FNR on SCOPe families does not establish 1% FNR for DALI hits. The authors apply the threshold to a separate task. (PDF p. 7).

## Methods

- The experiments use a Learn Then Test variant that selects a threshold through a risk bound and a specified risk limit. (PDF p. 10).
- Isotonic regression maps similarity scores to nondecreasing match probabilities. Venn-Abers prediction fits two regressions with hypothetical test labels 0 and 1. (PDF p. 10).
- CLEAN calibration uses Euclidean distances to 5242 EC cluster embeddings and per-query hierarchical losses. (PDF p. 11).
- The DALI prefilter uses Protein-Vec scores calibrated on SCOPe families. The Kneedle algorithm defines a query-specific elbow in sorted DALI Z-scores. (PDF p. 7).

## Applications

- The authors apply calibrated Protein-Vec retrieval to genes of unknown function in the minimal genome JCVI Syn3.0. (PDF p. 4).
- Conformal selection permits an empty EC annotation set. In an antibody example, it returned no enzyme annotation while both CLEAN selection methods returned annotations. (PDF p. 5).
- The structural search application filters the 2.3 million protein clustered AlphaFold Database before DALI alignment. (PDF p. 7).

## Optimization

- The prefilter reduces the number of candidate structures passed to the more expensive DALI search. (PDF p. 7).
- DALI searches use parallel batches of 1000 structures. Protein embeddings use one A6000 GPU, and FAISS indexes the embeddings and generates similarity scores. (PDF p. 11).
- Isotonic regression is a convex optimization problem with linear-time solution algorithms for the ordered data. (PDF p. 10).

## Evaluation and testing

- Pfam experiments use exact annotation matches and 100 shuffled calibration trials with risk level 0.1 for FNR and FDR. (PDF p. 3).
- The New-392 benchmark uses 300 calibration proteins and 92 test proteins across 10 shuffled trials. The transfer test uses 380 New proteins for calibration and all 149 Price proteins for testing. (PDF p. 6).
- The UniProt split separates 2350 calibration or validation proteins from 540560 lookup proteins by creation date. A temporal check compares FNR and FDR loss distributions across two later periods. (PDF p. 10).
- The structural benchmark compares DALI and Protein-Vec searches for 73 SCOPe domains against the clustered AlphaFold Database. (PDF p. 7).

## Results

- At FDR risk level 0.1, 39.6% of previously unannotated JCVI Syn3.0 coding genes met the exact functional match criterion. The Pfam test retrieved about 25% of true positives. (PDF p. 4).
- On New-392, conformal selection achieved mean F1 57.65, compared with 50.96 for max-separation and 52.04 for p-value selection. (PDF p. 6).
- On Price-149, conformal selection achieved F1 49.62 and AUC 74.59. Both exceeded the two CLEAN selection results, but precision was lower. (PDF p. 6).
- The DALI prefilter retained a mean database fraction of 0.685. Table 4 reports mean TPR 0.818 and FNR 0.182 for hits above the Z-score elbow. (PDF p. 8).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Unequal organism sampling, missing protein families, and variable sequence quality can violate exchangeability. Calibration also depends on the amount and quality of labeled data. (PDF p. 8).
- The guarantees are marginal. Thresholds may perform differently across protein classes, and the method does not bound the underlying pairwise similarity score. (PDF p. 8).
- The New and Price datasets are not exchangeable. Their observed transfer performance therefore does not establish that the theoretical guarantee holds under this shift. (PDF p. 5).
- The DALI tables report mean TPR 81.8%, while the preceding results text reports 82.8% retention. The excerpts contain this numerical discrepancy. (PDF p. 8).
