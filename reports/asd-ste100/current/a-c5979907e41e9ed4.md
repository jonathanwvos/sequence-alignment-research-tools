## Executive summary

- Clustal Omega combines a scalable guide tree method with profile HMM alignment to construct protein MSAs. (PDF p. 2).
- It also uses external profiles to transfer information from existing alignments into new alignment tasks. (PDF p. 4).

## Objectives

- Align large protein sequence sets quickly while retaining accuracy comparable to slower methods on small sets. (PDF p. 1).
- Reuse existing alignments when new sequences become available. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- The authors identify all-pairs guide tree construction as a major scaling barrier because its time and memory requirements grow quadratically. (PDF p. 1).
- The authors argue that external profiles can supply useful position-specific information without forcing new sequences to follow an old alignment exactly. (PDF p. 5).

## Analysis

- The modified mBed method has O(N log N) complexity. Each sequence is represented by distances to a reference set whose size is proportional to log N. (PDF p. 2).
- Runtime comparisons across families vary with sequence length and identity. The authors also sample increasing subsets from one family to reduce this variation. (PDF p. 3).
- Iteration can reduce accuracy on small sets. Its benefit increases for sets with about 1000 or more sequences. (PDF p. 4).

## Methods

- mBed converts sequences to distance vectors for rapid clustering. HHalign then aligns profile hidden Markov models during progressive alignment. (PDF p. 2).
- External profile alignment first aligns each input sequence to a supplied HMM. It then transfers pseudocount information to each sequence position. (PDF p. 4).
- Iteration converts the current MSA to an HMM and uses it to realign the input sequences. The guide tree can also be recalculated. (PDF p. 4).

## Applications

- Users can add individual sequences or an aligned sequence set to an existing alignment. (PDF p. 4).
- Curated protein or domain alignments can supply external profiles for repeated alignment tasks, including metagenomics work. (PDF p. 4).

## Optimization

- The implementation uses k-means++ for sequence-vector clustering and adapted MUSCLE routines for fast UPGMA and guide tree handling. (PDF p. 6).
- OpenMP supports parallel computation of pairwise distances and alignment match states. (PDF p. 6).
- One or two combined HMM and guide tree iterations improve large HomFam alignments. (PDF p. 4).

## Evaluation and testing

- The benchmarks are BAliBASE 3, PREFAB 4.0, and HomFam. HomFam combines Pfam version 25 with Homstrad data from June 2011. (PDF p. 6).
- BAliBASE evaluation uses bali_score on core columns across 218 families. TC scores measure recovered reference columns. (PDF p. 2).
- HomFam inserts at least five sequences with known structures into larger homologous Pfam sets. Accuracy is scored only on the sequences with known structures. (PDF p. 3).
- Large-set comparisons include Clustal Omega, MAFFT, MUSCLE, and Kalign. MUSCLE is limited to two iterations above 3000 sequences, and MAFFT PartTree is used for the largest sets. (PDF p. 3).

## Results

- On BAliBASE, Clustal Omega obtains a mean TC score of 0.554 in 539.91 seconds. Probcons scores 0.558 and T-Coffee scores 0.551, with longer runtimes. (PDF p. 2).
- For the largest HomFam group, Clustal Omega scores 0.464 TC in 27,328.9 seconds. MAFFT PartTree scores 0.253 in 6119.4 seconds. (PDF p. 4).
- Pfam external profiles increase mean HomFam accuracy from 0.627 to 0.653 across 94 cases. (PDF p. 4).
- The authors report aligning more than 190,000 sequences on one processor within a few hours. (PDF p. 1).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The authors state that available benchmarks are limited and biased toward single-domain globular proteins. (PDF p. 5).
- Using BAliBASE reference alignments as external profiles raises TC from 0.554 to 0.857. The authors explicitly reject this as a valid accuracy comparison with other programs. (PDF p. 4).
- The tests omit gap positions that lack structural conservation. This limits assessment of methods designed to handle gaps for phylogenetic analysis. (PDF p. 3).
- Clustal Omega is not the fastest method in every comparison. MAFFT is faster across the HomFam test sizes described. (PDF p. 3).
