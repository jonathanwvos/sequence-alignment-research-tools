## Executive summary

- The authors investigate the computational complexity of recognizing point visibility graphs in the Euclidean plane. (PDF p. 1).
- They prove that point visibility graph recognition is complete for the existential theory of the reals. (PDF p. 1).

## Objectives

- Determine whether an arbitrary graph can be realized as the visibility graph of a point set in the plane. (PDF p. 1).

## Arguments

- Point visibility graphs share geometric constraint properties with pseudoline arrangements and order types. (PDF p. 3).

## Analysis

- The authors construct polynomial-time reductions from the stretchability of pseudolines to PVG recognition. (PDF p. 6).

## Methods

- Geometric gadgets force collinearity of point subsets along rays and sightlines. (PDF p. 6).
- Fan constructions translate projective incidence theorems into graph visibility constraints. (PDF p. 11).

## Applications

- Computational geometry, visibility graph characterization, and geometric graph drawing algorithms. (PDF p. 2).

## Results

- Recognizing point visibility graphs is ER-complete, establishing that the problem is NP-hard. (PDF p. 12).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The ER-completeness result rules out efficient polynomial-time recognition algorithms unless complexity classes collapse. (PDF p. 12).
