## Executive summary

- HHsearch aligns pairs of profile HMMs to detect remote protein homologs. It generalizes sequence–HMM log-odds scoring to a log-sum-of-odds score. (PDF p. 3).
- The benchmark compares 3691 SCOP domains with sequence identities below 20%. HHsearch improves both homology detection and alignment quality over the tested methods. (PDF p. 1).
- HHsearch can add confidence-dependent secondary structure scores for predicted versus predicted or predicted versus known structure. (PDF p. 5).

## Objectives

- The author aims to improve remote homology detection while retaining reliability, speed, and ease of use. (PDF p. 2).
- The study tests whether profile HMMs improve sensitivity and alignment quality compared with simple sequence profiles. (PDF p. 2).

## Arguments

- Profile HMMs represent position-specific insertion and deletion probabilities. The author argues that these probabilities penalize chance matches more than homologous matches. (PDF p. 2).
- The author argues that secondary structure remains informative for distant homologs and can help distinguish them from chance matches. (PDF p. 2).
- The author concludes that HMM transition information and secondary structure scoring contribute more than the choice of profile column score in this benchmark. (PDF p. 9).

## Analysis

- The log-sum-of-odds score sums co-emission odds over sequences that both HMMs can emit along an alignment path. It reduces to sequence–HMM log-odds when one HMM represents one sequence. (PDF p. 3).
- The score separates into match-column scores and the logarithm of the path transition probability. The column score is log(sum_a q_i(a)p_j(a)/f(a)). (PDF p. 3).
- Inverse background-frequency weights increase the contribution of rare amino acids. A column with background frequencies contributes zero, as do insert states. (PDF p. 3).

## Methods

- HHsearch uses Viterbi dynamic programming to find the HMM alignment path with the maximum log-sum-of-odds score. (PDF p. 3).
- Profile HMMs contain match, insert, and delete states. Their emission probabilities describe residue distributions, and transition probabilities describe position-specific gap behavior. (PDF p. 3).
- The secondary structure model uses DSSP assignments and PSIPRED predictions. Ten 3-by-7 substitution matrices represent the ten prediction confidence levels. (PDF p. 5).
- Secondary structure scores are added to amino acid column scores with weight 0.15. Predicted-versus-predicted scoring sums over the seven underlying DSSP states. (PDF p. 5).

## Applications

- HHsearch supports remote homology detection for functional annotation, structure prediction, and protein evolution studies. (PDF p. 9).
- For structure prediction, HHsearch can use known template secondary structure and rank database matches as candidate structural templates. (PDF p. 9).

## Optimization

- The column score requires only one logarithm. The author identifies this property as a computational advantage. (PDF p. 3).
- The correlation correction is added after alignment. Directly optimizing the score with this correction gave only a marginal improvement at about twice the computation time. (PDF p. 5).
- The correlation weight of 0.1 was selected on 317-by-317 pairwise alignments. The secondary structure weight had a broad empirical optimum near 0.15. (PDF p. 5).
- Semi-global realignment of HHsearch 4 matches produced slightly better structural-template alignment results than HHsearch 4 alone. (PDF p. 9).

## Evaluation and testing

- The study uses an all-against-all comparison of SCOP 1.63 domains filtered to a maximum sequence identity of 20%. Comparators are BLAST, PSI-BLAST, HMMER, PROF_SIM, and COMPASS. (PDF p. 5).
- Seed alignments were built with up to eight PSI-BLAST iterations. Inclusion thresholds were 10^-5 in earlier iterations and 10^-4 in the final iteration. (PDF p. 5).
- HHsearch variants isolate fixed-gap profile scoring, HMM transitions, correlation correction, and predicted or known secondary structure. COMPASS settings were adjusted to improve its benchmark performance. (PDF p. 5).
- Structural-template evaluation uses the best match from each database scan. It separates family, superfamily, and fold relationships and evaluates alignment quality with MaxSub. (PDF p. 9).

## Results

- At a 10% false-positive rate, HHsearch with secondary structure detected 2.7–4.2 times as many homologs as PSI-BLAST or HMMER. It detected 1.44–1.9 times as many as COMPASS or PROF_SIM. (PDF p. 1).
- HHsearch produced 1.2, 1.7, and 3.3 times as many good alignments as COMPASS at family, superfamily, and fold levels. Good alignments had a balanced score above 0.3. (PDF p. 1).
- A 200-residue query took 33 seconds to scan 3691 domains on a 2 GHz AMD64 PC. This was ten times faster than PROF_SIM and 17 times faster than COMPASS. (PDF p. 1).
- HHsearch 4 produced usable template alignments with MaxSub at least 0.1 in 66% of family cases, 44% of superfamily cases, and 19% of fold cases. (PDF p. 9).

## Limitations and uncertainty

- The author reports unreliable E-values for several tools, including HHsearch. HHsearch also reports true-positive probabilities estimated from the benchmark comparisons. (PDF p. 2).
- Preliminary blind structure-prediction results were below expectations. The author attributes this partly to overly selective multiple alignment construction that reduced sensitivity. (PDF p. 9).
